One act, three sizes. This is the only booking on the roster that changes shape during the night instead of being replaced.

The reason to care is the contract. Most events buy the front of the night and the back of the night from two different acts, which means two load-ins, two sound checks, two sets of names to keep straight and a seam in the middle that guests can hear. This booking ladders instead: a duo through arrival and dinner, then the rhythm section arrives and the same voice fronts the dance floor. The party sees one band getting bigger.

At quartet it stops being a cocktail act. The full rhythm section behind one voice, keys or a second guitar on top, playing a covers set built for a floor, and it is the size to book when filling the floor is the actual objective.

It is a premium cover band. It is not a show band. Nobody changes costume, there is no choreography and there is no host segment. The band plays, reads the floor, and plays the next thing.

This is a named act, published under the name of the person who fronts it, and any substitution in the lineup is named to you in advance.

Where it lands

Four nights, one voice, sized to each.

Covers are the working repertoire of a private event. The four nights below each ask for a different size of the same act, and each one carries a full page of its own.

Office holiday parties
The quartet is the dance floor booking of the season. It plays dinner at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, then opens the floor at 88 to 95 once the program ends. The November and December dates are published on the season page, and Denver carries the downtown calendar in detail.
Client evenings and corporate dinners
At duo size the same songs sit under conversation instead of over it, the register a client dinner or a member evening asks for. An hour on its own is priced on the rate card, and the corporate page covers the paperwork side, purchase orders and net-30 included.
Milestones and private parties
A milestone birthday or an anniversary usually wants both halves of the night: dinner guests can talk through, then a floor. One booking covers both without a second act, and the trio is the size for a party that wants a floor without a full band.
Wedding cocktail hours
The duo is the pick when the hour should have a voice in it rather than an instrumental set. It covers arrival and cocktails at conversation volume, and if the reception wants a floor later, the same act grows instead of handing off.

How the night is paced around speeches and a program is run of show work, written before the day. Where the ladder's figures sit in the wider Colorado market is set out in the cost guide.

Booking questions

The money detail is on the rate card, and the operational detail, footprints to power, on the technical page.

How does the duo to quartet ladder work on one contract?
One act books at every size the night needs: a duo through arrival, cocktails and dinner, then the rhythm section arrives and the same voice fronts the dance floor. One contract, one load-in, one sound check, and no seam in the middle of the night for guests to hear.
What does the quartet add?
The dance floor. At quartet the same voice fronts guitar, bass, drums and keys or a second guitar, playing a covers set built for a floor. Dinner holds at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, the floor runs 88 to 95, and the quartet moves between the two on cue.
What does Tejas Singh play?
Covers, fronted by one voice. The style is singer-songwriter covers, and the set pulls from the published song list, which runs to 240 titles. A set is built against your run of show and read song by song on the night, and the first learned song you choose is included in every booking.
Does the duo work for a wedding cocktail hour?
Yes. The duo covers arrival, cocktails and dinner at a volume guests talk across, and it is the pick when the hour wants a voice out front rather than an instrumental set. A single hour is priced on its own line of the rate card, and the same act can grow to the quartet later on the same contract.
