The set

Standards at conversation volume.

What should play is the easier half of the question: jazz standards and singer-songwriter covers, songs guests recognize without having to name. The harder half is volume, and volume is where a cocktail hour is won or lost.

The level is the decision. Cocktail and dinner sets run at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level two people who have just met can talk across without leaning in. That is the actual work of the hour, because half the guest list is meeting the other half. A band that plays this hour at dance floor level has guests shouting introductions over their first drink.

The repertoire stays familiar on purpose. Standards read as considered without asking to be watched, and a duo arrangement of upright bass and guitar leaves space in the middle of the sound where conversation sits. The instrumentation, the trio option and the longer sample set are on the jazz duo and trio page.

Timing

Sixty minutes, sometimes ninety.

A standard cocktail hour runs 60 minutes. Plan 90 when ceremony and reception share one space, because the guests wait out the venue flip with a drink in hand, and the duo is what they are doing while they wait.

Where the time goes. The couple is away for photographs, the venue is turning ceremony rows into dinner tables, and the guests are sorting themselves into the evening's conversations. Sixty minutes covers all of that when the reception has its own space. When one floor does both jobs, the flip sets the length, and 90 is the honest booking.

One booking through dinner. The duo that plays the hour can keep playing: cocktails through dinner is one line of the contract at $2,000–2,800, one load-in and one sound check. If the hour stretches, added time bills at the same hourly rate in 30-minute increments. There is no overtime rate.

Audio continues through any break, so the hour never goes silent. The same duo plays dinner and cocktail parties year-round and covers client dinners on the corporate side, priced off the same card.

Duo or DJ

Two players for the hour, a DJ for the late night.

Both answers are honest and both sit on the same rate card. A duo reads the crowd while the crowd is still forming, shades its volume live and takes a request. A DJ earns the booking after dinner, holding a floor for hours at a stretch.

Wedding dates book ten to twelve months out, and twelve to eighteen for a mountain venue or a fall Saturday, so the cocktail hour is decided when the band is.

The paperwork is the fast part. A certificate of insurance naming the venue, a W-9 and one itemised invoice come in the same email as the contract, and a named contact with a mobile number in writing is reachable from load-in to load-out.

Cocktail hour questions

The operational questions, from substitutions to decibel caps, are on the full question list.

Is cocktail hour music necessary at a wedding?
Yes, and it earns its line. The ceremony has ended, the couple is away for photographs, and the guests are deciding what kind of night this is. Live music carries that turn. A duo at conversation volume holds the hour without competing with the conversations it exists to start.
What music should play during a wedding cocktail hour?
Jazz standards and singer-songwriter covers, at conversation volume. The repertoire matters less than the level: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table lets two strangers introduce themselves without leaning in. Upright bass and guitar is the instrumentation built for exactly that.
How long is a wedding cocktail hour?
Sixty minutes is standard. It stretches to 90 when the venue turns one space from ceremony to reception, because guests cannot go back in until the flip is done. Added time bills at the same hourly rate in 30-minute increments; there is no overtime rate.
Should we book a band or a DJ for cocktail hour?
Two players, for this hour. A duo reads the crowd, takes a request, and lands at a volume people can talk across. A DJ earns its fee later, carrying the dance floor after dinner. Both sit on the same published rate card, so the comparison costs nothing to make.
Can one booking cover the cocktail hour and dinner?
Yes. The same two players carry cocktails and dinner on one line of the contract, with sound included and a published range covering the whole block. One load-in, one sound check, one invoice, and the hour never goes silent between sets.
