# Signet Artists — ICP

Who we are actually selling to. Derived from the site's own evidence:
`_src/data/site.json` (credits, planner FAQs, event types, corporate shapes),
`_src/data/reviews.json`, and the corporate page set. Internal only.

Nothing in this file is published. It exists so that every post, every DM and
every outbound note is written to a named person with a named fear, instead of
to "businesses in Denver."

---

## The one-line ICP

**A Denver-area professional who has been handed responsibility for an evening
that their colleagues will judge them on, and who has never booked live music
before.**

Everything else is segmentation.

---

## Primary buyer — "the one who gets the credit or the blame"

### A. The internal organiser (highest volume)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Titles** | Executive Assistant, Office Manager, People Ops / HR Manager, Culture or Employee Experience Lead, Marketing Manager, Chief of Staff |
| **Company** | 75 to 600 staff, Denver metro / DTC / Boulder / Colorado Springs. Tech, finance, healthcare, law, real estate, energy, professional services |
| **Budget authority** | Usually recommends, rarely signs. Reports to a VP or the CEO |
| **How they buy** | Google, then vendor sites, then two or three quotes, then a recommendation from the venue |
| **Frequency** | Once or twice a year. They are not experts and they know it |

**What they actually want:** to walk into Monday and have three people tell them
the party was great.

**What they are afraid of:** the dance floor stays empty. The band plays over the
CEO. Somebody asks for a certificate of insurance ten days out and the vendor
goes quiet. The evening ends early and everyone gets their coat at 8:40.

**What they say out loud:** "We want something more fun than last year." "We have
speeches at 7:30." "Our finance team needs a W-9 and a PO number."

**The sentence that converts them:** the music stops while the CEO is still three
steps from the microphone.

### B. The event professional (highest value, repeats)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Titles** | Corporate Event Planner, Event Producer, DMC account manager, Meeting Planner, Incentive Travel Planner |
| **Company** | Independent planners, agencies, DMCs working Denver and the mountain corridor |
| **Frequency** | Many events a year. One good relationship is worth a decade of bookings |

**What they want:** a vendor who makes them look good and never generates a
surprise. Advance sheets, a stage plot, a named contact, a load-in that runs
itself.

**What they are afraid of:** being the one who recommended the problem.

**They are vetting us differently.** They do not read the homepage. They look for
the technical page, the run of show page, and whether our Instagram shows real
work or three years of nothing.

### C. The venue and hotel referrer (best-quality referral)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Titles** | Catering Sales Manager, Director of Catering, Conference Services Manager, Banquet Manager |
| **Where** | Downtown Denver hotels, resorts at Vail / Beaver Creek / Breckenridge / Aspen, private clubs, museums, event venues |

**What they want:** a preferred-vendor name they can hand a client without
thinking about it. Someone who respects the decibel cap, honours the curfew,
coordinates with in-house AV instead of fighting it, and never leaves a mess.

**Why they matter more than they look:** they are asked "do you know a band?"
several times a week and the answer is whoever they thought of last.

### D. The nonprofit / association organiser

| | |
|---|---|
| **Titles** | Development Director, Events Manager, Membership Director, Executive Director |
| **Occasions** | Galas, award nights, auctions and paddle raises, member evenings, donor dinners |

**What they want:** music that disappears completely under a program and comes
back up for the floor at the end. A vendor who understands that the auction is
the event and the band is not.

### E. The private host (smaller, warmer, fast to close)

Milestone birthdays, anniversaries, holiday parties at home, dinner parties in
Cherry Creek, Boulder, Castle Pines, Evergreen. Fewer approvals, faster yes,
often the same person in a different role. Frequently the executive who attended
a corporate booking we played.

## Anti-ICP — who we are not writing for

- Couples price-shopping a wedding DJ on a marketplace
- Bars and restaurants booking a weekly happy-hour slot
- Anyone whose first and only question is what it costs
- Public festival and ticketed-show bookers
- Musicians looking for work. They will make up a large share of any social
  following we build, and they are not customers. Do not let their engagement
  steer the content

## The buying window (this is the whole calendar)

| Occasion | Decided | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Office holiday party | August to October | November, December |
| New Year's Eve | Earliest of all | December 31 |
| **January office party** | **October to December** | **Second and third weeks of January** |
| Corporate retreat | 4 to 8 months out | Year round, mountain-heavy in winter and summer |
| Gala and award night | 6 to 12 months out | Spring and fall |
| Client dinner | 3 to 10 weeks out | Year round, Q4 heavy |
| Wedding cocktail hour | 10 to 18 months out | May to October |

Two things follow from this table, and they set the whole content plan:

1. **We are mid-window for Nov/Dec 2026 right now.** Decisions are being made
   this month and next.
2. **The January window has not opened yet.** It is the only major Q4-adjacent
   occasion where we can still get in at the front of the decision rather than
   the back.

## What the buyer must believe before they enquire

In order. Social has to deliver these in this sequence, not all at once.

1. **These people are real and currently working.** The single lowest bar and
   the one we are failing today.
2. **They sound good.** Footage, not adjectives.
3. **They have done this at my size and in my kind of venue.**
4. **They will not embarrass me in front of my leadership.**
5. **My finance team can pay them without a fight.**
6. **Someone like me has already used them and was glad.**

---

## Language the buyer uses (use theirs, not ours)

They search and write: *company holiday party, office party, corporate event
entertainment, band for a corporate event, live music for a work party, holiday
party entertainment, January work party, employee appreciation, client
appreciation, awards night, team offsite.*

They do not say: *ensemble, configuration, roster, act, program, palos, set
piece.* Those are our internal words. They are correct on the site, where the
reader has already arrived with intent. They are wrong in a feed, where the
reader has none.
