The Music

Two bands, because a night has two halves.

The Standards play the half where people talk. The Headliners play the half where nobody sits down. A DJ bridges the two and carries the late end, through one contract and one sound system.

The players

Who is actually on the stage.

Signet opened this year. The people on its stages did not. Named here with the chair they hold and the record that backs it, because for a planner meeting us for the first time a name you can look up is worth more than an adjective. The first one is the number that arrives with your contract.

Your contact for the night. Bass, both books.

Daniel Fox

There are two kinds of lead on a stage and they are rarely the same person. One calls the show in front of the room. The other holds the booking. Daniel is the second one. The number that arrives with your contract is his, he answers this website himself, and he is on the stand all night, so there is nobody to hand you off to on the day.

He came to this from the other side of the table. He founded Unfold Fest in Costa Rica in 2021 and booked the whole thing without playing a note on it. He co-owns Dizzy Charlies Music and Events in Colorado Springs, where he has run a summer concert series end to end and booked every act on it, playing exactly one himself. He has held a run of show rather than played to one, and that is most of the reason this site publishes what it publishes.

He plays bass in both books, upright with The Standards and electric with The Headliners. That makes him the one person on stage at every size on the rate card, from the two-piece at six o'clock to the thirteen-piece at eleven.

Bass, drums, guitar and keys are all gig-ready, which matters most on the nights that go sideways. He has played a full show with a band he had never met and whose material he did not know, and covered bass for Booty and the Beat at an Aurora festival on three days' notice with no rehearsal. On a Signet date he does it before anyone notices: he covers a chair, reassigns a part, swaps a song, and rebuilds the set while it is still running.

Fronts The Headliners. Guitar and lead voice.

Tejas Singh

Tejas is the other kind of lead. On a Headliners date he calls the show from the stage: counts the tunes, reads the floor, decides what follows what. It is the leadership the room can see, and it is why he can rebuild a set three songs in without anyone noticing he did.

He came up as a songwriter rather than a covers player, through years in the Washington, D.C. and New York music scenes before Denver. He has released two records under his own name, Good Years and Closer/Warmer.

He writes across rock, Americana, soul, folk and pop. That range is why the band can play Motown and a song released this year inside one set without sounding like two different groups working in shifts.

On his own he performs with live looping, building an arrangement in real time instead of playing to a track. It is why one player can cover a cocktail hour and still fill the room.
