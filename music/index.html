The Roster

Eight acts, and what every one of them costs.

Signet books named acts. Not one house band with parts swapped in and out depending on the night, and not a pool of musicians assembled after you sign. You book the act, fronted by the person whose name is on it, at a price published before you write to us.

Signet opened this year. The acts on it did not. Each one below is fronted by someone with a book they already play, and the name is on the page so you can go and look them up rather than take an adjective for it.

Three of them have a full page: configurations, a sample of the set, and the footage slot. The rest carry the same published floor and the same contract. For the songs themselves, see the song book.

Every act, every size

What the price on the card already covers.

The number next to an act is not a musician fee with production quoted later. It is the booking. Two acts of the same size cost the same because the thing being delivered is the same.

  • A named contact for the night, given to you before the event
  • Sound and lighting sized to the room, with an engineer
  • Load-in, setup, strike and load-out
  • Certificate of insurance naming your venue
  • W-9 and one itemised invoice
  • Timeline coordination before the event
  • Continuous audio during breaks

Travel outside the metro, holiday weekends and New Year's Eve are quoted as their own lines on the contract, and all three are published on the pricing page. Nothing else appears on an invoice that was not on the quote.

Eight of a few hundred. The whole book, and the six sets a night gets built from, are on the songs page.
