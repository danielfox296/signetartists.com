Footage slot

This is the cocktail hour booking. Upright bass and guitar, playing the standards book, at a volume you can talk across.

It is sold as a format rather than under a name, and that is a deliberate promise rather than a hedge. What you are buying for this hour is an instrumentation, a repertoire and a volume. Which two players deliver it does not change any of the three, and pretending otherwise would mean naming a pair we cannot guarantee for your date. You get the spec, on every date it is available.

Most of what goes wrong in the first two hours of an event is a volume decision, not a repertoire decision. A band that plays this hour at the level it plays the dance floor makes guests shout at each other while they are still deciding what kind of night it is. A duo is the honest instrument for it: two players cannot get loud by accident, and the arrangement stays open enough that conversation sits on top of it rather than under it.

Standards are the book because they are the one repertoire nobody has to be told about. Guests do not identify the tunes and do not need to. The room reads as expensive and unhurried, which is the whole job for that hour.

The duo covers arrival, cocktails and dinner on one line of the contract. If the night also needs a dance floor, that is a second booking rather than this one turning up.
