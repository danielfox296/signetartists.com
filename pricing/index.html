Pricing

Every rate, on this page.

A solo act starts at $900. A quartet through a Denver evening is $4,000 to $5,500, and the same quartet in a resort town is $5,500 to $7,500. Every figure in between is published below, so you can put a real number in a budget before you write to us.

Almost nobody in this market publishes a rate card. The usual answer is a form, a discovery call, and a quote three days later that you cannot compare to anything. We would rather lose the enquiries that were never going to close and keep the ones that can.

What moves a price inside the range

Up: a wedding rather than a private party. A Saturday in June, September or October. A holiday weekend. A venue with a hard load-in, a long carry or a late curfew. A run of show with ceremony, cocktails, dinner and dancing all needing cover.

Down: a weeknight. A short call. A single block rather than the whole evening. A date inside a month we have not filled. Booking three or more parts of the night together, which takes 10% off performance fees.

Ranges are honest rather than decorative. If you tell us the date, the venue and the guest count, the number we send back is inside the range on this page and it does not move afterwards.

How the number is built

Two parts. A call-out covers the evening we commit regardless of length: travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike, load-out and production. Then an hourly rate for performance time.

A duo playing one hour still uses a whole evening, so the call-out prices the evening and the hourly rate prices the music. A one-hour cocktail set and a four-hour reception both come out honest.

There is no overtime rate. Additional time bills at the same hourly figure in 30-minute increments. Hour five costs what hour four cost, so the only question at 11pm is whether the crowd wants one more.

Booking more than one part of the night

Once we are in the building the call-out is already paid. So adding a smaller group for the ceremony, cocktails or dinner bills at the hourly rate only, with no second call-out.

An hour of cocktail duo added to a full band booking is $450. An hour of DJ after the band is $350. Booking three or more parts of the night together takes 10% off performance fees. How much of the night to book is your call.

If you would rather bring your own ceremony musician and use us for the dance floor, that is a normal booking.

Common questions

The money ones. Everything else, from substitutions to decibel caps, is on the full question list, and the market around these figures is set out in the cost guide.

What does a band cost for a corporate event in Denver?
Our published rates run from $900 for a solo player at cocktail hour to $5,500 for a quartet through a Denver evening. An hour of jazz duo on its own is $1,050. Sound is included at every size. Travel outside the metro and holiday weekends are quoted as their own lines on the contract.
How is the price calculated?
Two parts. A call-out covers the evening we commit regardless of length: travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. Then an hourly rate for performance time. A one-hour booking is priced as one hour, and there is no overtime rate: added time bills at the same hourly figure in 30-minute increments, so hour five costs what hour four cost.
Do you provide a certificate of insurance?
Yes, naming your venue as additional insured, sent in the same email as the contract. A W-9 and an itemised invoice come with it.
Can one booking cover cocktails and dancing?
Yes. Once we are in the building the call-out is already paid, so a smaller group for ceremony, cocktails or dinner bills at the hourly rate only. An hour of cocktail duo added to a full band booking is $450, an hour of DJ after the band is $350, and booking three or more parts of the night together takes 10% off performance fees.
How far in advance should we book?
Corporate holiday parties are typically decided August through October for November and December dates, and New Year's Eve books earliest of all. Weddings and galas run longer: ten to twelve months, and twelve to eighteen for a mountain venue or a fall Saturday.
