Factors that affect price

Price Increase: a wedding rather than a private party. A Saturday in June, September or October. A holiday weekend, which this market prices 20 to 30 percent above a standard date. A venue with a hard load-in, a long carry or a late curfew. A run of show with ceremony, cocktails, dinner and dancing all needing cover. A drive over the passes, and past them a night's lodging.

Price Decrease: Booked on an early weeknight. Fewer hours of performance time. A single set rather than the whole evening. A smaller build for the same music. A venue where the load-in is easy.

Send us the date, the venue and the guest count, and the price that comes back accounts for all of it.

How signet puts together a live entertainment quote

Most acts in this market bill two things. A call-out covers the evening committed regardless of length: the drive, load-in, setup, sound check, strike, load-out and the production that rides along with all of it (in business parlance, they're the fixed costs). Then a rate for performance time (these map to variable cost). That is why the price for one hour of cocktail entertainment is not strictly a quarter of the price of a four-hour reception.

Sound is the line on a quote worth checking hardest. With some acts it's part of the figure. With others it shows up later as its own charge, or the venue is expected to have a system that was specified for speeches. Ours is included at every size, so the figure we send already covers it.

Travel and lodging are separate line items for us. Similarly, high-demand times like holiday weekends add a little extra to the bill.

Included for every act

  • A personal contact for the night.
  • Sound sized for a small-to-medium sized event venue
  • Load-in, setup, strike and load-out
  • Certificate of insurance
  • W-9 and one itemised invoice
  • Timeline coordination before the event
  • Continuous audio during set breaks

Stage lighting and a dedicated engineer are optional, and quoted as their own line when you want them.

Quoted separately

Each of these arrives as its own line on the quote and on the contract, never folded into a total.

  • Front Range travel: Boulder, Colorado Springs, Fort Collins
  • I-70 corridor travel: Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge, Winter Park
  • Far resort travel and a night's lodging: Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat, Crested Butte
  • Holiday and peak weekends, and New Year's Eve
  • Live-band karaoke
  • Learned songs, with four weeks' notice

Booking more than one part of the night

Once an act is in the building, the evening is already committed: the drive is made, the system is up, the sound check is done. So covering the ceremony or the cocktail hour alongside the main booking is a cheaper way to fill the night than hiring twice, and it's one contract, one load-in and one invoice.

How much of the night to cover is your call, and a smaller group early is a real answer as often as a bigger one later. If you would rather bring your own ceremony musician and use us for the dance floor, that's a normal booking and it should feel like one.

Tell us the flow of the evening when you send the date. The number that comes back is for that evening as you've described it.

Common questions

These are the ones about money. Everything else, from substitutions to decibel caps, is on the full question list, and the Colorado market around these questions is set out in the cost guide.

What does a band cost for a corporate event in Denver?
In this market a cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000, and a four to six piece band, the size the Denver market treats as its middle, runs $4,000 to $6,000. Those are the market's figures, not ours. Signet prices the date you're asking about, because a Tuesday in September and a Saturday in December aren't the same job.
Do you publish your prices?
We quote each date rather than publish a list, because a Saturday in December and a Tuesday in September aren't the same job. What's published here is what live music costs in this market, with the source for each figure, so you can set a budget before you write to us. Send the date and we'll come back with a recommendation and a price for your night.
Do you provide a certificate of insurance?
Yes, naming your venue as additional insured, sent in the same email as the contract. The coverage limits match what Colorado venues write into their vendor requirements. A W-9 and one itemized invoice come with it.
How far in advance should we book?
Corporate holiday parties are typically decided August through October for November and December dates, and New Year's Eve books earliest of all. Weddings and galas run longer: ten to twelve months, and twelve to eighteen for a mountain venue or a fall Saturday.
