Flamenco

Dirty Flamenco, the set piece of the night.

Flamenco is the one booking on this roster that almost nobody else in Colorado sells as a private event product. It exists here as a concert act, or as a guitarist playing Spanish-flavored background. Dirty Flamenco is neither: a working group with a standing repertoire, fronted by Gary Meyers, sold at private event scale from $2,800, with every configuration priced on the rate card.

It is the act you hire when the program has a moment in it that needs to be the moment: a cultural centerpiece at a member event, a corporate dinner that has to be memorable rather than pleasant, a milestone party that wants one thing guests will describe to people who were not there.

This is a named act rather than a format, an existing group booked under its own name, and any change to the lineup is named to you in advance.

The set piece

Built to be watched, placed where the program leaves space.

Most live music at a private event is designed to be felt and not watched. This is the opposite booking, and that changes how it sits in an evening.

The moment is scheduled rather than hoped for. A set piece lands where the program leaves space for it: after remarks, between dinner and the late end, at the turn of a milestone evening. We place it against your timeline before the night, the same way a toast or an award is placed, and the run of show page shows how that document works.

The repertoire is palos, the traditional forms. Bulerias, solea, alegrias, tangos: the forms themselves, not a set of pop songs with nylon strings on them. That is the difference between a flamenco booking and a Spanish guitar booking, and it is audible inside eight bars.

The trio still does the whole evening's work around its moment. Flamenco guitar, bass and cajon play cocktails and dinner with a full musical range that does not depend on a dance floor, so guests hear one act all night and the set piece reads as its peak rather than an interruption.

Flamenco booking questions

The operational questions, from substitutions to decibel caps, are on the full question list.

Is Dirty Flamenco traditional flamenco or Spanish guitar?
Traditional flamenco. The repertoire is palos, the traditional forms: bulerias, solea, alegrias, tangos. A Spanish guitar booking plays familiar songs with a Spanish flavor; a flamenco booking plays the forms themselves, and the difference is audible inside eight bars.
Can we add a dancer?
Yes. The quartet configuration is the trio plus a dancer, and the dancer is the set piece. Both configurations sit on the published rate card.
Does flamenco work as dinner music, or only as a show?
Both, in one booking. The trio plays cocktails and dinner with a full musical range that does not depend on a dance floor, and the set piece lands where your program leaves space for it, placed against the run of show the same way a toast is placed.
Who fronts Dirty Flamenco?
Gary Meyers. This is a named act rather than a format: an existing group with a standing repertoire, booked under its own name. Any change to the lineup, the front included, is named to you in advance along with the reason.
What kind of events book flamenco?
Member and association events, corporate dinners that want one photographed moment, milestone parties, and wedding cocktail hours that want a flavor.
