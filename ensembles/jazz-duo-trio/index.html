Jazz duo and trio

The jazz duo and trio, standards at conversation volume.

The duo is Denver's standard cocktail-and-dinner booking: upright bass and guitar playing standards at a volume you can talk across, $1,050 for a single hour and $2,000–2,800 for cocktails and dinner together. The trio adds drums, played with brushes, for larger floors. Both sit on the published rate card, with resort columns for the mountain towns.

Arrival, cocktails and dinner are this format's regular work: the hours when the music has to hold the event without anybody raising their voice over it. Standards are the repertoire because they are the one set of songs nobody has to be told about. Guests do not identify the tunes and do not need to. The night reads as expensive and unhurried.

Two sizes

The duo carries the night. The trio carries a bigger one.

Most of what goes wrong in the first two hours of an event is a volume decision, not a repertoire decision. A band that plays this stretch at dance floor level has guests shouting introductions over their first drink. Dinner and cocktail sets here run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level two people who just met can talk across, and two players cannot get loud by accident.

The duo
Upright bass and guitar, for cocktails and dinner together on one line of the contract, or a single hour on its own. It sets up in an 8 by 6 ft corner on one dedicated circuit, which is why it fits venues a band cannot.
The trio
The duo plus drums, played with brushes. Brushes keep the kit at dinner level while giving a larger floor and a longer guest list the pulse a two-piece cannot. The footprint grows to 12 by 8 ft, still on a single circuit.

Both sizes are sold as a format rather than under a name, and that is a promise rather than a hedge. What you are buying for these hours is an instrumentation, a repertoire and a volume. Which players deliver it does not change any of the three, so you get the spec on every date it is available.

Footprints, power and load-in by size are on the technical page. If the night ends on a dance floor, that is a second booking rather than this one turning up, and Tejas Singh covers it from duo to quartet.

Jazz duo and trio questions

The operational questions, from substitutions to decibel caps, are on the full question list. The roster's wider covers song list is published and searchable on the repertoire page.

What is the difference between the jazz duo and the trio?
One player, and the size of floor each holds. The duo is upright bass and guitar, the standard booking for cocktails and dinner. The trio adds drums played with brushes, which carries a larger floor and a longer guest list at the same conversation volume. Both play the same standards set.
Is a jazz duo enough for a dinner of sixty guests?
Yes. Dinner and cocktail sets run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level guests can talk across, and the duo holds that from twenty guests at one table to eighty at ten. Past that point, or on a larger floor, the trio is the size to book.
Why is the duo sold as a format instead of under a name?
Because the booking is an instrumentation, a repertoire and a volume, and the contract names all three. Naming a specific pair would promise a personality we might not field for your date. The spec is the promise we can keep.
Can one booking cover cocktails and dinner?
Yes, and that is the standard shape. The duo covers arrival, cocktails and dinner on one line of the contract, with sound included and audio continuing through breaks. A single hour on its own is also a published rate, and added time bills in 30-minute increments with no overtime rate.
Does the trio need a stage?
No. The trio plays fine on the floor and takes a 12 by 8 ft footprint on one dedicated 20-amp circuit, with load-in running about an hour. If you want a riser, the venue or your production company provides it, and every footprint is published on the technical page.
