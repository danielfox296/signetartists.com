The set

One voice and a guitar, or the guitar alone.

Two ways to run it. Sung, it is one voice and a guitar working blues, roots and the singer-songwriter catalog. Instrumental, it is guitar on its own: jazz standards and Spanish-tinged sets that hold a background without asking for attention.

The choice usually follows the occasion. Dinner conversation sits easiest over instrumental guitar, and a cocktail hour or a patio takes a voice well. Either way the set runs at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, the same dinner level every size on this roster plays, and one player cannot drift above it by accident.

It is sold as a format: an instrumentation, a repertoire and a price, guaranteed on any date it is available. The covers list the roster works from is published and searchable on the repertoire page, and the first learned song you ask for is included in every booking.

The footprint

A footprint, a circuit and 45 minutes.

A solo setup takes 6 by 4 feet, one dedicated 20-amp circuit, a 45-minute load-in and a 30-minute strike. That is the whole ask, and it is why this booking works in venues that could not seat a trio.

The footprint matters more often than the price. A private dining space at a restaurant, a gallery evening set between the works, a house with furniture nobody wants moved, a client dinner on the top floor of an office: these are venues where the first question is what fits, and one player with a small PA fits. Power is one circuit, and the requirement that it be dedicated, shared with nothing from catering, is the single detail worth checking with the venue early.

Footprints, inputs and circuits for every size are published on the technical page, written so you can hand your venue the link.

Solo guitarist questions

What does the solo guitarist play?
Blues, roots, jazz standards and Spanish-tinged sets, sung or purely instrumental. The choice follows the occasion: instrumental guitar sits easiest under dinner conversation, and a voice carries a cocktail hour or a patio. Send a do-not-play list or build the set with us, and the first learned song is included in every booking.
How much space and power does a solo guitarist need?
A 6 by 4 ft corner, one dedicated 20-amp circuit, and 45 minutes for load-in, with strike in 30. The circuit needs to be dedicated rather than shared with catering equipment, which is the detail most often missed. Full footprints for every size are on the technical page.
Is one guitarist enough for a cocktail hour?
For a shorter guest list, yes. A solo holds a cocktail hour or a dinner at conversation volume, 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table. When the crowd is large enough that one instrument disappears into it, the duo is the honest answer, and both figures sit on the published rate card.
Who plays a solo booking?
A working guitarist from the roster. The booking is sold by its spec: the instrumentation, the repertoire and the volume above, at a published price. That is the promise we can keep on every date the format is available, and it is the same rule every spec'd format on the roster follows.
Can the solo guitarist play outdoors?
Yes, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it. Colorado evenings drop fast, and instruments and electronics both stop behaving in the cold and the wet, so cover is required and a move-indoors call time goes in the plan. Patios and courtyards are this format's regular work.
