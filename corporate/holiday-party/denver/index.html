Priced for the season

A duo through dinner, a DJ to close.

A Denver holiday party has two halves: two hours that must work at conversation volume, and a floor that has to turn. The pairing that covers both honestly is a duo through cocktails and dinner with a DJ carrying the back half, and it is the most common booking of the season.

When one act should carry the whole night instead, Tejas Singh ladders inside a single contract: a duo at six, the same voice fronting a quartet at ten. Either way the night is one invoice, with sound included at every size.

Every size and both columns are on the rate card. Holiday and peak weekends carry a published 25 percent line and New Year's Eve 50 percent, and the cost guide walks the arithmetic around every figure.

Downtown venues

The ballroom is booked through a dock.

Denver's December concentrates in the hotels off 17th Street, the Brown Palace and Oxford class of calendar, with the Four Seasons a few blocks south and the club floors at the University Club of Denver and the Denver Athletic Club holding the member events. One shape of night runs through all of them: a program with remarks in it, dinner at conversation volume, and a floor that has to fill by nine.

The dock is the first booking. A downtown hotel schedules its dock the way it schedules its ballroom. Load-in runs through a dock window and a service elevator with its own calendar, and the banquet team wants footprint, power draw and timing before either is confirmed. We settle all three with the venue before the day, so the first your guests know of it is a band already playing.

The commonest load-in problem is shared power. A quartet takes 14 by 10 feet, 90 minutes of load-in and two dedicated 20-amp circuits, and dedicated is the word that matters: sharing a circuit with catering warmers is the usual cause of trouble at load-in. A duo needs 8 by 6 feet, one circuit and an hour.

The full sheet by size, stage footprints to channel counts, is on the technical page. Hand the link to your venue contact and the back-and-forth stops.

Questions

Asked about Denver holiday parties.

How many holiday party dates does Denver actually have?
Twenty-one. Thursdays, Fridays and Saturdays across November and December 2026, minus Thanksgiving and Christmas, come to 21 published party dates, and 10 of them are in December. The board on this page shows the current status of every one, with the date it was last checked printed under it.
What does load-in look like at a downtown Denver hotel?
Through the dock and the service elevator, inside the windows the venue sets. We coordinate dock time, elevator window and power with the venue before the day. A band needs 45 to 90 minutes to load in depending on size, plus one or two dedicated 20-amp circuits; the full sheet by size is on our technical page.
Do December dates in Denver cost more?
The base rates hold; the premiums are published. Holiday and peak weekends carry a 25 percent line and New Year's Eve carries 50 percent, printed on the rate card rather than discovered at contract. Every configuration's range is public too, so the arithmetic can be run before you write to us.
When should a Denver company book its holiday party?
August through October. That is when the season is decided for November and December dates, and established acts are booked out by Thanksgiving. New Year's Eve books earliest of all. After October you are choosing from what is left rather than from the calendar.
