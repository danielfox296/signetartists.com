The volume is the product

Most of what goes wrong with live music at a dinner is a volume decision. A band playing at party level makes guests shout across a table they were invited to talk at, and the host spends the night apologising for it. So the dinner figure is the one we publish and hold to: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, measured where your guests actually sit.

A duo is the honest size for that number. Upright bass and guitar playing standards leave the middle of the sound open, so conversation sits on top of the music rather than under it, and two players hold a dinner for hours without drifting louder. The dinner reads as expensive and unhurried, which is the whole job. Sets run 45 to 60 minutes with 15 to 20 between them, and audio continues through every break, so the evening never goes silent.

Toasts and remarks are covered at every size: a dedicated announcement microphone is live on its own channel from the end of sound check, and the music stops while your speaker is still steps away from it. If the dinner wants a voice out front instead of standards underneath, Tejas Singh leads our roster and plays the same hours as a duo.

Built for a firm's paperwork

A client dinner is usually a firm's money, which means procurement, and procurement is where event bookings go slow. The path is written down here so your finance team can check it before anyone commits to anything.

Net-30 on approval
Approved corporate accounts hold the date on signature and are invoiced after the event, due 30 days later, with a purchase order number on the invoice if your finance team uses one.
One itemised invoice
Performance, sound and any travel on separate lines, in a form accounts payable accepts the first time. The W-9 comes with the contract rather than after a chase.
Certificate of insurance
$1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate, naming your venue as additional insured, in the same email as the contract. Clubs ask for this before they confirm a date, so it arrives before they ask.

Vendor onboarding is usually the slowest step in a corporate booking. Start it when you shortlist, and the music becomes the one vendor line that is settled early.

Where these dinners happen

Client dinner questions

The rest of the operational set is on the planners question list.

How loud is a jazz duo during dinner?
Dinner and cocktail sets run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level you can talk across without raising your voice. If your venue or club enforces a cap, give us the number at contract and the evening is built under it rather than argued about on the night.
What does music for a client dinner cost?
The jazz duo prices by the hour, and both the hourly figure and the cocktails-and-dinner range are published on this page and on the rate card. There is no overtime rate: added time bills at the same hourly figure in 30-minute increments, agreed on the night with whoever holds the budget.
Can you invoice against a purchase order?
Yes. We can be set up as a vendor, quote against a PO, and invoice net-30 on approval, with the purchase order number on one itemised invoice. The certificate of insurance naming your venue and the W-9 arrive in the same email as the contract, before anyone has to ask for them.
Does the music stop for toasts and remarks?
Yes, before the speaker reaches the microphone. A dedicated announcement microphone is live on its own channel from the end of sound check, so toasts, introductions and remarks get a working mic of their own rather than the singer's. Transitions are set against your run of show before the event.
How much space does the duo need?
An 8 by 6 foot corner, one dedicated 20-amp circuit, and a 60-minute load-in. No stage and no house sound system are required: sound sized to the venue is included in the rate, and the footprint for every size up to quartet is published on the technical page.
