Holiday season 2026

Denver corporate holiday parties, November and December.

The season is decided between August and October. By early November the good Fridays and the second and third Saturdays in December are gone, and what is left is whoever had a cancellation. The board below shows what is actually open, and every rate on this page is published.

One contract covers the music, the sound, the lighting and an engineer. A certificate of insurance naming your venue arrives with it, along with a W-9 and one itemised invoice, because the slowest part of a corporate booking is almost never the band. It is vendor onboarding, and starting it in October is the difference between a booked party and a scramble.

The season itself

Why December is not just a party with tinsel on it.

There are speeches, and they are the point. Somebody thanks the team, somebody announces the number, somebody hands out an award. A dedicated announcement microphone is live on its own channel from the end of sound check at every size we bring, so nobody borrows the singer's mic and nothing cuts out halfway through.

The room is a restaurant or a hotel function space, not a venue built for a band. That means a real load-in question, a real power question, and a ceiling on volume. All three get settled before the day rather than at 5pm on it.

Half the room does not want to dance and the other half is waiting. The night has to work at conversation volume for two hours and then turn. That is why the duo-plus-DJ pairing wins the season: it is the cheapest honest way to cover both halves without one of them being an afterthought.

Procurement is slower than you think. Vendor setup, a PO, net-30 on approval, a COI naming the venue. All of it is available and none of it is fast. Start in October and it is invisible. Start in December and it is the reason the booking falls over.
