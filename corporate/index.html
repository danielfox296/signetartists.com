Working around a program

A corporate evening runs on a program: remarks from leadership, an award, an auction, a video, a window where people need to hear each other talk. Dinner and networking sit at conversation volume, and the night gets loud later, when loud is the point.

The music stops while your CEO is still three steps from the microphone. Transitions are rehearsed against the run of show the week before, and whoever holds it has the number of our contact for the night.

We work awards dinners, auctions, remarks and a video the same way: music up to the moment, silence through it, music again when the applause starts. If the program wants a louder chapter, live-band karaoke adds $1,500. Your people sing, the band plays behind them.

Galas and fundraisers book this way too, at the same sizes. A reception set through arrival, dinner music around the program, and the auction, the paddle raise and the thank-yous treated as cues the band works around. What a gala needs from the music is precision against its program rather than a bigger production, and solo through quartet covers it.

Playing the mountain corridor

Mountain venues run on their own rules. Decibel limits and curfews are honoured as written, and where there is a cap we want the number at contract rather than at 10pm: a band that knows the ceiling in advance builds a different night under it, choosing arrangements and instrumentation to land inside the limit instead of just turning down.

Load-in is coordinated with the venue before the day. Dock times, elevator windows, dedicated circuits, where the cases sit during dinner. All of it is written down by band size, so you can hand the venue a link rather than relay questions between us.

Travel is its own line on the quote, at the rates on the pricing page. The caps, the curfews, the altitude and what Colorado’s statewide noise law does to a town sound permit are all set out on the mountain corridor page, cited and dated.

Corporate booking questions

The full set, from substitutions to decibel caps, is on the planners question list.

What does live music cost for a corporate event in Denver?
Every rate is published before you write to us: solo through quartet plus a DJ, each priced as a range on the rate card, with sound included at every size. Travel outside the metro is its own published line, holiday and peak weekends carry +25%, and New Year's Eve +50%.
How far in advance should we book?
Corporate holiday parties are typically decided August through October for November and December dates, and New Year's Eve books earliest of all. Established acts book out by Thanksgiving. Client dinners and retreats run on shorter windows, and any date gets a straight yes or no by email.
Can you work with our procurement process?
Yes. We can be set up as a vendor, quote against a purchase order, and invoice net-30 on approval. A certificate of insurance naming your venue, a W-9 and one itemised invoice arrive in the same email as the contract. Vendor onboarding is usually the slowest step, so start it early.
How do you handle speeches and a program?
The music stops while your speaker is still steps from the microphone, and transitions are rehearsed against the run of show the week before the event. A dedicated announcement microphone is live on its own channel from the end of sound check at every size, so remarks, awards and auctions never wait on a cable swap.
How loud is the band during dinner and networking?
Dinner and cocktails run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level you can talk across without raising your voice. Dancing runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor, later in the night, when loud is the point. If your venue enforces a cap, give us the number at contract and the night is built under it.
