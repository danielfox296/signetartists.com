Retreats and incentive events, priced for the mountain.

The three nights a retreat books

The welcome dinner
The group has been travelling all day and the first night's job is conversation. A duo plays it at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, the level people talk across, so colleagues who flew in from three offices can actually meet.
The program night
Awards, remarks, the number for the year. Music runs up to the moment and stops while the speaker is still steps from the microphone, with a dedicated announcement mic live on its own channel from sound check on.
The last night
The loud one. A quartet fills a floor at 88 to 95 dBA, inside whatever cap and curfew the resort sets, and where the retreat wants a named act in front, Tejas Singh leads the roster.

Each night is its own line on one contract. And once we are in the building for a date, the evening is already committed, so a second set that same evening is a cheaper way to cover the night than a second booking would be. Retreat dates book on shorter windows than the holiday season, and any date gets a straight yes or no by email. When the evening is a dinner in town rather than a retreat, client dinners has a page of its own.

What a resort date costs

What the corridor adds, and why.

A five to six piece band in Mountain West markets runs $2,500 to $6,500. That is the market for a resort date, and it sits above a metro date everywhere for the same two reasons.

The first is the drive. Vail and Beaver Creek are a mountain highway from Denver, Aspen is most of a working day each way, and the vehicle, the fuel and the hours are all real. Travel is its own line on the quote rather than folded into the fee, so what your leadership approves reads the same as a Denver evening with a drive attached.

The second is the night at the far end. A band that struck at eleven in Aspen is not driving back over the passes at one in the morning, so a far-resort date carries a night's lodging, billed at what it cost with the receipt behind it. The I-70 towns are close enough that most dates do not.

Then the calendar. A holiday weekend is priced above a standard date across this market, and the strong resort dates are decided early. Neither of those is a surprise, and both are named at contract rather than after it.

How a quote is put together is on the pricing page, and where the corridor sits against the wider Colorado market is set out in the cost guide. Send the date, the town and the venue, and the number for that night comes back.

Altitude, power and the load-in window

Resorts run on their own rules and we honour them as written. Decibel caps and curfews go into the plan at contract, with the last twenty minutes shaped to land on the curfew. The town-by-town caps, cited and dated, are on the mountain corridor page.

Power is the requirement most often missed: one or two dedicated 20-amp circuits within reach of the stage, dedicated meaning not shared with catering warmers or a lighting truss. Load-in runs 45 to 90 minutes depending on size, strike 30 to 60, timed to the dock and elevator windows the venue gives us. Footprints and circuit counts for every size are on the technical page, written to be forwarded.

Altitude is real. The corridor runs from about 7,900 feet in Aspen to roughly 9,600 in Breckenridge, singers and horn players pace a night differently up there, and the temperature swing between an afternoon sound check and a late set means more retuning across the evening. A dinner on the deck works too, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it and a move-indoors call written before the day, because mountain evenings drop fast.

The corridor

Four resort markets, four pages.

Each retreat market in the corridor runs on its own logistics, so each carries its own page: the venues that market books, the travel line that applies, and the questions specific to the town.

Vail
The corridor's incentive-event center, with the Sonnenalp and Arrabelle class of venue inside the villages and Donovan Pavilion at the edge of them. I-70 travel and Eagle County load-in patterns are on the Vail page.
Aspen
The far-resort booking, where the Little Nell and Hotel Jerome class of calendar meets the far travel tier: lodging at cost, and a two-day commitment for the band whatever the run of show says. The arithmetic is on the Aspen page.
Beaver Creek
On-mountain venues, Beano's Cabin above the Park Hyatt class of village property, and load-ins that run through a gated village. The Beaver Creek page covers the access windows that shape an evening there.
Breckenridge
The highest town on our travel card at roughly 9,600 feet, with a historic district doing the venue work that hotels do elsewhere. Altitude and the town's own caps are the Breckenridge page's subject.

Retreat questions

The wider operational set is on the planners question list, and the corridor's caps and curfews on the mountain page.

What does live music cost at a mountain resort?
A resort date costs more than a metro date for two reasons, both named on the quote as their own lines. Travel, because Vail and Beaver Creek are a mountain highway from Denver and Aspen is most of a working day each way. And a night's lodging past the passes, because a band that struck at eleven is not driving back at one in the morning. A five to six piece band in Mountain West markets runs $2,500 to $6,500, which is the market to budget against.
What does the resort need to provide?
One or two dedicated 20-amp circuits within reach of the performance area, a load-in window of 45 to 90 minutes depending on size, and the cap and curfew figures at contract. Dedicated is the word that matters: sharing a circuit with catering warmers is the most common cause of a load-in problem.
Do you honour decibel caps and curfews?
Yes, as written. Mountain venues run on their own rules, so tell us the cap and the curfew when you book and both go into the plan, including how the last twenty minutes are shaped to land on the curfew.
Can the band play outdoors at altitude?
Yes, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it and a move-indoors call time written before the day. Mountain evenings drop fast, and instruments and electronics both stop behaving in the cold. Altitude also changes pacing: singers and horn players tire faster in thin air, so the night is paced for it.
Is lodging marked up?
No. Lodging applies on far-resort dates, Aspen among them, and is billed at cost as its own line on the quote, the receipt and nothing added. It exists because far-resort dates are two-day commitments for the band, whatever the run of show says.
