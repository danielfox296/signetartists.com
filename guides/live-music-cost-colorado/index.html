The cost guide

What live music costs in Colorado.

Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a live band, and the Denver market runs $2,500 to $8,000, with a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top. Those are the market's published figures. This page is what they cover, why they spread that far, and what changes a price inside the range.

We don't publish our own prices, because the date, the size, the hours and the drive all change them. What's here is the market you're shopping in, sourced line by line, so you can set a budget before you write to anybody. The look

What the figures are paying for.

Road cases in a backstage hall and a stage deck under cover: the load-in and the sound system that sit behind the numbers in this guide.

What the figure is made of.

With most acts in this market, the figure has two parts. A call-out covers the evening committed, whatever its length: the drive, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. A rate for performance time covers the music. The split is why a one-hour cocktail set is never a quarter of a four-hour reception.

Overtime is the next thing to look for on a quote. This market charges $200 to $400 for time beyond the contracted set, which is worth knowing before the night. Added time with us costs what the earlier hours did.

Then there are the lines that sit outside the fee: travel beyond the metro, a night's lodging when the drive home crosses the passes after a late strike, and a holiday weekend, which this market prices above a standard date. Each of those belongs on its own line, because folding them in makes a quote look cheaper than the evening is.

Market context

Why the figures spread this far.

Live music here prices in tiers, and each one buys a different thing. Reading a number without knowing which tier it came from is how two quotes end up looking like the same purchase.

The marketplace tier
One booking marketplace averages $1,150 for a Denver party band, across listings running $230 to $3,550. The figure buys the act. What else sits inside it (sound, insurance, travel, production) varies listing by listing, so two quotes at the same number can cover two different evenings.
The working middle
The Denver market treats the four to six piece band as its middle, at $4,000 to $6,000, and Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a band across the whole state. Out in the resort corridor, a five to six piece runs $2,500 to $6,500 in Mountain West markets. Nationally most couples land $3,000 to $8,000, with the average sitting around $4,500. This is the range most private and corporate bookings are actually decided in.
The production tier
A seven to nine piece show band runs $7,500 to $17,000, and full production adds show direction, lighting, staffing and a program designed around the act. For a gala carrying a production budget, it's the right purchase.

Trio and duo

What a jazz trio costs, and what an acoustic duo costs.

These are the two sizes most Colorado evenings actually book, and they're the two the published guides skip over fastest. The figures below are the market's for each, at the length the market quotes them in.

The jazz trio
A trio across a four-hour evening runs $2,000 to $3,500 in this market. For a dinner rather than a full evening, a jazz trio or acoustic duo runs $500 to $3,000, and the spread inside that is how long the music plays and how much production travels with it. Denver has no trio figure of its own in print: the Denver band range of $2,500 to $8,000 begins at the trio and ends at a full band with horns, so the trio sits at the bottom of that range rather than in the middle of it.
The acoustic duo
An acoustic duo across a four-hour evening runs $1,500 to $2,500, the smallest full-evening figure published anywhere in this market. Two musicians cover a dinner or a cocktail hour without a stage, and for a short booking the Denver market puts a duo or trio at a ceremony or cocktail hour at $800 to $2,000. A duo evening and a trio evening are one musician apart, and the market prices that one musician as the difference between $1,500 to $2,500 and $2,000 to $3,500.
What moves either one
How many musicians there are, how many hours they play, and what the date is. A December Saturday in Denver carries 20 to 30 percent across this market, and a resort date carries the drive and a night's lodging at the far end of it.

What is a jazz trio?

Three musicians, and the instrument list is the part people are usually asking about. The traditional combos are piano, bass and drums, or organ, guitar and drums. The one that works at a private event is guitar, upright bass and drums played with brushes, because brushes are what let a drum kit sit under a conversation instead of over it. A quartet is the same three with a horn or a voice added.

At an event the trio does a specific job. It holds 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a level guests talk across without leaning in, and it covers a larger floor and a longer guest list than a duo at that same level. It takes 12 by 8 feet, one dedicated 20-amp circuit and about an hour to load in, so it fits a private dining venue as readily as a ballroom. What it plays is standards.

The instrumentation, the trio and duo configurations and a sample set are on the jazz duo and trio page. The singer-songwriter version of the same footprint is the acoustic duo, and the hour these two are most often booked for is the wedding cocktail hour. The whole size ladder, solo to quartet, hour by hour, is in what a jazz band costs in Denver, and the DJ figures are in what a DJ costs in Denver.

By occasion

What each kind of night comes to.

Four bookings cover most of the enquiries we see. Each one is a different configuration, and the market prices them differently.

The corporate holiday party
A cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 nationally, and December is the month this market charges above a standard date for: 20 to 30 percent on a holiday weekend in Denver. The season is decided August through October, and the formats a holiday party takes are on the holiday party page.
The client dinner
The conversation is the event, so the music sits under it: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level guests talk across. A jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner runs $500 to $3,000 in this market, the spread being how long the music plays and how much production comes with it. The format and the paperwork are on the client dinners page.
The private party
A cover or acoustic band for a private party runs $3,000 to $8,000, and a smaller build for the same music sits well under that: an acoustic duo over a four-hour reception runs $1,500 to $2,500 and a trio $2,000 to $3,500. What fits a home against what fits a club is on the private parties page.
The wedding cocktail hour
One hour between ceremony and reception, and the smallest real booking on this list. A classical duo or trio for a ceremony or cocktail hour runs $800 to $2,000 in Denver. What should play, and how the hour is timed against the ceremony, is on the cocktail hour page.

What does the mountain premium cover?
Travel and lodging. A resort date costs more than a metro date everywhere in this market for two reasons: the drive over the passes, and the night in a bed at the far end of it, because a band that struck late in Aspen isn't driving back over the passes the same night. Both belong on their own lines on a quote.
Is a band or a DJ cheaper?
A DJ, usually, and the gap narrows as the night gets longer. A Denver DJ carrying a night with production lighting runs $2,500 to $6,000, against $2,500 to $8,000 for a Denver band. A corporate night can use both, a duo through dinner and the DJ carrying the floor, on one contract and one invoice, which is cheaper than hiring twice.
Does booking early get a lower price?
No. Booking early buys availability, which on a December Saturday is worth more than a discount would be. What does change the price is the date itself: this market charges 20 to 30 percent for a holiday weekend, and New Year's Eve is its own case everywhere. Corporate holiday dates are decided August through October.
