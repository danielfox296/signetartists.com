The cost guide

What live music costs in Colorado.

A solo player runs $900–1,200. An hour of jazz duo is $1,050. A quartet through a Denver evening is $4,000–5,500, and the same quartet in the resort corridor is $5,500–7,500. Those are our published rates, and this page is the arithmetic behind them and the market around them.

What the figure is made of.

Two parts. A call-out covers the evening we commit, whatever its length: travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. An hourly rate covers performance time. The split keeps a one-hour cocktail set and a four-hour party both honest, because the evening is committed either way: the call-out carries the evening, and the hourly rate carries the music.

There is no overtime rate. Added time bills at the same hourly figure in 30-minute increments, agreed on the night, so hour five costs what hour four cost. Booking three or more parts of one night together takes 10% off performance fees.

Market context

Where these figures sit in the Colorado market.

Live music here prices in three tiers. Each buys a different thing, and a figure only reads right next to what it covers.

The marketplace tier
Commodity averages run roughly $1,600–$2,100 for a band on a standard date. The figure buys the act. What else sits inside it, sound, insurance, travel, production, varies listing by listing, so two quotes at the same number can cover two different evenings.
The boutique tier
Full production starts at $8,000 and up: show direction, lighting, staffing and a program designed around the act. For a gala carrying a production budget, it is the right purchase.
The published card, between them
High mid-market, and we say so. A quartet through a Denver evening is $4,000–5,500 with sound, insurance, timeline coordination and a named contact for the night inside the figure, and the whole card is published, so where a night lands is arithmetic you can run before you write to us.

By occasion

What each kind of night comes to.

Four bookings cover most of the enquiries we see. Each is priced below from the published card, and each occasion's own page carries the fuller picture.

The corporate holiday party
A quartet covers dinner at conversation volume, then fills the floor: $4,800 over four hours. The season's other winning shape pairs a duo through cocktails and dinner, $2,000–2,800, with the DJ carrying the late half on the same contract, and the added hours bill at the hourly rate with no second call-out. The season is decided August through October, and the open dates are published on the holiday party page.
The client dinner
The conversation is the event, so the music sits under it: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level guests talk across. An hour of jazz duo is $1,050, and the whole front of the night, arrival through dessert, is $2,000–2,800. The format and the paperwork are on the client dinners page.
The private dinner party
A solo player holds a dinner for twelve or a garden party for sixty at $900–1,200, and a duo adds an upright bass and a fuller sound at $2,000–2,800 for the evening. The formats, and what fits a home against what fits a club, are on the private parties page.
The wedding cocktail hour
One hour between ceremony and reception. An hour of jazz duo is $1,050, and a solo runs $900–1,200. What should play, and how the hour is timed against the ceremony, is on the cocktail hour page.

Cost questions, answered.

Why do live music prices vary so much in Colorado?
Because the market runs in tiers and quotes cover different scopes. Marketplace commodity averages run roughly $1,600 to $2,100, and what sits inside a figure varies listing by listing. Boutique production starts at $8,000 and up. Our published card sits between them, with sound, insurance and a named contact for the night inside every figure.
What does the mountain premium cover?
Travel and lodging. The resort column runs above the Denver column because the corridor costs real money to reach: the Front Range adds $300, the I-70 corridor adds $600, and the far resorts add $600 plus lodging billed at cost rather than marked up. Winter passes and late strikes make the overnight the safe call at the far resorts.
Is a band or a DJ cheaper?
A DJ, once the night runs past two hours. Four hours of DJ is $2,200 against $2,400 for a duo and $4,800 for a quartet, all published, while a single hour flips it: $1,050 for an hour of jazz duo against $1,150 for the DJ. The most common corporate pairing uses both, a duo through dinner and the DJ carrying the floor, on one contract and one invoice.
Does booking early get a lower price?
No. The rates are published and the figure holds wherever the date lands, apart from two published premiums: holiday and peak weekends add 25% and New Year's Eve adds 50%. Booking early buys availability instead. Corporate holiday dates are decided August through October, and the strong Saturdays go first.
