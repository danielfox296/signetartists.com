The cost guide

What live music costs in Colorado.

Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a live band, and the Denver market runs $2,500 to $8,000, a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top. Those are published figures from the market, not ours. This page is what they cover, why they spread that far, and what moves a number inside the range.

We do not publish our own prices, because the date, the size, the hours and the drive all move them and a single figure would be wrong for most of the nights it was read on. What is published here is the market you are shopping in, sourced line by line, so a budget can be set before anybody writes an email. Send us a date and you get our number for that night.

What the figure is made of.

Two parts, with most acts in this market. A call-out covers the evening committed, whatever its length: the drive, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. A rate for performance time covers the music. The split is why a one-hour cocktail set is never a quarter of a four-hour reception, and why an act that quotes purely by the hour is either carrying a very high hourly figure or leaving something out of it.

Overtime is the next thing to read. This market charges $200 to $400 for time beyond the contracted set, which is worth knowing before eleven o'clock rather than after. Ask whether the rate for hour five is the rate hour four was. Ours is.

Then the lines that sit outside the fee. Travel beyond the metro. A night's lodging when the drive home crosses the passes after a late strike. A holiday weekend, which this market prices above a standard date. Each of those belongs on its own line, because folding them in makes a quote look cheaper than the evening is.

Market context

Why the figures spread this far.

Live music here prices in tiers, and each one buys a different thing. Reading a number without knowing which tier it came from is how two quotes end up looking like the same purchase.

The marketplace tier
One booking marketplace averages $1,150 for a Denver party band, across listings running $230 to $3,550. The figure buys the act. What else sits inside it, sound, insurance, travel, production, varies listing by listing, so two quotes at the same number can cover two different evenings.
The working middle
The Denver market treats the four to six piece band as its middle, at $4,000 to $6,000, and Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a band across the whole state. Nationally most couples land $3,000 to $8,000. This is the band of figures most private and corporate bookings are actually decided in.
The production tier
A seven to nine piece show band runs $7,500 to $17,000, and full production adds show direction, lighting, staffing and a program designed around the act. For a gala carrying a production budget, it is the right purchase and the figure is not the interesting part of it.

Where a Signet night lands depends on the night. A solo player through a dinner for twelve and a quartet filling a floor until midnight are different jobs, and so are the same quartet in a Denver ballroom and in Aspen in February. By occasion

What each kind of night comes to.

Four bookings cover most of the enquiries we see. Each one is a different shape, and the market prices them differently.

The corporate holiday party
A cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 nationally, and December is the month this market charges above a standard date for: 20 to 30 percent on a holiday weekend in Denver. The season is decided August through October, and the shapes a holiday party actually takes are on the holiday party page.
The client dinner
The conversation is the event, so the music sits under it: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a level guests talk across. A jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner runs $500 to $3,000 in this market, the spread being how long the music plays and how much production comes with it. The format and the paperwork are on the client dinners page.
The private party
A cover or acoustic band for a private party runs $3,000 to $8,000, and a smaller build for the same music sits well under that: an acoustic duo over a four-hour reception runs $1,500 to $2,500 and a trio $2,000 to $3,500. What fits a home against what fits a club is on the private parties page.
The wedding cocktail hour
One hour between ceremony and reception, and the smallest real booking on this list. A classical duo or trio for a ceremony or cocktail hour runs $800 to $2,000 in Denver. What should play, and how the hour is timed against the ceremony, is on the cocktail hour page.

How much does a live band cost in Colorado?
Colorado couples budget $2,338 to $2,858 for a live band, and the Denver market runs $2,500 to $8,000, a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top. Nationally most couples land $3,000 to $8,000. Those are the market's published figures. Signet does not publish prices: send a date and the size of the night, and we price that night.
Why do live music prices vary so much in Colorado?
Because the market runs in tiers and quotes cover different scopes. One booking marketplace averages $1,150 for a Denver party band, the Denver market treats the four to six piece as its middle at $4,000 to $6,000, and a seven to nine piece show band runs $7,500 to $17,000. What sits inside a figure, sound above all, varies as much as the figure.
What does the mountain premium cover?
Travel and lodging. A resort date costs more than a metro date everywhere in this market for two reasons: the drive over the passes, and the night in a bed at the far end of it, because a band that struck at midnight in Aspen is not driving back over the passes at one in the morning. Both belong on their own lines on a quote rather than folded into a fee.
Is a band or a DJ cheaper?
A DJ, usually, and the gap narrows as the night gets longer. A Denver DJ carrying a night with production lighting runs $2,500 to $6,000, against $2,500 to $8,000 for a Denver band. A corporate night can use both, a duo through dinner and the DJ carrying the floor, on one contract and one invoice, which is cheaper than hiring twice.
Does booking early get a lower price?
No. Booking early buys availability, which on a December Saturday is worth more than a discount would be. What does move a number is the date itself: this market charges 20 to 30 percent for a holiday weekend, and New Year's Eve is its own case everywhere. Corporate holiday dates are decided August through October.
