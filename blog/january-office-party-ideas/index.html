January office party ideas

The company party that happens after the holidays: why the date works, seven ways to run it, and the themes that do not need a tree.

Signet Artists · September 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways
  • A growing share of companies hold the holiday party in January, and the venues have followed: several Denver-area holiday offers now run into the third week of January and past it.
  • In January the party is the only invitation your people have that week. In December it competes with everyone else's.
  • Nothing about the music changes. Standards, covers and a dance set were never seasonal, so a January party books the same acts as a December one.
  • The formats that want the crowd's attention, live band karaoke above all, get more of it in January than they ever get in December.

The planners who moved the party say the same two things about why. December belongs to everybody else, so the office party competes with three other invitations, a school concert and a flight home. And the people who plan it spend December planning it instead of attending anything. Move the date past the new year and the evening becomes the only thing on the calendar that week, the venue has dates, and the planner gets to enjoy the night they organised.

Denver’s venues have priced it in. Several now write their holiday packages to run from the middle of November into the third week of January and past it, with the same seasonal framing they use for December. The January party is the second half of the season now.

The January holiday party #

The party you’d have held in December, held in the second or third week of January instead. Same venue class, same bar, same speeches, with one difference guests notice immediately: everybody came. The music books the way a December night does, a duo through drinks and dinner and the DJ or a band taking the back half on the same contract. The holiday party page carries the season, January included.

The kickoff dinner with music under it #

January is when the sales team is in one place for the kickoff, and the evening after a day of sessions is a party by another name. A dinner with a jazz trio at conversation volume, 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, gives a group that spent eight hours in a ballroom a reason to talk to each other. If the night should turn after the awards, the same booking carries a floor act.

The live band karaoke night #

The January idea, because it’s the format that wants the crowd’s attention and gets all of it when the evening isn’t competing with the season. A real band plays the 80s and 90s hits on its own and hands the mic to anyone who wants a song, with the words on a screen and a host on the floor. Nobody has to sing for it to work, and by the third song the list fills on its own. The live band karaoke page has the songbook and the builds.

The awards-and-dance night #

The year in review, the awards for it, and a floor afterwards. The band plays dinner at talking level, goes silent for each walk-up and the remarks, and turns the floor on the emcee’s last word. A soul and R&B band or the yacht rock band for the second half, and the cues in the program handled the way the galas and award nights page sets out.

The low-key dinner with solo piano #

For the company that wants January to feel like a reset. A long table or a private dining floor, one musician at the piano through arrival and dinner, standards and covers the table talks across. It’s the smallest booking on the roster and the one that makes a dinner feel hosted without a program.

The apres-ski evening #

A January date at a Vail, Beaver Creek or Breckenridge venue is an apres-ski party by default: the snow is the theme. The Americana band plays the Colorado songbook by the fire, and the retreats page carries the drive and the lodging lines that a mountain date adds to the contract.

January work party themes that don't need a tree #

Winter white, which the venue is already dressed for. Apres-ski, in the city or in the mountains. The year in review, with the awards built into it. The second act of New Year’s, for the office that couldn’t get everyone together on the thirty-first. And the plainest one, which is no theme: a good dinner, a good band and a crowd that showed up because nothing else was on.

The music is the part of a January party that never needed a theme. Standards, covers and a dance set aren’t seasonal repertoire, so the same acts play a January night as a December one, and the acts that want attention get more of it.
