Office holiday party ideas in Denver

Seven shapes a Denver office party can take, each with the size it actually needs, and what this market charges for that size.

Signet Artists · August 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways
  • Every idea here is a format with a size attached, so the pitch and the build arrive together.
  • A cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 in this market, which is the figure to take into a budget conversation.
  • Denver holiday dates get decided between August and October, and a holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market. New Year's Eve is its own case again.
  • The venue is a separate decision from the entertainment. Sixteen venues that host this market are covered one by one in our venue guide.

The assignment usually lands sideways. Someone in a Tuesday meeting decides the holiday party should be better this year, and by Thursday it is yours. Ideas are not the hard part. The hard part is that ideas lists never come with numbers, so every option you bring back opens a second research project into what it costs, and the party gets planned at the speed of the slowest quote.

This list runs the other way. Each idea below comes with the build it actually needs, which moves a number far more than the choice of act does. For the budget itself: a cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 in this market, and Denver bands run $2,500 to $8,000, a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top. How figures like those are built is set out in what live music costs for a corporate event, and the sourced market table is on the pricing page. Where the party goes is its own decision, and the sixteen venues that host this market are covered one by one in the Denver venue guide.

The DJ night #

The most common single booking of the corporate season, and the simplest to approve: one line and one load-in, with the whole night covered. An open-format DJ reads the crowd from the first drink to the last song, on one line and one load-in.

This is the idea to pitch when the budget line is the point. Across this market a DJ carrying a night with production lighting runs $2,500 to $6,000, under what a band of any size costs for the same hours, and it still holds a dance floor.

The jazz duo dinner #

For the party that is really a dinner: a seated evening, a toast or two, and conversation as the main event. The jazz duo plays standards on upright bass and guitar at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a level people talk across without leaning in. Three hours is the usual shape of it.

If the group outgrows the duo, the trio adds drums played with brushes for larger floors. A jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner runs $500 to $3,000 in this market, which is the band this idea sits in.

The in-office afternoon #

Not every office party leaves the office, and the format that fits a catering spread on the main floor at the end of a workday is a small one. A single hour of the acoustic duo is a real booking rather than a minimum to work around: voice and guitar playing covers people know at a volume they can talk across. A solo guitarist is smaller again and sets up in 6 by 4 feet, which fits an office floor without moving the desks.

The dance floor #

A quartet through a four-hour evening, playing dinner at conversation volume and getting loud on cue when the program ends and the night turns. This is the booking for a party where the dancing is the point, and four players is what a ballroom floor actually needs. The horns and the second vocalist are what take a booking into show-band money, at $7,500 to $17,000.

The flagship version is Tejas Singh: a duo through arrival and dinner that grows to a quartet when the dancing starts, same voice, same songs, one contract. Guests hear one band getting bigger rather than two acts swapping over.

The band into the DJ #

The full-evening shape for a crowd that will outlast the band: a quartet for three hours, then a DJ carrying the late end through the same sound system on the same contract. Because the setup is already paid for, adding that hour costs far less than a second booking would. It comes to a five-hour evening with two different kinds of music in it, on one contract and one invoice.

Live-band karaoke #

The band learns the songs, your people sing them, and the floor belongs to whoever is brave enough to take it. It books as a chapter of the night added to any band booking, quoted as its own line. What is itemised separately on a quote is listed on the pricing page.

For one chapter of the night the people on stage are the people everyone came with, and a live band is behind them instead of a backing track.

The set piece #

Some parties want one thing guests photograph, and a format none of them have seen at an office party before. Dirty Flamenco is that booking: real palos played by a working flamenco trio, with a dancer configuration when the night wants a floor show. Its work is the set moment: the entertainment as the centerpiece of the party.

What the calendar does to the number #

Denver holiday parties get decided between August and October, and the calendar does most of the moving. There are 21 party dates across November and December, Thursdays through Saturdays, and a peak December date costs more than a standard one everywhere in this market: a Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent. New Year’s Eve is its own case again, and it books earlier than anything else on the calendar.
