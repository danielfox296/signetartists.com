Office holiday party ideas in Denver, priced

Seven shapes a Denver office party can take, each with its published figure attached, so the idea you pitch and the number you take to finance arrive together.

Signet Artists · August 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Every idea here is a format with a published rate, so the pitch and the price arrive together. The full card is on the pricing page.
  • The published card spans $1,500 to $5,500 for an office party evening. The bottom of the range is the DJ. The top is a quartet carrying the whole evening.
  • Denver holiday dates get decided between August and October. The peak December dates price at plus 25 percent, New Year's Eve at plus 50.
  • The venue is a separate decision from the entertainment. Sixteen venues that host this market are covered one by one in our venue guide.

The assignment usually lands sideways. Someone in a Tuesday meeting decides the holiday party should be better this year, and by Thursday it is yours. Ideas are not the hard part. The hard part is that ideas lists never come with numbers, so every option you bring back opens a second research project into what it costs, and the party gets planned at the speed of the slowest quote.

This list runs the other way. Each idea below is a format we publish a rate for, so the figure is attached to the idea the day you pitch it. The arithmetic behind every number is set out in what live music costs for a corporate event, and the whole card sits on the pricing page. Where the party goes is its own decision, and the sixteen venues that host this market are covered one by one in the Denver venue guide.

The DJ night #

The most common single booking of the corporate season, and the simplest to approve: one line and one load-in, with the whole night covered. An open-format DJ reads the crowd from the first drink to the last song, and the published figure for four hours is $2,200: an $800 call-out plus $350 an hour.

This is the idea to pitch when the budget line is the point: one booking that runs from the first drink to the last song and still holds a dance floor, at less than half what the quartet evening costs.

The jazz duo dinner #

For the party that is really a dinner: a seated evening, a toast or two, and conversation as the main event. The jazz duo plays standards on upright bass and guitar at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a level people talk across without leaning in. Three hours runs $1,950: a $600 call-out plus $450 an hour.

If the group outgrows the duo, the trio adds drums played with brushes for larger floors, and the same arithmetic carries: $1,000 call-out, $600 an hour.

The in-office afternoon #

Not every office party leaves the office, and the format that fits a catering spread on the main floor at the end of a workday is a small one. A single hour of the acoustic duo is $1,050, voice and guitar playing covers people know at a volume they can talk across. A solo guitarist runs $900 to $1,200 for the evening and sets up in 6 by 4 feet, which fits an office floor without moving the desks.

The dance floor #

A quartet through a four-hour evening is $4,800, playing dinner at conversation volume and getting loud on cue when the program ends and the night turns. This is the booking for a party where the dancing is the point.

The flagship version is Tejas Singh: a duo through arrival and dinner that grows to a quartet when the dancing starts, same voice, same songs, one contract. Guests hear one band getting bigger rather than two acts swapping over.

The band into the DJ #

The full-evening shape for a crowd that will outlast the band: a quartet for three hours, then a DJ carrying the late end through the same sound system on the same contract. Because the setup is already paid for, the DJ hour bills at the hourly rate with no second call-out. Quartet call-out $1,600, three hours at $800, one DJ hour at $350: $4,350 for a five-hour evening with two different kinds of music in it.

Live-band karaoke #

The band learns the songs, your people sing them, and the floor belongs to whoever is brave enough to take it. It books as a chapter of the night added to any band booking, published at $1,500 on the extras list.

For one chapter of the night the people on stage are the people everyone came with, and a live band is behind them instead of a backing track.

The set piece #

Some parties want one thing guests photograph, and a format none of them have seen at an office party before. Dirty Flamenco is that booking: real palos played by a working flamenco trio, from $2,800 in Denver, with a dancer configuration when the night wants a floor show. Its work is the set moment: the entertainment as the centerpiece of the party.

What the calendar does to the number #

Denver holiday parties get decided between August and October, and the calendar prices itself. There are 21 party dates across November and December, Thursdays through Saturdays, and the peak December dates carry a 25 percent premium on the fees: the $4,800 quartet evening becomes $6,000 on the second Saturday of December. New Year’s Eve adds 50 percent and books earlier than anything else on the calendar.

The season’s dates, premiums tagged, are laid out on the holiday party page, and whether yours is free is a two-line email: the date and the venue.

The entertainment decision and the venue decision usually run in parallel, and the venue’s fine print moves the music budget more than most planners expect: in-house catering mandates, start-time rules and load-in windows all live in the venue contract.
