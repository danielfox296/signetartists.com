What live music costs for a corporate event

A band's price has two parts. Here is what each one covers, what two hours costs against four, and what the total comes to for the formats a Denver corporate night actually takes.

Signet Artists · August 5, 2026 · 10 min read

Key takeaways
  • A cover or jazz band for a corporate event runs $2,500 to $8,000 in this market, and Denver bands run $2,500 to $8,000, a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top.
  • Most acts bill two things: a call-out covering the evening they commit to, whatever its length, and a rate for performance time. That's why one hour is never a quarter of four.
  • Sound is the line that most often makes two quotes at the same number cover two different evenings. Ask whether it's inside the figure.
  • Travel beyond the Denver metro and a December weekend are separate lines on a quote. A Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market.

The question usually arrives without much warning. Someone decides the holiday party should have a band this year, and by the end of that meeting you’re the person who has to find out what a band costs and report back with a figure somebody upstairs can approve.

Then you start looking, and the numbers that come back are hard to compare, because two quotes rarely cover the same things: one includes sound and the other assumes the venue has it, and travel is on one and missing from the other. Weeks go by and you still can’t put a line in a budget.

We don’t publish our prices, because a Saturday in December and a Tuesday in September aren’t the same job. What we can do is show you the market, sourced, so the budget conversation can happen today. Every figure below is somebody else’s published number, with the page it came from listed at the end. For ours, tell us the date and we’ll come back with a recommendation and a price.

Why the price has two parts #

A call-out covers the evening we commit to, whatever its length. That means travel, load-in, setup, sound check, strike, load-out, and the production that rides along with all of it. An hourly rate then covers performance time.

The split exists because of the short booking. A duo playing one hour of cocktails costs us the same evening a four-hour reception costs: the same drive, the same load-in, the same sound check, the same strike after the guests leave. Price it purely by the hour and the one-hour booking has to carry a rate nobody would agree to, or the four-hour booking has to subsidise it. Splitting the fee lets both come out honest, and it means the arithmetic on your quote is something you can check yourself.

What the market actually charges #

Read those next to what each one covers, because that’s where two quotes at the same number stop being the same purchase. A booking marketplace averages $1,150 for a Denver party band across listings running $230 to $3,550.

The other thing the table hides is size. A dinner for thirty and a ballroom of two hundred want different builds, and most corporate evenings need fewer musicians than the person booking them expects. The number moves far more on how many musicians are in the band and how many hours they play than on which act is standing there.

Two hours against four, which is how buyers actually ask #

Almost nobody types the question the way the industry answers it. What people search is how much it costs to hire a band for an hour, for two hours, for four. Hours are the unit the budget line is written in, so here’s what the market charges, read that way.

The first thing to know is that two hours isn’t half of four, and the reason is the call-out above. A two-hour booking costs the act the same evening a four-hour booking costs: the same drive, the same load-in, the same sound check, the same strike after the guests leave. Only the playing time is halved, and playing time is the smaller half of the fee. A short booking is closer to two thirds of a long one than to half of it, which is why the short end of every published range sits higher than the arithmetic suggests it should.

Read the two ends together. The Denver market puts a short ceremony or cocktail-hour booking at $800 to $2,000, and a jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner at $500 to $3,000 nationally, the spread inside that second figure being how long the music plays and how much production travels with it. A four-hour evening moves to $1,500 to $2,500 for an acoustic duo and $2,000 to $3,500 for a trio across this market, and a cover or jazz band carrying a corporate evening runs $2,500 to $8,000.

For a two-hour corporate booking specifically, the useful anchor is the dinner figure, because a two-hour job is almost always a seated evening with remarks in it. Two musicians for two hours is the cheapest real booking on any of these lists, and it’s the one most often over-specified: the quartet gets quoted for an evening that a duo would have carried.

Going the other way, the market’s price for time added on the night is $200 to $400 for each hour past the contracted set. That line is worth asking about before you sign anything, because a four-hour quote that becomes a five-hour night is where a budget gets broken after the fact.

Three common configurations of a corporate night #

A market range is abstract until somebody puts an evening against it. These are the three that come up most in a Denver corporate year, and the size each one actually needs.

A client dinner. A jazz duo for three hours, sitting under the conversation for the whole evening so nobody has to raise their voice across the table. That’s two musicians, low volume and no stage. This is the cheapest real booking on the list and it’s the one most often over-specified.

A holiday party with a real dance floor. A quartet for four hours, playing dinner at conversation volume and getting loud on cue. Four musicians cover a ballroom floor; the horns and the second vocalist are what take a booking into show-band money.

A gala that runs across the whole night. A quartet for three hours, a cocktail duo for the hour before it, and a DJ for the hour after. The thing worth knowing here is that covering all three from one booking is much cheaper than three separate ones, because the drive, the load-in and the sound system are paid for once. Ask any act you’re comparing whether they price it that way.

Factors that affect price #

Six things affect it, and each one should arrive as its own line on the quote and its own line on the contract. If they don’t, that’s the question to ask before you compare two numbers.

  • Travel. The Front Range is a short line. The I-70 corridor, which covers Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park, is a longer one. Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat and Crested Butte add travel plus a night’s lodging, billed as what it actually cost.
  • Holiday and peak weekends. A Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market.
  • New Year’s Eve. Its own case everywhere, and it books earlier than anything else on the calendar.
  • Live-band karaoke. Its own line, if the program wants a chapter where your people sing and the band plays behind them.
  • Learned songs. The first one is included with us; each one after that is its own line.
  • Overtime. This market charges $200 to $400 for each hour beyond the contracted set. We don’t: added time costs what the earlier time did, in 30-minute increments, so if the night runs long the only question left is whether the crowd wants more.

The holiday calendar, worked through #

The corporate season concentrates into eight weeks, so the December premium deserves its own paragraph. A Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market, applied to the fee for the music rather than to travel. On a strong December Saturday you should expect to be quoted above the ranges in the table above. New Year’s Eve is its own case again, everywhere.

There are 21 party dates across November and December in the Denver season, laid out on the holiday party page. If you’re still at the ideas stage, the ideas list runs seven party formats with a size attached to each.

What should live music cost for a company holiday party? #

Budget $2,500 to $8,000 for a cover or jazz band, which is what this market charges for the format most holiday parties book, and $2,500 to $8,000 if you want the Denver figure, a trio at the bottom of that and a full band with horns at the top. Then move the number for the date: a Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market, and New Year’s Eve is its own case everywhere.

If the party is a seated dinner rather than a dance floor, the figure is lower and the format is smaller: a jazz trio or acoustic duo for a dinner runs $500 to $3,000. If it’s a full evening with dancing in a ballroom, the four-hour figures above are the range you’re in, and the horns and the second vocalist are what take a booking into show-band money at $7,500 to $17,000.

Two lines decide whether the figure you take upstairs survives contact with the quote. They’re sound, which is inside some numbers and a later conversation with others, and travel, which is a short line on the Front Range and a longer one over the passes. Ask about both before you compare two figures.

What's inside every one of those numbers #

Comparing two band quotes is only possible once you know what each of them covers, and this is the part where quotes diverge most. At every size, ours includes:

  • Sound support that’s the right size for the venue
  • Load-in, setup, strike and load-out
  • A certificate of insurance
  • A W-9 and one itemised invoice
  • Timeline coordination before the event
  • Continuous audio during breaks
  • A personal contact for the night

Sound is the line worth checking hardest against another quote. A band that doesn’t carry production will either add it later or expect the venue to have it, and the venue’s house system was probably specified for speeches. Stage lighting and a dedicated engineer are optional add-ons, quoted as their own line when you want them.

Why any of this is published

In almost every corporate booking the person choosing the band and the person approving the spend are two different people, and they’re rarely in the same meeting. That’s the reason this page exists: the market figures on it can be forwarded, questioned and approved without anybody waiting on a vendor to reply. How far ahead should this be booked? #

Corporate holiday parties tend to get decided between August and October for November and December dates. New Year’s Eve goes earliest of all. Galas run longer, closer to ten or twelve months, and a mountain venue or a fall Saturday runs twelve to eighteen.
