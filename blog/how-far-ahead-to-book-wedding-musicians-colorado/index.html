WEDDINGS

How far ahead to book wedding musicians in Colorado

What couples actually report about their own timing, the order the decisions come in, and the eight things to have ready before you write to anybody.

Signet Artists · September 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Couples report shopping for wedding music nine to fourteen months out. That is what they say to each other in the forums, not a rule anybody enforces.
  • The venue decision comes first and settles most of the music decisions for you: the sound cap, the curfew, the power, and whether the ceremony is outdoors.
  • Shorter timelines work. What shrinks is the choice of act, and songs learned specially need four weeks' notice.
  • Eight things make an enquiry answerable in one exchange instead of four. They are listed near the bottom of this page.

Somebody has just said yes, there is a venue with a date on it, and somewhere on a list between the photographer and the flowers is the word music. The honest answer to how far ahead you need to sort it is that it depends on how particular you are, and the useful answer is the order to do things in, which is what most of this page is.

Start with what other couples say, because it is the only real data anybody has. In wedding forums, when couples describe their own timing, the phrases they use are “in about a year”, “roughly fourteen months”, “getting married in June next year” asked in the August before, and at the other end, “less than 80 days away”. Read across enough of those and the cluster sits between nine and fourteen months for the music, with the venue booked before it.

9 to 14 months
Where couples' own reported lead times for wedding music cluster. Private-party music, by contrast, gets shopped four to six weeks out, which is why advice written for one reads wrong for the other.

Why the number lands where it does #

Three things stack up to produce it, and none of them is really about music.

Engagements cluster in December, January and February, so a large share of couples start planning in the same eight weeks, for dates that are mostly in the following summer and fall. That is roughly a year of runway by arithmetic rather than by anybody’s rule.

Colorado then narrows it further. The outdoor season here is short and the good weather is concentrated, so a large share of the year’s weddings happen in a handful of months, and the mountain venues compress that further still. A June date in Colorado is competing with more other June dates than a June date in a state with a longer season.

Last, the music is downstream of the venue by necessity. Until you know where you are getting married you do not know the sound cap, the curfew, whether the ceremony is outdoors, or whether there is power where the players will stand. Couples work that out and then shop, which puts the music enquiries a few months behind the venue search.

The order the decisions actually come in #

The date and the venue. Everything else is a consequence of these two. Ask the venue for three things while you are still deciding: the decibel cap if it has one, the time music has to stop, and where the power is for anything happening outdoors.

How the day splits. Ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner, dancing. Most couples do not need the same music for all four, and most of the hours are conversation rather than dancing. Deciding the split is what turns a vague budget into a bookable one.

The ceremony. The processional and the recessional are specific choices, and they are the only part of the day where the music has to land on a cue. A solo guitarist or a duo covers a ceremony and then moves to the cocktail hour, which is one booking rather than two.

The cocktail hour. The hour where live players do the most work and the one couples most often skip. Whether you need it, what it should sound like and how many songs it takes are answered on our cocktail hour page.

The reception. Band, DJ, or both in sequence, which is the shape most full evenings land on. That decision has its own page in band or DJ for a Denver wedding.

What booking a musician actually involves #

Less than most couples expect, and the parts that take time are the parts nobody warns you about.

The enquiry is short. A date, a venue, roughly how many guests, and which parts of the day you want covered is enough for anyone competent to answer with a yes or a no and a number.

A contract is what fixes anything. Until a contract is signed, nothing is settled with any vendor, which is worth knowing because plenty of wedding advice online implies otherwise.

Then paperwork, which is the step that surprises people. Many Colorado venues will not confirm a vendor until they have a certificate of insurance naming the venue as additional insured. Ask your venue whether it requires one, and ask any musician you are considering whether they carry insurance at all. Plenty do not.

The detail comes much later and it is not urgent. Song choices, the processional, a do-not-play list and the run of show are settled in the weeks before the day rather than at booking. The one item with a real deadline attached is a song learned specially for you, which needs four weeks’ notice.

The eight things to have ready when you write #

An enquiry with these in it gets answered in one exchange. Without them it becomes four.

  1. The date, and whether it is fixed or still moving.
  2. The venue and its address, or the two you are choosing between.
  3. Roughly how many guests.
  4. Which parts of the day you want music for.
  5. Whether any of it is outdoors, and whether there is cover and power.
  6. The venue’s decibel cap and the time music has to stop, if it has given you either.
  7. Whether the venue requires a certificate of insurance.
  8. Anything you already know you want or definitely do not want.

Numbers five and six are the ones that change the answer most, and they are the two couples almost never think to include. Footprints, power and load-in by size are published on our technical page if your venue asks for them in writing.

What changes on a shorter timeline #

Short timelines are ordinary. Couples ask about music at eight weeks, at five weeks, and closer, and those weddings happen and are good. Two things are genuinely different.

The choice narrows. Fewer acts will be free for any given Saturday, which matters most if you have your heart set on one specific act and not at all if you have decided you want a jazz trio.

Special requests need lead time of their own. A song learned for your first dance takes four weeks’ notice. Inside that, the answer is the catalogue an act already plays, which for most couples is a better outcome anyway, because a band plays a song it knows better than a song it learned last week. What our roster already plays is on the repertoire page.

If the wedding is in the mountains #

Mountain dates ask for more planning and the reason is travel rather than music. A corridor date means a drive with weather on it, a dock or a shuttle at the other end, sometimes a night’s lodging, and altitude that changes how long a sound check takes. None of that is hard, but it is all easier to arrange with months in hand than with weeks.

The corridor numbers, cited and dated, are on our mountain page. If the weekend around the wedding is what you are planning, the welcome party and the rehearsal dinner are private parties in everything but name, and those book on their own shorter clock.

Methodology
What we measured
The lead times couples state about their own bookings, in their own words, in public wedding discussions. We counted what people said they did, not what vendors advise.
Sample
113 Reddit threads with their comment trees across 16 subreddits, plus 18 threads from WeddingWire and Weddingbee. Colorado-specific threads are thin because r/Denver removes vendor-recommendation posts under its own rules, so most of the reported timing is national.
Date range
Threads spanning 2023 to 2026, read on 2026-09-06.
Caveats
Forum posters skew toward planners who research online, which probably lengthens the reported lead times against the real average. Nothing here is a statement about what is available on any particular date, ours included.
What this page deliberately does not say

You will find plenty of wedding-music advice built on urgency, telling you which dates disappear first and how late is too late. We do not publish anything about our own calendar, because a booking calendar changes daily and a sentence about it on a page is out of date the moment it is written. Send a date and you get a straight answer about that date.

Send the date and the venue, whatever the timeline looks like.

Nine months out or nine weeks, the answer comes back the same way: a yes or a straight no for that date, and a number for the parts of the day you want covered.
