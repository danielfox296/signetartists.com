What a DJ costs in Denver

The word covers two different purchases, and the market prices them a long way apart. Here is what a DJ costs for four hours, for a wedding and for a corporate party, and where the production DJ with lighting sits above all of it.

Signet Artists · September 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • A Denver DJ's four-hour block runs $350 to $700 across this market, and a Denver DJ carrying a night with production lighting runs $2,500 to $6,000. Those are two different purchases with one name.
  • Most US couples pay a wedding DJ $1,000 to $2,500, and the 2026 national average is $1,800. The premium tier runs $3,000 to $5,000.
  • A four-hour corporate party DJ runs $300 to $500, the cheapest live-entertainment line a company event can carry.
  • A DJ's time runs $100 to $200 for each hour, and the number moves on the gear and the extras far more than on the hours.

DJ cost is the number every other number gets measured against. Couples compare the band to it, companies compare the karaoke night to it, and the person handed the office party opens a spreadsheet with it in the first cell. So it’s worth getting right, and the first thing to get right is that the market publishes two very different figures under the same word.

One is a working DJ for a four-hour block: a laptop, a controller, two speakers and somebody who reads the dance floor. The other is a production DJ: the same person with a lighting rig, a wireless microphone for the toasts, an MC role through the program, and a truck to carry it. Both are DJs. The market prices them a long way apart, and most of the confusion in DJ quotes is one of these being compared to the other. All of the figures below are somebody else’s published numbers. How much does a DJ cost for four hours? #

Four hours is the block the market quotes in, and it’s the right unit to price in, because a DJ’s evening is the same length whether the floor is full or not. The market puts a DJ for four hours at a private event at $400 to $800, and a Denver DJ’s four-hour block at $350 to $700. Denver is slightly under the national figure, which is typical of a market with a deep bench of working DJs.

For a corporate party the same four hours run $300 to $500, lower still, because a company party usually books the DJ without the wedding extras. That figure is the cheapest live-entertainment line a company event can carry, and the reason a DJ is the most common single booking of the corporate season.

How much does a wedding DJ cost? #

A wedding DJ costs more, and for a reason. The DJ runs the ceremony sound, the cocktail hour, the introductions, the first dance, the toasts and the open floor, and the package price reflects the whole day rather than four hours of it.

Most US couples pay $1,000 to $2,500, the 2026 national average is $1,800, and a wedding DJ for a four-hour block on its own runs $600 to $1,200. In a larger city the figure is $1,600 to $2,200, and the premium tier, seasoned professionals in major metros with full-day coverage, runs $3,000 to $5,000. Denver sits between the four-hour figure and the large-city one, depending on what the package carries.

What the market charges, from a four-hour block to a production night #

The table runs from the four-hour block at the top to the production night at the bottom, and the jump between the two Denver rows is the whole point. A Denver DJ for four hours and a Denver DJ with a lighting rig, a wireless system and an MC role are the same person with a different truck, and the market prices the truck. When two DJ quotes arrive a long way apart, the first question is which of these rows each one is quoting.

What moves the number #

  • The gear. Speakers sized to the venue, a subwoofer for a floor, uplighting, a dance-floor rig, a wireless microphone for the toasts. Each one is its own line on the quote, and together they’re the difference between the top and the bottom of the table.
  • The role. A DJ who also MCs the program, runs the introductions and holds the timeline is doing a second job, and the wedding packages price it in.
  • The hours. A DJ’s time runs $100 to $200 for each hour, and time added on the night runs $200 to $400 for each hour past the contracted set across this market. Hours move the number less than the gear does.
  • The date. A Denver holiday weekend carries 20 to 30 percent across this market, and New Year’s Eve is its own case everywhere.

A DJ against a band, and a DJ with a band #

This is the comparison most people are actually making. A live wedding band averages $4,500 nationally against the DJ’s $1,800, and Denver bands run $2,500 to $8,000. A band is something guests watch; a DJ is something guests dance to; and the even-handed version of that argument is on the band or DJ page.

The answer a lot of evenings settle on is both, in order. Live musicians play through arrival and dinner, and the DJ carries the late end through the same sound system on the same contract, which costs far less as one booking than as two. The other version is the hybrid: a saxophonist or a bassist playing on the floor over the DJ set, which is the difference between a playlist and a crowd watching something happen. Both are on the DJ page.
