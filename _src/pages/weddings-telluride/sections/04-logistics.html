Logistics

The drive, the night in town, the altitude and the sound hours.

Telluride is a far resort from Denver, about six hours each way over the passes, and a band that has driven six hours isn’t driving home the same night. So the quote carries three things honestly: the fee for the music, one travel line for the drive, and a night’s lodging at cost, which means the receipt with no markup on it. A winter date is planned with the passes in mind and the musicians travel a day early when the forecast says so.

The town allows amplified sound, indoors and out, from 7am to 11pm on Fridays, Saturdays and defined holidays under a permit, as long as it isn’t plainly audible beyond what the permit sets. That is the clock the floor is planned to, and a ceremony guitarist and a cocktail hour duo sit inside it on their own. The rule is cited and dated on our mountain corridor page, and we confirm the current version with the venue before the day.

The altitude reaches the gear and the guests. Telluride sits at 8,750 feet and Mountain Village at about 9,545, the air is thin and dry, strings drift between an afternoon ceremony and an evening set and get retuned, and anyone singing or playing a horn paces a set differently, so the sound check is longer here than anywhere else we play. A ceremony reached by gondola runs on a battery-powered system or the site’s own circuit, and the guitarist is set up and tuned well before the first guest steps off.
