The day

The three parts of a Telluride wedding day, and what suits each one.

The gondola sets the ceremony plan, the altitude sets the sound check, and the town’s amplified-sound hours set the last song. The music rides with the guests from one to the next.

The ceremony, up the gondola
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, with a small battery-powered system that carries the officiant’s microphone too, riding up with the guests to an overlook or a ranch on the mountain. Nothing about the ceremony music changes at nine thousand feet except the tuning, which happens twice, and the order and timing of the pieces is on the ceremony page.
The cocktail hour, on a Mountain Village terrace
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, while the couple is off with the photographer and the light is still on the peaks. The terrace turns cold the moment the sun leaves the canyon, so the hour is timed to the light and the move indoors is agreed before the day. What plays in the hour is on the cocktail hour page.
The floor, before eleven
A trio or a quartet in a lodge, on the Opera House stage or in a tent in Town Park, or the DJ from the end of dinner to the last song, with that last song planned to the town’s amplified-sound hours rather than discovered at the door. Tejas Singh starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet on one contract, and the wedding jazz band grows through the day the same way.

Every size arrives with sound, load-in and strike, a certificate of insurance and a W-9. The whole song list is on the repertoire page.
