The day

The three parts of a Telluride wedding day, and what suits each one.

The gondola sets the ceremony plan and the altitude sets the sound check. The music rides with the guests from one to the next.

The ceremony, up the gondola
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, with a small battery-powered system that carries the officiant’s microphone too, riding up with the guests to an overlook or a ranch on the mountain. Nothing about the ceremony music changes at nine thousand feet except the tuning, which happens twice.
The cocktail hour, on a Mountain Village terrace
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo, quiet enough to talk across, while the couple is off with the photographer and the light is still on the peaks. The terrace turns cold the moment the sun leaves the canyon, so the hour is timed to the light and moving indoors is agreed before the day.
The floor, inside the town’s sound hours
A trio or a quartet in a lodge, on the Opera House stage or in a tent in Town Park, or the DJ from the end of dinner to the last song, with that last song planned to the town’s amplified-sound hours rather than discovered at the door. Tejas Singh starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet on one contract, and the wedding jazz band grows through the day the same way.
