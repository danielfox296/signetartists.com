Altitude, power and the load-in window

Resorts run on their own rules and we honour them as written. Decibel caps and curfews go into the plan at contract, with the last twenty minutes shaped so the night ends on a high note rather than a cut-off. The town-by-town caps, cited and dated, are on the mountain corridor page.

Power is the requirement most often missed: one or two dedicated 20-amp circuits within reach of the stage, dedicated meaning not shared with catering warmers or a lighting truss. Load-in runs 45 to 90 minutes depending on size, strike 30 to 60, timed to the dock and elevator windows the venue gives us. Footprints and circuit counts for every size are on the technical page, written to be forwarded.

Altitude is real. The corridor runs from about 7,900 feet in Aspen to roughly 9,600 in Breckenridge, singers and horn players pace a night differently up there, and the temperature swing between an afternoon sound check and a late set means more retuning across the evening. A dinner on the deck works too, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it and a move-indoors call written before the day, because mountain evenings drop fast.
