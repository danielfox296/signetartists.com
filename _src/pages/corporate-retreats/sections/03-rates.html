What a resort date costs

What the corridor adds, and why.

A five to six piece band in Mountain West markets runs {{market:mountain_west_band}}. That is the market for a resort date, and it sits above a metro date everywhere for the same two reasons.

The first is the drive. Vail and Beaver Creek are a mountain highway from Denver, Aspen is most of a working day each way, and the vehicle, the fuel and the hours are all real. Travel is its own line on the quote rather than folded into the fee, so what your leadership approves reads the same as a Denver evening with a drive attached.

The second is the night at the far end. A band that struck at eleven in Aspen is not driving back over the passes at one in the morning, so a far-resort date carries a night's lodging, billed at what it cost with the receipt behind it. The I-70 towns are close enough that most dates do not.

Then the calendar. A holiday weekend is priced above a standard date across this market, and the strong resort dates are decided early. Neither of those is a surprise, and both are named at contract rather than after it.

How a quote is put together is on the pricing page, and where the corridor sits against the wider Colorado market is set out in the cost guide. Send the date, the town and the venue, and the number for that night comes back.
