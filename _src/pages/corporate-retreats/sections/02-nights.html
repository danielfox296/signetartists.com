The three nights a retreat books

The welcome dinner
The group has been travelling all day and the first night's job is conversation. A duo plays it at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, the level people talk across, so colleagues who flew in from three offices can actually meet.
The program night
This is the night with the awards, the remarks and the number for the year. Music runs up to the moment and stops while the speaker is still steps from the microphone, and the speaker gets an announcement microphone of their own.
The last night
This is the loud one. A quartet fills a dance floor at 88 to 95 dBA, inside whatever cap and curfew the resort sets, and where the retreat wants a named act in front, Tejas Singh leads the roster.

Each night is its own line on one contract, and a second set on the same evening usually costs less than a second booking would. Retreat dates get booked on shorter notice than holiday-season dates. When the evening is a dinner in town rather than a retreat, client dinners has a page of its own.

Daytime activities usually plan themselves, and the evenings are what a group remembers. Eight of them, with the size each one needs, are set out in corporate retreat evening ideas in Vail.

A retreat has mornings as well, and one booking on the roster is for them. Firstwater is a seventy-five-minute sound bath with the whole group lying on the floor and nothing to do, and it goes into the agenda to open a morning or close an evening.

A retreat evening that wants a songwriter has one on the roster. René Moffatt plays his own songs, folk and Americana on voice and acoustic guitar, solo or as a trio, and a deck or a fire after dinner is the setting the songs are written for.
