Downtown venues

The ballroom is booked through a dock.

Denver's December concentrates in the hotels off 17th Street, the Brown Palace and Oxford class of calendar, with the Four Seasons a few blocks south and the club floors at the University Club of Denver and the Denver Athletic Club holding the member events. One shape of night runs through all of them: a program with remarks in it, dinner at conversation volume, and a floor that has to fill by nine.

The dock is the first booking. A downtown hotel schedules its dock the way it schedules its ballroom. Load-in runs through a dock window and a service elevator with its own calendar, and the banquet team wants footprint, power draw and timing before either is confirmed. We settle all three with the venue before the day, so the first your guests know of it is a band already playing.

The commonest load-in problem is shared power. A quartet takes 14 by 10 feet, 90 minutes of load-in and two dedicated 20-amp circuits, and dedicated is the word that matters: sharing a circuit with catering warmers is the usual cause of trouble at load-in. A duo needs 8 by 6 feet, one circuit and an hour.

The full sheet by size, stage footprints to channel counts, is on the technical page. Hand the link to your venue contact and the back-and-forth stops. If the venue itself is still open, the Denver venue guide reads sixteen of this market's spaces one by one.
