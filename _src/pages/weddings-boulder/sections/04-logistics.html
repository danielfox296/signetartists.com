Logistics

The sound policy, the parking, and the short drive.

Boulder has its own rules on evening sound and every venue in it has a policy on top, so the first question we ask about a Boulder date is the number. Whatever your venue, HOA or rental agreement puts on evening sound, and whatever cut-off it sets, both go into the contract, including how the last set lands on the cut-off. A ceremony guitarist and a cocktail hour duo sit inside any of those numbers on their own. A dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor by design, which is why it goes indoors, and why a trio is the size most Boulder venues allow for it.

Parking is the question worth settling early. Downtown is metered, Chautauqua shares its lot with a trailhead that fills on summer evenings, and a house on the Hill shares its curb with students during the university year. Tell us the address and we plan the carry: a solo needs 6 by 4 feet and one dedicated circuit at the end of it, a duo 8 by 6 on one, a trio 12 by 8, and load-in at those sizes is one van and 45 to 60 minutes through whichever door the venue prefers.

The drive is one short line on the quote, up from Denver and back, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line, so the fee for the music itself is the one a Denver wedding sees. The evening cools fast once the sun is behind the Flatirons, so an outdoor cocktail hour is timed to the light and the move indoors is agreed ahead of time, and the ceremony gets a covered, level and dry spot with power run to it in case a summer afternoon turns.
