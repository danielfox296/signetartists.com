The day

The three parts of a Boulder wedding day, and what suits each one.

The neighbours set the volume and the venue sets the size. A guitarist and a duo fit anywhere in Boulder on their own; the floor is the part that has to be planned.

The ceremony, on a lawn or a porch
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, on a covered spot near the aisle with a small system that carries the officiant’s microphone as well. The system is small because it only has to reach the last row, and a guitarist stretches the processional to however long the walk takes. The order of ceremony music, and how many songs each part needs, is on the ceremony page.
The cocktail hour, at dinner volume
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a level the fence line never hears and the conversation sits on top of. On a Chautauqua porch or a hotel terrace this is the whole hour, and the move indoors when the evening cools is agreed before the day. What plays in the hour is on the cocktail hour page.
The floor, indoors
A trio of guitar, bass and drums fills a Boulder floor without a stage, and it’s often the biggest size the venue allows. Where the venue takes four, Tejas Singh starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet, and the wedding jazz band grows the same way through the day. The DJ takes the same sound system from the end of dinner to the last song, and the cut-off the venue sets is the cut-off we plan the last set around.

Every size arrives with sound, load-in and strike, a certificate of insurance and a W-9. The whole song list is on the repertoire page.
