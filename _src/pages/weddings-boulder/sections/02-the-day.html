The day

The three parts of a Boulder wedding day, and what suits each one.

The neighbors set the volume and the venue sets the size. A guitarist and a duo fit anywhere in Boulder on their own; the floor is the part that has to be planned.

The ceremony, on a lawn or a porch
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, on a covered spot near the aisle with a small system that carries the officiant’s microphone as well. The system only has to reach the last row, and a guitarist stretches the processional to however long the walk takes.
The cocktail hour, on the porch
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo, quiet enough that the fence line never hears it and the conversation sits on top of it. On a Chautauqua porch or a hotel terrace this is the whole hour, and moving indoors when the evening cools is agreed before the day.
The floor, indoors
A trio of guitar, bass and drums fills a Boulder floor without a stage, and it’s often the biggest size the venue allows. Where the venue takes four, Tejas Singh starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet, and the wedding jazz band grows the same way through the day. The DJ takes the same sound system from the end of dinner to the last song, and the venue’s cut-off is the one the last set lands on.
