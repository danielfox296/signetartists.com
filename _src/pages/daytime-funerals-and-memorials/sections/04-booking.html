Booking it

Planned with the officiant, on short notice.

Send the date, the venue and the time of the service as soon as you have them, even if the service is this week, and we’ll answer against the booking calendar. Then the musician and the officiant or the funeral director agree the order of service and the cues, so on the day nobody has to signal anything from a pew.

The songs are yours to choose. Hymns, the standards, a folk song, the song from a first dance forty years ago: tell us the titles and the musician learns what isn’t already in the set, with the first learned song included. Where a family isn’t sure, the musician suggests from what’s usually asked for, and the published song list is searchable on the repertoire page.

Churches, funeral homes and cemeteries each have their own rules about amplification and about where a musician can stand, and we ask them before the day rather than on it. A certificate of insurance goes to any venue that wants one, with a W-9 and one invoice.

What a solo musician or a duo costs in this market is on the pricing page. A service is priced as its own date, and a service and its reception together are one booking.
