The service

Where the music goes in a funeral or a memorial.

The order is the officiant’s or the family’s. These are the four places a musician usually plays, and any one of them can be booked on its own.

As people arrive
Twenty to thirty minutes of prelude while people find a seat, played by one guitarist or a pianist: hymns, the standards, and the songs the family names. It’s the easiest part to add, and it means the service begins in music.
During the service
One song, or two, at the point the officiant or the family chooses: after a reading, during a slideshow, before the eulogy. The song is the one that was theirs, instrumental on guitar or piano or sung, with the timing agreed with the officiant beforehand so the musician takes the cue from them.
The recessional and the graveside
Music as everyone stands and leaves, and at the graveside if the family wants it there. The same guitarist who played the chapel plays the graveside, without power, on a battery-powered system or none at all.
The reception or the celebration of life
An hour or two of music under the conversation at the church hall, the restaurant, the club or the house afterward, from a duo playing the standards or a singer with a guitar playing the songs the person loved. Where the family wants a jazz band, this is the place for it, and the jazz duo and trio page covers the sizes.
