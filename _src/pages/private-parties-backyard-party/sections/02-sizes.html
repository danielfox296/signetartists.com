The configurations

The patio size, the lawn size, and the DJ.

A backyard has two places a band can go, the patio and the lawn, and the size follows from which one the party is going to face.

The patio: one guitar, or a singer with a guitar
The size for a dinner on the deck or a party of thirty around it. A guitarist plays the standards or a singer with a guitar plays the songs people ask for, at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, and it sets up in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit through the nearest window. Tejas Singh is the act we recommend first for a patio, because he plays it on his own and the same booking grows to a quartet if the lawn turns into a floor. A guitarist can also play with a battery-powered system where there’s no socket at all.
The patio, bigger: the duo or the flamenco trio
Upright bass and guitar in 8 by 6 feet for a long dinner outdoors, or Dirty Flamenco at trio size for a summer evening that wants something with real technique in it, with a dancer when the evening wants a set piece. Both play at a volume the table talks across.
The lawn: the party band
A trio in 12 by 8 feet on one circuit, or a quartet in 14 by 10 on two, playing the songs everyone knows to a lawn with a floor on it. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, and the same band plays the dinner first at conversation volume before the yard turns.
The DJ, and the garage
A DJ sets up in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit and is the size that moves indoors easily when the loud part of the night has to. Live band karaoke is the other summer idea a house can hold: the four-piece with its host and two lyric screens takes 14 by 10 feet and two dedicated circuits, which is a garage with the cars out.
