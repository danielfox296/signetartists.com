The party

The surprise, the toast, and the set built for one person.

The surprise entrance
The guest of honour thinks it’s dinner with three friends, the private dining space is full of everyone they’ve known since school, and the band was already there playing at conversation volume, so the first thing they hear as the door opens is their song. The proposals and surprise songs page covers how a cue and a learned song work, and a surprise birthday runs on the same mechanics.
The toast
Somebody stands up with a glass, and the music stops before they reach the microphone rather than after. An announcement microphone is ready from sound check on, the band goes silent on the host’s cue, and it comes back in the moment the glasses go down. When the toasts are a program, three speakers and a slideshow, we plan them with you on the run of show.
The set built for the person
Tell us the name, the number and a few of the songs, and the set is built from the years the guest of honour actually cares about: the college years for a 40th, the wedding years for a 60th. The first learned song is included in every booking, and the whole song list is searchable on the repertoire page.
The 50th that dances anyway
Some 50ths sit down and some don’t, and the way to cover both is a band that plays dinner at conversation volume and then turns up. The yacht rock band, the soul and R&B band and Tony Medina all do that on one contract.
