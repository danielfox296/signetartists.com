Sizes

The size follows the number on the cake.

A 30th and a 50th are different parties, and the band that suits one is the wrong size for the other.

The 30th: a trio, and the DJ after it
The one that runs latest. A trio of guitar, bass and drums fills a floor without a stage, and the DJ takes the same sound system when the band is done. The funk band is the trio for a crowd that wants a groove.
The 40th: a band that plays both halves
A seated dinner and then the floor once somebody moves the furniture. Tejas Singh and Tony Medina both start small and finish as a four piece on one contract, and the soul and R&B band is the quartet for a crowd that grew up on those records.
The 50th: a jazz duo under the dinner and the toasts
More often a seated dinner with a toast in it than a floor. Upright bass and guitar, quiet under the conversation, silent for the toasts and back the moment the glass goes down. The jazz trio with brushed drums is the same booking for a bigger crowd.
The 60th and past it: one musician, or two
Earlier, warmer, more family and more of it sitting down. A solo guitarist, a pianist or the duo holds the evening from arrival through dessert, and a Spanish guitarist under a long family dinner is the request that comes up most.
