The booking

How a sing-along runs.

Two musicians, a stack of booklets, and an hour of songs a mixed crowd already knows.

The booklets
The duo brings the sing booklets and hands them out, so the words are in front of everyone who wants them. They’re the reason the act works for a crowd that has never rehearsed anything together, and they’re yours to keep for as long as the program runs.
The set
One set of 45 to 60 minutes, or two shorter sets with a break in between, drawn from the Beatles catalogue and built around the songs a crowd sings back. Requests are taken as they come, and the first one a client sends ahead is learned for the date.
The space
A duo sets up in 8 by 6 feet on the floor and needs one standard outlet. Load-in takes about an hour before the program starts and packing up takes about three quarters of an hour. A common area, a dining space after lunch, a lobby, a library meeting space, a school gym, a garden or a courtyard all work.
The volume
65 to 72 dBA at the nearest seat indoors, which is a level people can talk across and a resident at the back of a common area can still hear. In a small lounge the duo plays without amplification at all, and outdoors it lifts to carry a courtyard.
A standing date
The same afternoon each month, or every other week, on one contract with one personal contact for the whole run. The set changes from visit to visit, so a community hears new songs alongside the ones it asks for again.

Footprints, power and the outdoor cover for every size are on the technical page.
