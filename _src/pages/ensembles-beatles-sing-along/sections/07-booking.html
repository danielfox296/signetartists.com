Booking it

The paperwork, the budget line and the calendar.

A senior living community, a library or a school usually needs three things before a musician is paid: a certificate of insurance naming them as additional insured, a W-9, and an invoice they can match to a purchase order. All three come in the same email as the contract, so the vendor setup is finished before the first visit.

Tell us the date, the space and roughly how many people will be in it, along with the budget line you have for live music, and we’ll come back with a recommendation and a price for that date or for a run of them. A family party at a house and a standing monthly afternoon at a community both book the same way, on one contract with one personal contact.

Send the date, the venue and the headcount. What live music costs in this market is set out with the source beside each number.
