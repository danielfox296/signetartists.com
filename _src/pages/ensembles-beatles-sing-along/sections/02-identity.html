Your Bird Can Sing is a two-person Beatles sing-along. The duo plays the songs and hands out booklets with the words in them, so nobody has to remember the second verse and nobody has to be able to sing to join in. The Beatles catalogue is the one body of songs where a five-year-old and a ninety-year-old already know the same choruses, and the whole act is built on that overlap rather than on a light show or a costume.

It isn’t a tribute act and it isn’t an impersonation. Nobody wears a wig and nobody puts on an accent. What the duo does is play Hey Jude, Let It Be, Yesterday, Blackbird, Come Together and the rest of the songbook, and hand out the words so that everyone who wants to join in has them.

The booklets are what make it work with a crowd of mixed ages. A grandparent who can’t quite hear the verse still has the words in front of them, a child who has never heard the song can follow the line, and the parents in between stop worrying about whether they’re singing it right. The duo can take the volume anywhere from a common area at conversation level up to a courtyard with a hundred people in it.

It books as one duo and it plays wherever a duo fits: the common area of a senior living community, a library meeting space, a school gym, a community center, a back garden, a courtyard, or a function space after lunch. Two musicians set up small, play on the floor rather than a stage, and need one outlet.

This is a named act, booked under its own name rather than the names of the two musicians in it, and if either of them changes we tell you in advance.
