The configurations

A duo for the dinner, a band if it dances.

Most anniversaries are a seated dinner with a toast in it, so the duo is the size to price first, and the rest follows from whether the night ends on a floor.

The jazz duo
Upright bass and guitar playing the standards at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, in 8 by 6 feet on one circuit, for a dinner at home, at a club or in a restaurant’s private dining space. The jazz duo and trio page covers it, and the set is built from the years the two of them have been married rather than from a generic list.
A singer with a guitar
One voice and a guitar for the anniversary where the couple’s song has to be sung rather than played. Tejas Singh learns it for the night if it isn’t already in the book, and the first learned song is included in every booking. The same booking grows to a quartet if the party turns into a floor.
The Spanish guitarist for a long dinner
A guitar with real technique in it under a long table, for the 50th that is a dinner for the whole family and nothing else. Dirty Flamenco plays it as one guitarist or as a trio.
The band, for the 25th that dances
A trio in 12 by 8 feet on one circuit, or a quartet in 14 by 10 on two, for the anniversary where the couple’s friends are still the people who danced at the wedding. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, the same band plays the dinner first at conversation volume, and the DJ can carry the late end through the same sound system.

What live music costs in this market is on the pricing page, and the cost guide sets out the Colorado figures for each kind of night.
