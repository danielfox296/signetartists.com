Three shapes

The three shapes a private booking takes.

Nearly every enquiry on this page is one of these. Each has a size that fits it and a figure already on the card.

The dinner party

A table of twelve, or three tables of eight. The music sits under the conversation for the whole night: standards on upright bass and guitar, at a level you can talk across. An hour on its own is {{rate_hour:duo}}; arrival through dessert runs {{rate_range:duo}}.

The format is the jazz duo. A solo guitarist holds the same night at {{rate_range:solo}} where the table is smaller.

The cocktail party that ends in dancing

Drinks at seven, and by ten the furniture is against the wall. Trio size is the honest answer: guitar, bass and drums fill a floor without a stage, {{rate_range:trio}} for the night. Tejas Singh is the act we field first here, the one booking on the roster that changes size during the night, a duo through drinks that becomes the band the dancing needs, on one contract.

Where dancing is a maybe rather than the plan, an acoustic duo covers the night at {{rate_range:duo}}.

The milestone with a moment in it

A fortieth, an anniversary, an engagement dinner. There is a toast and sometimes a surprise, and the band plays around both: a dedicated announcement microphone is live from the end of sound check, so nobody borrows the singer’s mic mid-speech. Tejas Singh at quartet size is the dance floor booking, {{rate_range:quartet}}.

When the moment should be the thing guests photograph, Dirty Flamenco is the specific flavor: real palos, the traditional forms, at trio size, {{rate_range:trio}}, with a dancer configuration for a set piece. Engagement dinners sit here too, and the wedding cocktail hour that follows is its own page.
