Milestone birthdays

The birthday that gets a band.

Most birthdays get a restaurant. Every so often one gets a night built for it, because the number on the cake is one of the round ones and the people who would fly in for a wedding will fly in for this. That party is worth a band, and the size that suits it changes with the decade.

A 30th
The one that runs latest and dances hardest. A trio fills a floor without a stage, and a DJ carrying the back half through the same sound system is how the night outlasts the band.
A 40th
Usually two parties in one evening: people sit down and eat, then somebody moves the furniture. The shape that covers both is a duo through dinner that grows for the second half, which Tejas Singh does on a single contract.
A 50th
More often a seated dinner with a toast in it than a floor. Live players at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table let a table of forty talk, and the set is built from the years the guest of honour actually cares about rather than from a generic list.
A 60th and past it
Earlier, warmer, more family in the space and more of it sitting down. A solo player or a jazz duo holds this evening from arrival through dessert, and standards are the repertoire that reaches everyone in the family at once.

The mechanics stay the same at every one of them. A toast gets a dedicated announcement microphone, live on its own channel from the end of sound check, so nobody borrows the singer’s mic mid-speech, and a surprise entrance gets planned with the music rather than around it. Tell us the name, the number and roughly who is coming, and the set is built for that specific person.

The brief nobody in Denver answers.

One request turns up again and again for a milestone dinner at home, and it is oddly specific: a Spanish guitarist. Someone wants a table of twelve, a long dinner, and a guitar in the corner playing something with real technique in it. Asked out loud in this city, that request usually gets pointed at a music school or at a restaurant with a guitarist on a weeknight, because there is no obvious place to book one.

There is one here. Dirty Flamenco plays the traditional forms at trio size, with a dancer configuration when the evening wants a set piece, and a single guitarist covers a dinner table alone. Both play a dinner at conversation volume and neither needs a stage to do it.
