Where

A restaurant, a rooftop, a trailhead, a table at home.

A restaurant is the easiest place to do this. We ask the manager in advance, the musician takes a corner with one small case, and the place hears live music for half an hour before it hears your song. A private dining space is easier still, because the door closes.

A rooftop, a park or a trailhead is a different plan. There may be a permit, there’s often no power, and the wind decides what carries. A guitarist plays those with a battery-powered system or none at all, and we walk the spot with you beforehand, on a map or in person, so the musician is where the view is and not where the traffic is.

At home, a dinner for twelve with a guitarist in the corner is the booking that turns into an anniversary, an engagement party or a milestone birthday, and the private parties page covers what a whole evening at home looks like when the one song grows into a set. In the mountains, the mountain corridor page covers what changes at altitude and what a resort town allows outdoors.

Travel outside the Denver metro is its own line on the quote, and what a short booking runs across this market is in the cost guide. A song at a graveside or a memorial service has a page of its own: funerals and memorials.
