How it works

Background music, until the song.

The whole trick of a proposal with a musician is that the musician was already there.

Before
You tell us the place, the time, the song and who is in on it. We tell the restaurant or the venue a musician is coming, so the manager knows where the small case goes and the door staff expect them. The musician is set up early and playing a few songs at conversation volume, so it looks like the place just has live music that night.
The cue
The cue can be a hand signal, a text from a friend at the next table or a nod from the server, whatever you and the musician agree on beforehand. The song starts on it, and the song is the one you chose, learned for the day if it isn’t already in the book. The first learned song is included in every booking.
After
The set keeps going while the two of you get your breath back, or it stops so the moment stays yours. Some people want a second song for the walk out; some want the musician gone before anyone thinks to ask. Tell us which one you want.

The same plan covers a surprise at a birthday or anniversary dinner: a musician in the restaurant before the guest of honor arrives, a short set under the meal, and the one song, the wedding song or the one from the year they met, placed when the table is ready for it.

Who plays it depends on the song and the place: a solo guitarist when the song should be recognized without a voice, a singer and guitarist when the words are the point, a Spanish guitarist for the song above, a violinist for A Thousand Years or a film theme, and the jazz duo when the place wants a little more sound.
