How it works

Background music nobody looks at twice, until the song.

The whole trick of a proposal with a musician is that the musician was already there.

Before
You tell us the place, the time, the song and who is in on it. We tell the restaurant or the venue a musician is coming, so the manager knows where the small case goes and nobody asks questions at the door. The musician is set up early and playing a few songs at conversation volume, which reads as the place having live music that night.
The cue
A hand signal, a text from a friend at the next table, a nod from the server. Whatever you and the musician agree on beforehand. The song starts on it, and the song is the one you chose, learned for the day if it isn’t already in the book. The first learned song is included in every booking.
After
The set keeps going while the two of you get your breath back, or it stops so the moment stays yours. Some people want a second song for the walk out; some want the musician gone before anyone thinks to ask. Both are fine. Tell us which.

The same plan covers a surprise at a birthday or anniversary dinner: a musician in the restaurant before the guest of honour arrives, a short set under the meal, and the one song, the wedding song or the one from the year they met, placed when the table is ready for it.

Who plays it: a solo guitarist when the song should be recognised without a voice, a singer and guitarist when the words are the point, a Spanish guitarist for the song above, and the jazz duo when the place wants a little more sound.
