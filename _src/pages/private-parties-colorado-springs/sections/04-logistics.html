Logistics

The drive down, and what the weather asks for.

Colorado Springs is a Front Range drive, not a mountain date. That distinction is the whole logistics story. Travel is one flat line on the quote covering the run down I-25 and back, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line, and the fee for the music itself is the same one a Denver host sees. Load-in runs 45 to 60 minutes at these sizes, one van, through whichever door the venue prefers.

The city sits at about 6,035 feet, high enough that guests from sea level feel it and low enough that it changes nothing about how players pace a set. The corridor pages carry the altitude planning; this one does not need it. What the Springs does ask for is a plan for the afternoon. Wind comes off the front of the range and drops as the sun does, and an evening on a terrace can lose fifteen degrees between the first drink and the toast.

So outdoor bookings here get three things written down before the day: a covered, level and dry performance area, power run to it, and an agreed move-indoors call. Instruments and electronics both stop behaving in the cold and the wet, and a duo moves indoors in a few minutes when the call is made early enough. Footprints, circuits and input lists for every size are published on the technical page, in a form a resort's events office can check line by line.
