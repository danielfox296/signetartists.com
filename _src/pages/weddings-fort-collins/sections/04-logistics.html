Logistics

Wind, weather, a river, and an hour up I-25.

Fort Collins is an hour up I-25 from Denver, so the drive is one flat Front Range travel line on the quote, the same line a Boulder or Colorado Springs date carries, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging. A ceremony up the Poudre canyon adds the canyon road and a drop in cell coverage to the plan, so the load-in is timed with the venue and the musicians leave early for it.

The wind is the Fort Collins fact. The plains east of town and the foothills west of it both get an afternoon wind and a fast afternoon storm, and an unamplified guitar on a lawn disappears in the first gust. An outdoor ceremony gets a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it, a small system that reaches the last row, and a move-indoors plan agreed before the day. A ranch without a circuit near the site needs a generator, and what that involves is set out in what changes when the reception is outdoors.

The sound rule is the venue’s own. A ballroom or a barn sets a number, a ranch near houses has neighbours, and Old Town has a city ordinance on evening sound, so tell us the number and the cut-off when you book and both go into the contract. A ceremony guitarist and a cocktail hour duo sit inside any of them on their own; a dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor by design, which is why the reception is indoors or in a barn with the doors shut.
