The day

The three parts of a Fort Collins wedding day, and what suits each one.

The wind sets the ceremony plan, Old Town sets the cocktail hour, and the reception is a ballroom or a barn. The music moves with the guests from one to the next.

The ceremony, on a ranch, by the river or in a courtyard
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, with a small system that carries the officiant’s microphone and reaches the last row across a lawn or over the sound of the Poudre. Outdoors here the system matters more than in the city, because a breeze off the plains takes an unamplified guitar with it.
The cocktail hour, in Old Town or on the patio
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo, quiet enough that the conversation sits on top of it, in a hotel courtyard, a taproom’s event space or on a ranch patio while the couple is off with the photographer. A singer and guitarist is the one-musician version of the same hour.
The floor, in a ballroom or a barn
A trio fills a barn floor without a stage, a quartet carries a ballroom past a hundred and fifty, and the DJ takes the same sound system from the end of dinner to the last song. Tejas Singh starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet on one contract, the Americana band is the one for a ranch wedding with the Colorado songbook in it, and the wedding jazz band grows through the day the same way.
