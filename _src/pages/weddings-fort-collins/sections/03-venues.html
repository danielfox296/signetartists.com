The venues

Old Town, the foothills, and the plains.

Old Town holds the downtown wedding. The Elizabeth Hotel and the Armstrong Hotel put the ceremony, the cocktail hour and the reception within a block of each other, Block One on Linden Street is the contemporary hall for a couple who want one floor and their own vendors, and the Lincoln Center is the city’s own hall for a bigger guest list. Their courtyards and terraces take a duo, their halls take a trio or a quartet, and parking downtown is the question to settle with the venue early.

West of town the foothills hold the estate wedding: Tapestry House, the 1890 manor with its lawns, and the ranches along the Poudre, where a ceremony beside the river books a guitarist with a system that carries the vows over the water and a reception in a barn books a trio. The Mishawaka, up the canyon on the river, is the venue for a couple who want the water in every photograph, and the canyon road is part of the plan rather than the price.

East and north, on the plains, Windsong Estate sits on a hill with the whole Front Range in view, and the wind that goes with the view is why an outdoor ceremony there gets a covered spot and a small system rather than an unamplified guitar. A ranch wedding out that way is where the Americana band does its best work.
