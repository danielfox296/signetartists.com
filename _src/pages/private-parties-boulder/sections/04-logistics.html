Logistics

The drive, the load-in and the last set.

Travel from Denver is one flat line on the quote, the published Front Range rate. No mileage arithmetic and no lodging line: Boulder is a drive home. It sits on the rate card and on the contract as its own row, so the performance figure you compare is the same one a Denver host sees. November and December dates run on the corporate season's clock, decided August through October, and New Year's Eve books first.

Load-in at these sizes is small: one van, 45 to 60 minutes, through whichever door the venue prefers. The Boulder question worth settling early is parking. Downtown is metered, Chautauqua shares its lot with a trailhead that fills on summer evenings, and a house on the Hill shares its curb with the university year. Tell us the address and we plan the carry; a solo needs 6 by 4 feet and a single dedicated circuit at the end of it, which most Boulder houses have to spare.

The curfew is a design input, and honoured as written. Give us the cap and the cut-off when you book and both go into the plan, including how the last twenty minutes are shaped so the night lands on an ending rather than a stop. Footprints and power for every size are on the technical page, and the Denver page carries the house-booking detail at metro scale.
