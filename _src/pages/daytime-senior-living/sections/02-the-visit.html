The afternoon

What a visit looks like.

Forty-five minutes to an hour of music in the common area, built from the decades the residents know, with requests taken as they come.

The set
One set of 45 to 60 minutes, or two shorter sets with a break, drawn from the standards and the Great American Songbook: Autumn Leaves, All of Me, Someday My Prince Will Come, The Girl from Ipanema, and the rest of a songbook nobody has to be told about. The duo plays it without a singer, so the melodies carry the songs and anyone who wants to sing along can. A singer with a guitar is the version for a community that wants the words: the Beatles, Motown, the folk songs and the country songs from the years the residents were young.
The space
The common area, the dining hall after lunch, the activity space, the lobby, a courtyard in summer. A duo sets up in 8 by 6 feet and a solo in 6 by 4, on the floor, and needs one outlet. Load-in is 45 minutes to an hour before the program starts, packing up takes half an hour, and the musicians come and go through whichever door the community prefers.
The volume
65 to 72 dBA at the nearest seat, the level of a conversation, so a resident at the back of the space can hear the melody and a resident at the front can talk to the person next to them. In a small lounge the guitar plays without amplification at all.
Memory care
For a memory care neighborhood the same musicians play a shorter set to a smaller group, closer to the residents and at the low end of that volume, and the set leans on the songs most likely to be recognised: the standards, a hymn or two, and the songs of the fifties and sixties. The musician follows the residents’ lead on requests and on when a set should end.
A standing date
The same weekday afternoon each month, or every other week, on one contract with one personal contact for the whole run. The set changes from visit to visit, so the residents hear new songs alongside the ones they ask for again, and in December the same musicians play the season’s songs, which the Christmas jazz trio page covers.
