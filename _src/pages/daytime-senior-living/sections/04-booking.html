Booking it

The paperwork, the budget line and the calendar.

A community’s business office usually needs three things before a musician is paid: a certificate of insurance naming the community as additional insured, a W-9, and an invoice it can match to a purchase order. All three come in the same email as the contract, so the vendor setup is done before the first visit.

Tell us the afternoon you have in mind, how often you’d like a visit and the budget line you have for live music, and we’ll come back with a recommendation and a price for that run of dates. A single visit for a resident’s milestone birthday, a Mother’s Day tea, a summer barbecue in the courtyard or a holiday open house for families is the same booking for one date, and a family evening that grows into a party is what the private parties page covers.

What live music costs in this market, with the source for each figure, is on the pricing page. Footprints and power for every size are on the technical page, written so a facilities manager can check it line by line.
