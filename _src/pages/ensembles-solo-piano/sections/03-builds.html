The booking

One musician, the piano, and a voice when you want one.

The piano
When the venue has a piano, the pianist plays it, and a hotel lobby or a club dining space usually does. When it doesn’t, a stage piano comes with the booking through the sound support every act carries, and nobody at the table hears the difference.
With a voice
On its own the piano is instrumental, and that’s the version most client dinners and galas book. When the evening wants singing, a voice joins for a set or for the night, and the booking stays one line on the contract.
The footprint
A 6 by 4 foot corner, one dedicated 20-amp circuit, a 45-minute load-in and a 30-minute strike when the piano travels with us. A venue’s own piano needs none of that, only the tuning the venue already keeps up.

It plays at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table for the whole booking, and a pianist can’t drift up to dance floor volume, which is the reason organisers ask for one by name for the dinner where the talking is the point.

Footprints and power by size are on the technical page. When the night wants a floor after dinner, a band or the DJ takes the same sound system on the same contract.
