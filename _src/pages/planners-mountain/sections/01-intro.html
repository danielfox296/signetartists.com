Mountain corridor

The rules above 7,000 feet.

Noise caps, curfews, altitude and travel for a band playing Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge, Aspen, Telluride or Steamboat. Every figure below is cited and dated at the foot of the page, and the towns we could not confirm are named as unconfirmed instead of guessed at.

Ninety decibels on the floor. Fifty at the fence.

Most planning conversations about volume happen at the town level. Colorado also caps noise statewide, and where the state number is the lower of the two, that is the number you have to meet. The Noise Abatement Act sets maximum levels measured 25 feet or more from the property line, and exceeding them is prima facie evidence of a public nuisance.

Zone7am to 7pm7pm to 7am
Residential55 dB(A)50 dB(A)
Commercial60 dB(A)55 dB(A)
Light industrial70 dB(A)65 dB(A)
Industrial80 dB(A)75 dB(A)

Two riders matter for an evening event. Between 7am and 7pm the limit rises by 10 dB(A) for up to fifteen minutes in any one hour, which is useful for a ceremony and useless for a dance floor. And after 7pm, a residential zone sits at 50 dB(A) at 25 feet, which is below conversation. A private estate or ranch reception in a residential zone is constrained by that number whatever the band does.

A town permit may not cover you

On 8 September 2025 the Colorado Supreme Court held that a municipality cannot authorise a private for-profit venue to exceed the state caps. The 1987 amendment that exempts cultural and entertainment events applies to property used by a government body or a nonprofit, and the court read that narrowly: Salida could not issue amplified-sound permits for concerts at a commercial bar.

For a planner this changes one habit. A sound permit from the town, held by a commercial venue, is no longer the end of the question. Ask the venue who holds the property and under what exemption, and get the answer before the contract rather than after a complaint.
