Altitude

Denver sits at 5,280 feet and the corridor runs well above it. Breckenridge is the highest of these at roughly 9,600 feet, Mountain Village above Telluride is about 9,545, Winter Park about 9,052, Telluride 8,750, Crested Butte 8,909, Vail 8,150, Beaver Creek near 8,100, Snowmass Village 8,104, Aspen 7,908 and Steamboat 6,732.

Two consequences reach the run of show. Singers and horns tire faster in thin dry air, so a band that plays four hours at 9,600 feet paces the night differently from the same band in Denver. And the temperature swing between sound check and the last set is wider up there than it is on the Front Range, which means more retuning across the evening.

Both are reasons to build a longer sound check into the document and to put water on the stage.

Travel and lodging

The I-70 corridor adds travel. The far resorts add travel and, past the passes, a night’s lodging billed at what it cost. Both sit on the quote and on the contract as their own lines.

Lodging bills at what the receipt says, and it appears on the far-resort tier because of I-70 and the passes. A drive that takes two hours in September takes considerably longer on a February Sunday, and a band that has driven six hours to Telluride isn't driving home the same night. If you'd rather we price the night without lodging and drive it, tell us and we'll look at it with you.

One more winter fact worth knowing early. Independence Pass closes, so Aspen from Denver runs the long way round through Glenwood Springs.
