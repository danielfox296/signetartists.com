Altitude

Denver sits at 5,280 feet and the corridor runs well above it. Breckenridge is the highest town on our travel card at roughly 9,600 feet, Mountain Village above Telluride is about 9,545, Winter Park about 9,052, Telluride 8,750, Crested Butte 8,909, Vail 8,150, Beaver Creek near 8,100, Snowmass Village 8,104, Aspen 7,908 and Steamboat 6,732.

Two consequences reach the run of show. Singers and horn players tire faster in thin dry air, so a band that plays four hours at 9,600 feet paces the night differently from the same band in Denver. And the temperature swing between a five o’clock sound check and an eleven o’clock last set is wider up there than it is on the Front Range, which means more retuning across the evening.

Neither is a problem. Both are reasons to build a longer sound check into the document and to put water on the stage, and both are why a band that has worked these venues before behaves differently at 8pm from one that has not.

Travel and lodging, and why they are separate lines

The I-70 corridor adds travel. The far resorts add travel and, past the passes, a night’s lodging billed at what it cost. Both sit on the quote and on the contract as their own lines, so the fee for the music your client sees is the same one a Denver client sees.

Lodging bills at what the receipt says, and it appears on the far-resort tier for one reason: I-70 and the passes. A drive that takes two hours in September takes considerably longer on a February Sunday, and a band that has driven six hours to Telluride is not driving home at one in the morning. Where the arithmetic favours it, tell us and we will price the night without lodging and drive it, though we will say plainly when we think that is the wrong call.

Winter dates carry one more line worth knowing about early: Independence Pass closes, so Aspen from Denver runs the long way round through Glenwood Springs.
