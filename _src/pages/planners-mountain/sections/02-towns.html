Town by town

Checked 7 August 2026. Municipal codes change, and several of these towns were actively revising their noise rules when we looked. Treat this as the starting question to put to your venue, not as the answer.

Aspen
The strictest and the clearest. Outdoor amplified sound at a private event is capped at 80 dBA at the perimeter, between 9am and 8pm. A public event with a Special Event Permit gets 100 dBA until 9pm. Neighbours within 250 feet get seven days’ notice, and a property with a noise variance may hold two events a week. An 8pm outdoor cutoff reshapes a wedding timeline, so plan the loud hour indoors or plan it earlier.
Telluride
Amplified sound, indoors and out, runs 7am to 11pm on Fridays, Saturdays and defined holidays, provided it is not plainly audible beyond the parameters the permit sets. The permit application asks for expected decibel levels at stated distances from each source, which is a question we can answer for you in writing.
Breckenridge
Reported limits are 55 dB(A) from 7:01am to 10:59pm and 50 dB(A) outside those hours in residential zones, and 70 and 65 in commercial. The town manager can permit above them at their discretion. Verify with the venue: we could not reach the code text directly.
Steamboat Springs
Reported at 60 dB(A) from 7am to 7pm and 55 dB(A) from 7pm to 7am. Amplified sound in a public place needs permission from the city manager.
Vail
Reported at 65 dB(A) outdoors in the resort villages, measured ten feet from the permitted area, with the town manager able to allow amplified sound for up to four consecutive days. Vail was reviewing these rules as outdoor programming grew, so confirm the current figure with the venue.
Beaver Creek, Winter Park, Snowmass Village, Crested Butte, Mountain Village
Not confirmed. We would rather say so than publish a number your venue then contradicts. Send us the venue and we will get the answer from the town before you have to.

One pattern holds across all of them. The published cap is a property-line or perimeter figure, and a dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor. Those two numbers are not comparable, and the distance between the band and the property line is what reconciles them. That distance is a site question, which is why we want the venue name early.
