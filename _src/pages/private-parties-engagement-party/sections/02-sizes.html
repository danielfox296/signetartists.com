The configurations

A duo for the families, a band if it dances.

An engagement party is usually a dinner or a cocktail party for forty, so the duo is the size to price first, and the rest follows from whether the night ends on a floor.

The jazz duo
Two families who’ve just met need to be able to hear each other, so the duo plays the standards on upright bass and guitar at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table and never above it. It fits in 8 by 6 feet on one circuit, at home, on a patio or in a restaurant’s private dining space, and it’s the same duo that plays a wedding cocktail hour, which makes the engagement party the first time the families hear the wedding’s musicians. The jazz duo and trio page covers the size.
A singer with a guitar
The song the two of them will dance to at the wedding, sung once at the engagement party by Tejas Singh, so the first dance gets a rehearsal nobody calls one. He learns it for the night if it isn’t already in the book, the first learned song is included in every booking, and he takes requests from both sides of the table for the rest of the evening.
The Spanish guitarist
A guitar with real technique in it under a long dinner, which is the first clip above. Dirty Flamenco plays it as one guitarist or as a trio, and the Spanish guitar is also the sound couples book for the ceremony itself.
The trio, for the party that dances
When the friends throw it rather than the parents, it’s a party with a floor, and that books a trio in 12 by 8 feet on one circuit or a quartet in 14 by 10 on two. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, and the DJ is there for the friends who want the night to run past the band.
