The practical side

What a daytime booking involves.

Daytime venues are rarely built for music, and that’s fine, because the sizes that play these bookings don’t need them to be. Here it is in one place, written to be forwarded to whoever signs off.

Space
A solo sets up in 6 by 4 feet and a duo in 8 by 6, on the floor of a common area, a dining hall, a chapel or a patio, with no stage. A sound bath needs enough floor for everyone to lie down.
Power
One dedicated 20-amp circuit within reach of the setup. Outdoors, where there’s no power, a guitarist plays on a battery-powered system or none at all.
Load-in
45 minutes for a solo and an hour for a duo, before the program starts, and 30 to 45 minutes to pack up afterward. A sound bath sets up about ninety minutes ahead of doors.
Volume
65 to 72 dBA at the nearest seat, which is the level of a conversation. Sets run 45 to 60 minutes with music through the breaks, and in a small space the guitar plays without amplification at all.
Paperwork
A certificate of insurance naming the venue as additional insured, a W-9 and one itemised invoice come in the same email as the contract, which is usually what a facility, a city or a library’s business office needs to set up a vendor.
Standing dates
A monthly afternoon, a weekly brunch or a summer series runs on one contract with one personal contact for the whole run. Tell us how often, and we’ll come back with a recommendation and a price for that run of dates.
