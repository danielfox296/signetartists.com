The day

The three parts of the day, and what suits each one.

Colorado Springs weddings run outdoors until the light goes and indoors after it, and the music has to follow the guests from one to the other. Each part of the day has a size that suits it.

The ceremony, outside
One guitarist, acoustic or Spanish, on a covered spot near the aisle with a small system that also carries the officiant's microphone. Nylon or steel strings carry in open air, and the musician stretches the processional to fit however long the walk takes. How ceremony music is ordered and how long each part runs is on the ceremony page; the nylon-string version has its own Spanish guitarist page.
The cocktail hour, on the terrace
An acoustic duo or a jazz duo, voice and guitar or guitar and upright bass, at a level two guests who have just met can talk across. The Springs is a terrace city, and the hour after the ceremony is the one guests remember being outdoors for. What plays, how many songs an hour takes and whether anyone notices are answered on the cocktail hour page.
The floor, inside
A band through dinner that becomes the dance band after it, like Tejas Singh, on one contract and one load-in, or the DJ from the end of dinner to the last song. Most Springs receptions settle on the band into the DJ, through the same sound system, so the music doesn't stop when the musicians do.

Every size arrives with sound, load-in and strike, a certificate of insurance and a W-9. The whole song list is on the repertoire page.
