Logistics

Sun, wind and a microphone for the officiant.

An outdoor ceremony in Colorado Springs is planned around two things the venue can't control. The first is the light: the sun drops behind the front range early, the colour on the rocks goes with it, and a ceremony timed for that light has a fixed start the music is built around. The second is the wind, which comes off the range in the afternoon and settles as the sun does. A guitarist's system is small and low to the ground for that reason, and the ceremony spot is chosen with the venue for cover as much as for the view.

The officiant's microphone rides on the same small system as the guitar, so the vows carry to the back row without a second vendor.

The drive is one flat line on the quote, the run down I-25 and back, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line, and the fee for the music itself is what a Denver wedding sees. The city sits at about 6,035 feet, which guests from sea level notice and musicians don't. Load-in runs 45 to 60 minutes at these sizes, and when the ceremony and the reception are in different buildings the second act loads in while the first is playing.
