The configurations

The size that reaches the table and the lawn.

The graduate’s friends and the graduate’s grandparents don’t want the same music, and the size that solves it is the one that can play both.

A singer with a guitar
One voice and a guitar in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit, playing the songs the whole family knows through the food and the songs the graduate asked for once the table thins out. Tejas Singh is the act we recommend first for a graduation party, because he builds a request from the ground up with a looper and the same booking grows to a quartet if the lawn turns into a floor.
The jazz duo for the family lunch
Upright bass and guitar in 8 by 6 feet, playing the standards at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table for the graduation that’s a lunch for the family first. The jazz duo and trio page covers it.
The DJ for the friends
A DJ in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit, with a set built around the graduate’s own playlist and the songs that have to happen, is the size the friends actually want, and it moves indoors easily when the loud part has to.
The party band
A trio in 12 by 8 feet or a quartet in 14 by 10 for a graduation party big enough to have a floor. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, and live band karaoke, the band that hands the microphone to whoever wants a song, is the one idea that gets the graduate’s friends and the graduate’s parents up in the same hour, because the book runs from the 80s and 90s onward.

What live music costs in this market is on the pricing page, and the cost guide sets out the Colorado figures for each kind of night, house parties included.
