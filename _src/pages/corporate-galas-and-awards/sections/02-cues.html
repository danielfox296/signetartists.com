Doors to dance floor

What the band does at every cue in the program.

The run of show is planned with whoever is running the program, and on the night the bandleader watches the emcee.

The doors
A reception act is already playing when the first guest walks in, at a level the check-in table can hear itself over. That can be a duo, a solo pianist or a Spanish guitarist, depending on what the evening should feel like in its first ten minutes.
Remarks and the welcome
The music stops while the speaker is still steps from the podium, and the speaker gets an announcement microphone of their own.
Dinner
The band plays at conversation volume, 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, from the first course to the coffee, so the tables can talk. A trio usually does this better than a full band turned down.
The video
The band goes silent, with the handoff to the house audio planned in advance, and comes back in on the applause.
The auction and the paddle raise
The band stays silent through the bidding unless the auctioneer wants music playing low under it, and plays a short piece as each lot closes if the program calls for it.
The walk-ups
Each honoree gets music from the moment the name is read to the moment they reach the podium, cut on arrival rather than faded over their first sentence. We take the names and the order ahead of the night.
The turn
When the last award is given and the dance floor opens, the night gets loud on cue: a dance band or the DJ through the same system, without a changeover the guests can see.
