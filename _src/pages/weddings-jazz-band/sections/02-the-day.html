The day

Four moments, four sizes of the same band.

A wedding jazz band is not one thing all day. It’s one or two musicians at the ceremony, two on the terrace, three at dinner and four on the floor, and the guests hear it as one band getting bigger.

The ceremony
A solo jazz guitarist or the duo, playing a prelude of ballads while the seats fill, the processional you chose, and something with a lift in it for the walk back up the aisle. The small system carries the officiant’s microphone too. How ceremony music is ordered and timed is on the ceremony page.
The cocktail hour
Upright bass and guitar, at a level guests who’ve just met can talk across. Standards are the repertoire because nobody has to be told what they are. The hour, what plays in it and whether anyone notices are on the cocktail hour page.
Dinner
The trio, with the drums played with brushes so the kit sits under the tables instead of over them. Toasts get an announcement microphone and the music stops while your speaker is still steps from it. Changes Jazz Trio plays this size under its own three names, with a modern edge on the book.
The first dance and the floor
The quartet, which is the trio plus a horn or a voice. The swing and the soul end of the standards book fill a floor for the hour after the first dance, and the DJ carries the late end through the same sound system, on the same contract, so the music never stops when the musicians do.

The whole song list is on the repertoire page, and the first learned song, your first dance if it isn’t already in the book, is included in every booking.
