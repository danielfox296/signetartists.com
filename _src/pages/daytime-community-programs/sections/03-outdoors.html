Outdoors

Power, weather, permits and paperwork.

Most of these bookings are outside, or in a building with no sound system of its own, so here is the practical side, written to be forwarded to the market manager or the facilities office.

Power
One dedicated 20-amp circuit within reach of the setup, where there’s power. Where there isn’t, a guitarist or a singer with a guitar plays on a battery-powered system or none at all. A band with drums needs the circuit, and a park stage with its own sound system needs a conversation with whoever runs it before the day.
Weather
A covered, level and dry performance area, and an agreed rain call for moving indoors or cancelling, written down before the day. Colorado mornings start cold and afternoons turn fast, and instruments and electronics stop behaving in the wet.
Permits
A park, a plaza or a public right of way sometimes needs a permit for amplified sound, and the market or the city usually holds it. We ask before the day, and where the answer is no amplification, the acoustic sizes play without it.
Paperwork
A certificate of insurance naming the city, the district or the library as additional insured, a W-9, and one itemised invoice that matches the purchase order, all in the same email as the contract.
Footprint
6 by 4 feet for a solo, 8 by 6 for a duo, 12 by 8 for a trio, on the ground or on a stage. Load-in takes 45 minutes for a solo and an hour for a duo or a trio, before the program starts.

Footprints, inputs and circuits for every size are on the technical page, written so a facilities office can check it line by line. A market or a program in a resort town is covered on the mountain corridor page, altitude included. What live music costs in this market is on the pricing page.
