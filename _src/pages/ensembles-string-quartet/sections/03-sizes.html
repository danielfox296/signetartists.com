Sizes

One violin for the aisle, a quartet for the ballroom.

Solo violin
One violinist, and the smallest strings booking. It plays a ceremony from the prelude to the recessional, an hour under a dinner for twenty, or the one song at a proposal.
Duo
Violin and cello. The cello carries the bass line under the melody, so Canon in D sounds the way people remember it and a processional has weight in a big space. It’s the size most often booked for the ceremony and the cocktail hour together.
Trio
Two violins and cello, or violin, viola and cello. The harmony parts arrive with the third player, so the pop songs and the film themes fill out, and it holds a cocktail hour on a terrace or a long dinner at a level the table talks across.
String quartet
Two violins, viola and cello, the size the quartet repertoire was written for. It’s the booking for a church or a ballroom, for a gala’s first hour, and for a reception that wants the full sound under dinner.
