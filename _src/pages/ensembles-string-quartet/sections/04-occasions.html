Occasions

Who books classical strings.

The wedding ceremony
Strings play all four parts of a ceremony, from the prelude while guests find their seats to the recessional. A violinist watches the aisle, so the processional lasts exactly as long as the walk, and in a church in Denver or Colorado Springs, or a ballroom in the mountains, the quartet carries where one instrument would be lost.
The cocktail hour
The same musicians walk from the ceremony to the terrace and play Coldplay, the Beatles and Taylor Swift arranged for strings, at a level guests talk across, on the same contract as the ceremony.
Galas and awards nights
A string quartet playing as the doors open is the sound of a black-tie evening. It’s the reception act on a gala’s contract, with the dinner act and the dance floor act on the same one.
Client dinners
A duo or a trio under a dinner for twenty to sixty, so the evening has live music in it and the conversation stays the point.
Anniversaries and milestone birthdays
A violinist or a trio at a dinner at home, a fiftieth anniversary at a club or an eightieth birthday, with the song from their own wedding on request.
Proposals and one-song bookings
One violinist for one song, A Thousand Years or the film theme that’s theirs, at a restaurant table or a trailhead.
Funerals and memorial services
Ave Maria, Amazing Grace and the hymns the family chooses, on one violin or violin and cello, in a chapel, at a graveside or at a celebration of life.
