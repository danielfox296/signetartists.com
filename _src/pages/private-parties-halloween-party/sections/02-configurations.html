The configurations

A DJ, a party band, or the band that hands over the microphone.

A Halloween party is a floor first and a dinner second, if it’s a dinner at all, so these are the three configurations a house books for it, in the order hosts ask.

The DJ
The usual first question, and the easiest one for a house: a DJ sets up in 6 by 4 feet on one dedicated circuit, loads in over 45 minutes, and plays the songs a costume crowd wants, built around the decade your guests grew up in. Tell us the crowd and the songs that have to happen, and the set is built for that party. The DJ can also take the late end of a night after a band, through the same sound system on the same contract.
The party band
A trio or a quartet playing the hits to a floor with no stage in it. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, the blues and the rock and roll that came out of it, and Tejas Singh plays a quartet built from the songs everyone in the house knows. A trio takes 12 by 8 feet on one circuit and a quartet 14 by 10 on two, and either can play the early part of the night at a volume people talk across before the floor turns.
Live band karaoke
The band plays the 80s and 90s hits on its own, sung out front, and hands the microphone to anyone in costume who wants a song, with the words on a screen and a host keeping a sign-up list on paper, so nobody has to sing for the night to work. Queen, Prince, Madonna and Bon Jovi are in the book. The four-piece with its host and two lyric screens takes 14 by 10 feet, two dedicated circuits and a 90-minute load-in, which means a big main floor, a finished basement or a garage with the cars out. The live band karaoke page covers how a guest gets to the microphone.
