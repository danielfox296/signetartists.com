Where it books

Six places a sound bath fits during the day.

The session is the same in each of them. What changes is who is lying on the floor and what the venue needs to supply.

A yoga studio’s schedule
A studio adds it as a class or a workshop on its own schedule: a Sunday morning, a Saturday afternoon, the session before a retreat weekend. The studio’s students lie down, the facilitator tunes the sound system to the studio, and for seventy-five minutes the space sounds like a different one.
An office at lunchtime
A session in a conference space or on a cleared floor, inside a long lunch, for a wellness week or the week after a hard quarter. The lights go low, and the session has a stretch of real silence in the middle of it.
A senior center or a senior living community
A sound bath for seniors is the same session with the lights low and the floor covered in mats and blankets, and a resident who can’t get down to a mat rests in a seat or a recliner instead. Nobody is asked to do anything but rest, and the facilitator tunes the sound system to the space.
A hospice or a recovery center
A session for the people in care, for their families, or for the staff. The session is guided in the lightest way, nobody is asked to speak or to share, and someone who can’t lie on a mat rests where they are.
A school
A staff day, a session for teachers at the end of a term, or a session built into a student program. A gym or a hall holds a whole staff on mats.
A retreat morning
The morning that opens a retreat, at a lodge in the mountains or a house on the Front Range. A sound bath in the morning and live music that night is one booking on one contract, and the corporate retreats page covers the evenings.
