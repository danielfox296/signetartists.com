The configurations

The size that fits the house.

A holiday party at home is usually smaller than the host imagines, because the music has to fit where the party already is. These are the four sizes a house books in December, and any of them can be the whole night or one part of it.

One guitar, or a singer with a guitar
The size for an open house or a dinner for twelve. A jazz guitarist plays the Christmas book with the standards threaded through it, or a singer with a guitar plays the songs your guests ask for, and either sets up in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit with a 45-minute load-in. Where the night is going to want a floor later, Tejas Singh is the act we recommend first, because he plays a dinner on his own and the same booking grows to a quartet for the dancing.
The Christmas jazz duo or trio
Upright bass and guitar, with drums played with brushes when the party is bigger than a dinner. It plays the Guaraldi record, the standards that are Christmas songs and the standards that aren’t, at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, and the balance of season to standards is yours to set. The duo sets up in 8 by 6 feet and the trio in 12 by 8, both on one circuit. The whole December book is on the Christmas jazz trio page.
The party band
A trio or a quartet playing the songs everyone in the house knows, for the part of the night when the furniture moves. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, and live band karaoke is the version where the band plays the hits and hands the microphone to whoever wants a song, which needs a big main floor or a finished basement, because the four-piece with its host and two lyric screens takes 14 by 10 feet and two dedicated circuits.
The DJ
On their own, or after the band through the same sound system on the same contract. A DJ sets up in 6 by 4 feet on one circuit and loads in over 45 minutes, which makes it the easiest late end of a night a house can book. The band or DJ question has its own post.
