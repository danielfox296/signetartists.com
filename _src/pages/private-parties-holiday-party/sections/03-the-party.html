The party

Three holiday parties a house throws.

Most December requests we get from a home are one of these three, and the size follows from which one it is.

The open house
Neighbors, family and the people from work drifting in and out over an afternoon and an evening, so there are twelve people in the house at one point and sixty at another. The music has to work at both, which is why this is a solo or a duo playing the season with the standards threaded through, so the people who stay the whole time don’t hear the same carols twice. It sits in the corner the party naturally faces and never gets louder than the conversation around it.
The seated dinner
One long table, or a few of them, and the meal is the point of the night. Upright bass and guitar play under it at conversation volume, and a toast gets an announcement microphone so nobody has to shout over the table. When the dinner is for someone, a fortieth in December or an anniversary that falls in the season, the milestone birthday section on the private parties page covers how the set gets built for that person.
The party that ends in dancing
The cocktail party where the coffee table ends up in the hall and somebody’s aunt is the first one dancing. A trio or a quartet fills a main floor with no stage in it, the same booking can play dinner first at a volume people talk across, and the DJ can take the late end of the night through the same sound system. It’s the loudest of the three, so it’s the one to book earliest and the one to keep indoors.
