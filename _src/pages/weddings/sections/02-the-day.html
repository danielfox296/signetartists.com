wedding day music

Three moments, three different jobs.

The ceremony
The one stretch of the day where everyone - friends and family alike - are paying close attention to it. The music is exposed and the timing has to be precise. A guitarist or a violinist plays the guests in, plays the processional, plays softly under the ring exchange if the couple wants it, and plays the recessional. Want to know more about how the different parts of the day come together, or how many songs each act needs? Learn more on the ceremony page
The cocktail hour
This is the hour when the vibe turns from formal to slightly less so. As the couple leaves for photographs, guests stand with a first drink feeling out what kind of night is ahead. The venue may be turning ceremony seating into dinner tables behind a wall.

A duo covers the whole hour at a level guests talk freely over. What plays, how many songs, whether anyone notices and whether a duo is enough for seventy guests are all answered on the cocktail hour page.
The reception
A floor that has to fill and stay full for hours, and that's a DJ's job rather than a duo's. An open-format DJ reads the crowd from the first dance to the last song. Where a wedding wants live musicians for dinner and a DJ after it, that's easy with Signet. One contract.

What live music costs across Colorado, format by format and figure by figure, is set out in the cost guide. The morning after, a brunch at the hotel with the same duo, is on the Sunday brunch page. An engagement party months before the day, with the same duo, is on the private parties side.
