For whoever is timing the day

Wedding dates get decided ten to twelve months out, and twelve to eighteen for a mountain venue or a fall Saturday. Music tends to be shopped nine to fourteen months out, later than the venue and earlier than almost everything else on the list. That is the calendar, not a countdown: send a date and you get a yes or a straight no, with the figure for that day attached.

Most of a wedding's music questions are really timing questions, and the working documents live on the planners pages: stage footprints and power by size, load-in and strike durations, what arrives with the contract. How each moment hands off to the next sits on the run of show page, and altitude, curfews and mountain-venue detail on the mountain corridor page.

The paperwork is the fast part. A certificate of insurance naming the venue, a W-9 and one itemised invoice come in the same email as the contract, and a named contact with a mobile number in writing is reachable from load-in to load-out.
