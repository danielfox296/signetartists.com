Where it lands

Where Spanish guitar lands.

Flamenco is a centerpiece booking, so it follows the events that have a centerpiece moment in them. Each occasion links to the page that prices the whole night around it.

The member or association event
An event that has remarks in it and wants one thing guests describe afterwards: a set piece, placed where the program leaves space for it. The client dinners and member events page covers the club and firm side of the calendar.
The corporate dinner with one photographed moment
Dinner runs at conversation volume and the set piece lands after remarks, placed against the timeline the way a toast is. The corporate page sets out the three shapes an enquiry takes and the finance path behind them.
The dinner party at home
Twenty around a long table, a courtyard or a back garden, and Spanish guitar under the whole meal at a level nobody has to talk over. This is the smallest booking the act takes and the one it fits most naturally, because flamenco was played in courtyards long before it was played in halls. A dinner party gets decided on a far shorter calendar than a wedding does, and the venue is usually somebody's own address.
The milestone birthday
A fiftieth, a sixtieth, an anniversary, a retirement: a night about one person, with a centerpiece the guest of honour does not have to host. Spanish guitar does that without a microphone in anybody's hand and without a program to run. Private parties and dinners covers the whole night, and the Denver page covers the city's venues and logistics.
The wedding, ceremony or cocktail hour
Spanish guitar wedding ceremony music is a search of its own, and it is the same instrument doing a quieter job: processional, prelude and recessional played on nylon strings. The ceremony page covers how the music is ordered and how long it runs. For the hour after it, an hour of palos instead of standards at the same conversation volume, the cocktail hour page covers length, timing and what plays.
