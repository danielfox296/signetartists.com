Where it lands

The ceremony, the terrace, the long table, and the single hour.

Spanish guitar follows the moments that want one instrument and no fuss around it. Each occasion links to the page that plans the whole event around it.

The wedding ceremony
Prelude, processional, an interlude if the ceremony has a still moment in it, and the recessional, on nylon strings. Spanish guitar wedding ceremony music is what a lot of couples are picturing when they say they want something warmer than strings. How the four parts are ordered and how long each runs is on the ceremony page.
The cocktail hour
An hour of rumbas and familiar songs on a terrace, at a level two guests who have just met can talk across. The cocktail hour page answers how many songs the hour takes and whether anyone notices.
The dinner party, and the milestone birthday
Twenty around a long table, a courtyard, a back garden with the lights strung: Spanish guitar under the whole meal is the booking this instrument was made for, because it was played in courtyards long before it was played in halls. Private parties and dinners covers the night, and the Denver page covers the city's venues and parking.
The corporate dinner
Dinner at conversation volume with one instrument that makes the evening feel hosted, for a client dinner or a member event that wants one thing guests mention afterwards. The client dinners page covers the paperwork side: net-30 on approval, a certificate of insurance with the contract.
The single hour
A proposal on a terrace. The toast at a sixtieth. A surprise at the end of an anniversary dinner. One player, one hour, no stage, on the same contract and with the same certificate of insurance as a full night. It is the booking people ask for at short notice, and it goes on the calendar like any other.

Where a solo sits against every other size is in the cost guide, and what live music costs in this market is on the pricing page.
