The set

The pieces people mean by Spanish guitar, and the songs they know.

A Malagueña, rumbas, a farruca, tangos, Entre dos Aguas: the pieces that sound like Spain to a guest who could not name one of them. Around those sit familiar songs played on nylon strings in the same hand, which is how Can't Help Falling in Love ends up in a processional and a Beatles song ends up under dessert.

It is instrumental, and it runs at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, the same dinner level every size on this roster plays. One player cannot drift above it by accident, and a guitarist watches the guests rather than a clock, so a prelude stretches while people find their seats and a processional lands its last chord where it belongs.

Two pages sit either side of this one. Dirty Flamenco is the form played in full, guitar, bass and cajón, with the traditional palos and a set piece placed where the program leaves space for it. The solo guitarist is one player on steel strings working blues, roots, jazz and the singer-songwriter catalog, sung or not. This page is the nylon-string solo in between: the sound of the trio, sized for a ceremony, a terrace or a dinner table.

The set is built with you before the night, and the first learned song you ask for is included in every booking. The roster's wider covers list is on the repertoire page.
