The day

The three parts of a Vail wedding day, and what suits each one.

The mountain sets the clock. The light leaves the ceremony site early, the terrace turns cold fast, and the village has a noise limit the floor has to respect. The music follows the guests from one to the next.

The ceremony, on a deck or a lawn
One guitarist, acoustic, jazz or Spanish, on a covered spot near the aisle with a small system that carries the officiant’s microphone too. The system is small on purpose: it sits inside the village cap on its own, and the guitarist stretches the processional to however long the walk takes. The order and timing of ceremony music is on the ceremony page.
The cocktail hour, on the terrace
A jazz duo or an acoustic duo at a level guests talk across, and the one thing a Vail terrace asks of a duo is the cold: fingers and strings both slow down once the sun is behind the ridge, so the hour is timed to the light and the move indoors is agreed before the day. What plays in the hour is on the cocktail hour page.
The floor, in the ballroom
A jazz band that grew through the day, a covers band like Tejas Singh that starts as a duo and finishes as a quartet, or the DJ from the end of dinner to the last song. Indoors the cap is the venue’s own, and the hotel ballrooms take a quartet without strain.

Every size arrives with sound, load-in and strike, a certificate of insurance and a W-9. The whole song list is on the repertoire page.
