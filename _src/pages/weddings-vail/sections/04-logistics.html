Logistics

Light, cold, a noise cap and the drive.

An outdoor ceremony in Vail is planned around the light. The sun goes behind the ridge early, the colour on the mountain goes with it, and a ceremony timed for that light has a fixed start the music is built around. The guitarist is set up and sound-checked well before it, on a covered, level and dry spot with power run to it, because a summer afternoon can turn to rain and a fall afternoon to snow in the time it takes to seat the guests.

The village measures sound at the property line. Vail’s outdoor limit in the resort villages was reported at 65 dBA, measured ten feet from the permitted area, and a dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor by design, so those two numbers are reconciled by where the floor is: indoors, or on a terrace the venue has already permitted. A ceremony guitarist and a cocktail hour duo sit inside the cap on their own. We confirm the current figure with the venue before the day, and every mountain town’s number, cited and dated, is on the mountain corridor page.

The drive is one line on the quote, the run up I-70 and back over Vail Pass, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line, because Vail sits inside the corridor we price as a single travel line rather than as a far resort. On a winter date the musicians travel early and the venue knows it. The altitude reaches the gear as well as the guests: strings drift between an afternoon ceremony and an evening set and get retuned, and a singer or a horn player paces a set differently in thin air, which is why the sound check is longer here than in the city.
