The mountain

Light, cold, a noise cap and thin air.

An outdoor ceremony in Vail is planned around the light. The sun goes behind the ridge early, the color on the mountain goes with it, and a ceremony timed for that light has a fixed start the music is built around. The guitarist is set up and sound-checked well before it, on a covered, level and dry spot with power run to it, because a summer afternoon can turn to rain and a fall afternoon to snow in the time it takes to seat the guests.

The village measures outdoor sound at the edge of the permitted area, so the dance floor goes indoors or onto a terrace the venue has already permitted, and a ceremony guitarist and a cocktail hour duo sit inside the cap on their own. Every mountain town’s number, cited and dated, is on the mountain corridor page.

The altitude reaches the gear as well as the guests: strings drift between an afternoon ceremony and an evening set and get retuned, and anyone singing or playing a horn paces a set differently in thin air, which is why the sound check is longer here than in the city.
