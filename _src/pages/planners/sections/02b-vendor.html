Vendor file

What your venue is going to ask for.

All five, without a phone call. Forward this section if that is faster.

Certificate of insurance
On request, and without one: a COI naming your venue as additional insured arrives in the same email as the contract, along with a W-9 and one itemised invoice. If the venue needs specific wording or a specific additional insured, send it and it comes back correct the first time.
General liability
{{gl_per_occurrence}} per occurrence, {{gl_aggregate}} aggregate. That is the pair most Colorado venues write into their vendor requirements, and it is what the certificate will show.
A written contract
Every booking. It names the date, the venue, the configuration, the hours, the total and the payment schedule. Retainer to hold, balance 14 days out, ACH or check at no fee and card with the processing fee added. Cancellation and refund terms are written into it rather than referred to, so your client is not signing a document that points at a policy nobody has read.
One point of contact
A named person, given to you weeks before load-in and reachable from load-in to load-out. They are a player rather than a coordinator, so they are in the building all night. You are never handed to a dispatcher on the day. How that works, and how substitutions are handled
Production included
Sound and lighting sized to the room, with an engineer, at every configuration. Load-in, setup, strike and load-out. There is no separate production line on the quote and no third-party AV to coordinate unless your venue mandates its own. Stage footprints, power draw and load-in times

Every rate is published on the pricing page, so the figure you forward to your client is the figure they sign.
