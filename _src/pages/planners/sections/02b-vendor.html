Vendor file

What your venue may ask for.

All five are below, so there's no phone call to make

Certificate of insurance
Whether you ask for it or not, a COI naming your venue as additional insured arrives in the same email as the contract, along with a W-9 and one itemized invoice. If the venue needs specific wording or a specific additional insured, send it and it comes back correct the first time.

General liability
Carried at the limits most Colorado venues write into their vendor requirements. Send us the limits yours asks for and we'll confirm them against the policy before you commit to a date; the certificate itself shows the numbers.

A written contract
Every booking gets one. It names the date, the venue, the configuration, the hours, the total and the payment schedule. A deposit holds the date, the balance falls due 14 days out, and payment is ACH, check or card through Stripe, all at no fee.

It's rare that we may choose to approved corporate accounts which run on net-30 terms instead. These will be invoiced after the event. Cancellation and refund terms are written into the contract rather than referred to, so your client isn't signing a document that points at a policy nobody has read.

Single point of contact
For each event, you'll have a single point of contact who is reachable from load-in to load-out. In many cases they'll be in the building all night, and you'll have their cell.

Small-to-medium sized sound included
We will always size our sound system to your specs and to the venue, at every configuration. This will be itemized in the invoice. Load-in, setup, strike and load-out are enumerated. Stage lighting and a dedicated engineer are optional add-ons, quoted as their own line when you want them.
