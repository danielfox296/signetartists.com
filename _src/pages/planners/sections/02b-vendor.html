Vendor file

What your venue is going to ask for.

All five, without a phone call. Forward this section if that is faster.

Certificate of insurance
On request, and without one: a COI naming your venue as additional insured arrives in the same email as the contract, along with a W-9 and one itemised invoice. If the venue needs specific wording or a specific additional insured, send it and it comes back correct the first time.
General liability
{{gl_per_occurrence}} per occurrence, {{gl_aggregate}} aggregate. That is the pair most Colorado venues write into their vendor requirements, and it is what the certificate will show.
A written contract
Every booking. It names the date, the venue, the configuration, the hours, the total and the payment schedule. A 50% deposit holds the date, the balance falls due 14 days out, and payment is ACH, check or card through Stripe, all at no fee. Approved corporate accounts run on net-30 instead, invoiced after the event. Cancellation and refund terms are written into it rather than referred to, so your client is not signing a document that points at a policy nobody has read.
One point of contact
A named person, given to you in writing before the event and reachable from load-in to load-out. They are in the building all night, and the number you have is the one that answers. How that works, and how substitutions are handled
Sound included
Sound sized to the room, at every configuration, inside the fee rather than a separate line. Load-in, setup, strike and load-out are included. Stage lighting and a dedicated engineer are optional add-ons, quoted as their own line when you want them. Stage footprints, power draw and load-in times
