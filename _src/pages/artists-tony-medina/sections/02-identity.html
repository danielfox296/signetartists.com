Some nights want a voice that sounds like it has been somewhere. Tony Medina sings with grit and gravel in it and plays guitar with a funk player’s right hand, which is why a set of blues and classic rock in his hands tends to catch a crowd that thought it had turned up for background music.

The book is the blues and most of what the blues turned into. Freddie King and B.B. King at one end, The Cars, Prince and the rock records of the seventies and nineties at the other, and his own songs threaded through. Guests recognise nearly all of it without being told what it is, which is the whole trick of a covers set at a private event.

It scales from one player to four. Solo it is a voice and an acoustic guitar, warm enough to sit under conversation through welcome drinks and dinner. Add percussion, then bass and drums, and at the four piece it is a band with a floor in front of it.

He is a working Denver musician with his own catalogue, his own site and his own channel, so none of this has to be taken on trust. The footage on this page is his, and there is a great deal more of it where that came from.
