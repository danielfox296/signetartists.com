Some nights want a voice that sounds like it’s been somewhere. Tony Medina sings with grit and gravel in it and plays guitar with a funk guitarist’s right hand, which is why a set of blues and classic rock in his hands tends to catch a crowd that thought it had turned up for background music.

The book is the blues and most of what the blues turned into. Freddie King and B.B. King sit at one end, The Cars, Prince and the rock records of the seventies and nineties at the other, and his own songs are threaded through. Guests recognise nearly all of it without being told what it is.

It scales from one musician to four. Solo, it’s a voice and an acoustic guitar, warm enough to sit under conversation through welcome drinks and dinner. Add percussion, then bass and drums, and at the four piece it’s a band with a floor in front of it.

He’s a working Denver musician with his own catalogue, his own site and his own channel. The footage on this page is his, and there’s a lot more of it on his channel.
