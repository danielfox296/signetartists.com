Who plays it

Four common configurations for the hour after the program.

Each one runs through the same sound system as the dinner music, on the same contract, so the dance floor opens the moment the program closes.

The DJ, with a live musician over the set
An open-format DJ reads a dance floor of people from six cities, with a saxophonist or a bassist playing over the set. A solid DJ gets people moving and is the most common single booking of the corporate season, and the live musician is the difference between a playlist and a crowd watching something happen.
Live band karaoke
A real band plays the 80s and 90s hits on its own and hands the mic to anyone who wants a song, with the words on a screen and a host on the floor. Nobody has to sing for it to work. For a conference crowd that spent the day in a ballroom, it’s the after-party they describe to the colleagues who skipped it.
The soul and R&B band
Stevie Wonder, Aretha Franklin, Al Green and the Motown songbook, sung out front with a horn on the choruses. It’s the band for a ballroom floor in a gala’s second half, or for a holiday party once the awards are done.
The yacht rock band
Doobie Brothers, Steely Dan, Hall and Oates and Toto in three-part harmony. It’s for the crowd that wants to sing every chorus rather than dance every song, and for a summer after-party on a terrace.

A DJ can also carry the late set after the band wraps, through the same system and on the same contract. The DJ page covers how that handoff works.
