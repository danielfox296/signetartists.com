The configurations above show what a corporate booking looks like. Each occasion also carries a full page of its own: the dates, the venues that kind of event usually books, the way the music works around the program, and the questions that come with it.

Office holiday parties
The page for the whole season. Every Thursday, Friday and Saturday of November and December is on it, with the peak-priced dates marked, and so is the January party, which Denver venues now run as part of the same season.
Galas and award nights
An evening built around a program, with remarks, an auction, a paddle raise, a video and a walk to the stage. That page sets out what the music does on either side of each cue, from the reception act to the floor after the last award.
Client dinners and member events
Dinners where the conversation is the event: client dinners, member and association evenings, private clubs, firms and showrooms. The jazz duo is the act that page is built around, playing low enough that the table forgets it is there and well enough that they remember on the way out.
Corporate retreats
That page covers retreats and incentive events in the mountain corridor, Vail through Aspen, and the Colorado evening a group that flew in for it expects. A resort date adds the drive to the quote, and a night’s lodging once the venue is past the passes, each as its own line.
After-parties
The hour after a conference dinner, a gala program or the holiday party’s speeches. That page covers a DJ with a live musician over the set, a band that lets the crowd sing, and a soul band on the ballroom floor, all on the sound system that’s already up.
