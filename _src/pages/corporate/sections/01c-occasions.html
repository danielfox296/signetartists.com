The occasions

Five corporate occasions, each with its own page.

The shapes above show what a corporate booking looks like. Each occasion also carries a full page of its own: the dates, the venues that market books, the way the music works around the program, and the questions that come with it.

Office holiday parties
The season page, with every Thursday, Friday and Saturday of November and December dated and the peak premiums tagged, plus the January party, which Denver venues now run as part of the same season.
Galas and award nights
The program-driven evening: remarks, an auction, a paddle raise, a video, a walk to the stage. That page sets out what the music does on either side of each cue, from the reception act to the floor after the last award.
Client dinners and member events
Dinners where the conversation is the event: client dinners, member and association evenings, private clubs, firms and showrooms. The jazz duo leads that page, playing low enough that the table forgets it is there and well enough that they remember on the way out.
Corporate retreats
Retreats and incentive events in the mountain corridor, Vail through Aspen, and the Colorado evening a group that flew in for it expects. A resort date carries the drive and, past the passes, a night’s lodging, both itemised.
After-parties
The hour after a conference dinner, a gala program or the holiday party’s speeches. A DJ with a live player over the set, a band that lets the crowd sing, or a soul band on the ballroom floor, on the sound system that is already up.

What each size costs, and where those figures sit in the wider Colorado market, is set out in the cost guide. Ideas for the whole evening, music and otherwise, are in corporate event entertainment ideas in Denver.
