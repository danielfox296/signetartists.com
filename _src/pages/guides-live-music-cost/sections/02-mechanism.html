What the figure is made of.

Two parts, with most acts in this market. A call-out covers the evening committed, whatever its length: the drive, load-in, setup, sound check, strike and production. A rate for performance time covers the music. The split is why a one-hour cocktail set is never a quarter of a four-hour reception, and why an act that quotes purely by the hour is either carrying a very high hourly figure or leaving something out of it.

Overtime is the next thing to read. This market charges {{market:denver_overtime}} for time beyond the contracted set, which is worth knowing before eleven o'clock rather than after. Ask whether the rate for hour five is the rate hour four was. Ours is.

Then the lines that sit outside the fee. Travel beyond the metro. A night's lodging when the drive home crosses the passes after a late strike. A holiday weekend, which this market prices above a standard date. Each of those belongs on its own line, because folding them in makes a quote look cheaper than the evening is.

The corporate version of this, worked through the shapes a Denver company actually books, is in what live music costs for a corporate event.
