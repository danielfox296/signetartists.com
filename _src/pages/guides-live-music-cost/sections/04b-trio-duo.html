Trio and duo

What a jazz trio costs, and what an acoustic duo costs.

These are the two sizes most Colorado evenings actually book, and they are the two the published guides skip over fastest. The figures below are the market's for each, at the length the market quotes them in.

The jazz trio
A trio across a four-hour evening runs {{market:us_trio}} in this market. For a dinner rather than a full evening, a jazz trio or acoustic duo runs {{market:us_dinner_band}}, and the spread inside that is how long the music plays and how much production travels with it. Denver has no trio figure of its own in print: the Denver band range of {{market:denver_band_range}} begins at the trio and ends at a full band with horns, so the trio sits at the bottom of that range rather than in the middle of it.
The acoustic duo
An acoustic duo across a four-hour evening runs {{market:us_duo}}, the smallest full-evening figure published anywhere in this market. Two players cover a dinner or a cocktail hour without a stage, and for a short booking the Denver market puts a duo or trio at a ceremony or cocktail hour at {{market:denver_strings}}. A duo evening and a trio evening are one player apart on the stand, and the market prices that one player as the difference between {{market:us_duo}} and {{market:us_trio}}.
What moves either one
How many players are on the stand, how many hours they play, and what the date is. Not which act is standing there. A December Saturday in Denver carries {{market:denver_holiday_surcharge}} across this market, and a resort date carries the drive and a night's lodging at the far end of it.

What is a jazz trio?

Three players, and the instrument list is the part people are usually asking about. The traditional combos are piano, bass and drums, or organ, guitar and drums. The one that works at a private event is guitar, upright bass and drums played with brushes, because brushes are what let a drum kit sit under a conversation instead of over it. A quartet is the same three with a horn or a voice added.

At an event the trio does a specific job. It holds 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a level guests talk across without leaning in, and it covers a larger floor and a longer guest list than a duo at that same level. It takes 12 by 8 feet, one dedicated 20-amp circuit and about an hour to load in, so it fits a private dining venue as readily as a ballroom. What it plays is standards, the one set of songs nobody has to be told about.

The instrumentation, the trio and duo configurations and a sample set are on the jazz duo and trio page. The singer-songwriter version of the same footprint is the acoustic duo, and the hour these two are most often booked for is the wedding cocktail hour.
