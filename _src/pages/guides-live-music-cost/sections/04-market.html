Market context

Why the figures spread this far.

Live music here prices in tiers, and each one buys a different thing. Reading a number without knowing which tier it came from is how two quotes end up looking like the same purchase.

The marketplace tier
One booking marketplace averages {{market:denver_marketplace_avg}} for a Denver party band, across listings running {{market:denver_marketplace_range}}. The figure buys the act. What else sits inside it, sound, insurance, travel, production, varies listing by listing, so two quotes at the same number can cover two different evenings.
The working middle
The Denver market treats the four to six piece band as its middle, at {{market:denver_midtier_band}}, and Colorado couples budget {{market:co_band_budget}} for a band across the whole state. Out in the resort corridor, a five to six piece runs {{market:mountain_west_band}} in Mountain West markets. Nationally most couples land {{market:us_band_range}}, with the average sitting around {{market:us_band_average}}. This is the band of figures most private and corporate bookings are actually decided in.
The production tier
A seven to nine piece show band runs {{market:us_showband}}, and full production adds show direction, lighting, staffing and a program designed around the act. For a gala carrying a production budget, it is the right purchase and the figure is not the interesting part of it.

Where a Signet night lands depends on the night. A solo player through a dinner for twelve and a quartet filling a floor until midnight are different jobs, and so are the same quartet in a Denver ballroom and in Aspen in February. Send the date and the shape of the evening, and the number for it comes back.
