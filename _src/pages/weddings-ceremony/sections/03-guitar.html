The instrument

Acoustic guitar, or Spanish guitar.

Both are one player and one instrument, and they sound less alike than couples expect. Which one suits a ceremony has more to do with the setting than with the songs.

Acoustic guitar. Steel strings, bright and clear, and the sound most people mean when they say guitar. It handles the songs couples actually ask for: the one that was playing when they met, a hymn, a film theme, a pop song arranged so it works without a voice. It is also the most portable booking on this roster, setting up in 6 by 4 feet and playing a small indoor ceremony without amplification at all. Details of the format are on the solo guitarist page.

Spanish guitar. Nylon strings, warmer and rounder, with less attack on each note and more body underneath it. It suits a garden, a courtyard, stone, and any ceremony where the couple wants something with an accent to it. The repertoire moves between Spanish pieces and whatever the couple asks for, played on nylon. That booking is Dirty Flamenco, whose craft is real flamenco and whose ceremony work is the quiet end of it.

A ceremony can also be played by more than one person. A duo puts upright bass under the guitar, which gives a processional weight in a large space, and a trio adds drums played with brushes for a ceremony in a hall rather than a garden. Both are on the jazz duo and trio page, and either one walks straight from the ceremony into the cocktail hour on the same contract.
