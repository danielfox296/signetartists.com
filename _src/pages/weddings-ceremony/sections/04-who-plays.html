Who plays it

A solo musician, a small ensemble, or the DJ.

Three answers are in common use in Colorado, and all three are legitimate. What separates them is how each one handles a day that doesn't run to the second.

A solo musician
A guitarist, a harpist, a violinist or a cellist. The most portable of the four is the guitarist, and the only one that plays outdoors on grass without a deck under it. It's one musician, one van, and a setup that’s out of the way before the ceremony starts. Where the couple wants a voice at the ceremony, René Moffatt sings his own songs and covers to an acoustic guitar and stays for the cocktail hour.
A small ensemble
A string duo or trio, or a guitar and bass duo. Two or three musicians give a processional more weight in a large or a hard-surfaced space, and the same ensemble carries the cocktail hour afterward, which is what most Colorado weddings end up booking.
The DJ
Most DJs cover a ceremony, and it’s a sensible choice when the ceremony and the reception are in the same building, with a second speaker at the ceremony site and a microphone for the officiant. Where a wedding wants both, our DJ takes the reception and live musicians take the ceremony, on one contract.
