Who plays it

A player, an ensemble, or the DJ.

Three answers are in common use in Colorado and all three are legitimate. What separates them is what happens when the day does not run to the second, which is every day.

A solo player
A guitarist, a harpist, a violinist or a cellist. The most portable of the four is the guitarist, and the only one that plays outdoors on grass without a deck under it. One player, one van, and a setup nobody notices before the ceremony starts.
A small ensemble
A string duo or trio, or a guitar and bass duo. Two or three players give a processional more weight in a large or a hard-surfaced space, and the same ensemble carries the cocktail hour afterwards, which is the shape most Colorado weddings end up booking.
The DJ
Most DJs will cover a ceremony, and it is a sensible choice when the ceremony and the reception happen in the same building. What the DJ brings is a second speaker at the ceremony site and a microphone for the officiant. What a recorded track cannot do is stretch to fit a longer aisle or land its last chord on the kiss. Where a wedding wants both, our DJ takes the reception and live players take the ceremony, on one contract.

The microphone question, which nobody asks until the day. A ceremony has speech in it, and outdoors an unamplified officiant is inaudible past about the fourth row. A dedicated announcement microphone rides on its own channel at every size here, live from the moment sound check finishes, so the officiant, the readings and the vows go through the same small system the guitar is going through. Nobody has to hand a singer's microphone around.
