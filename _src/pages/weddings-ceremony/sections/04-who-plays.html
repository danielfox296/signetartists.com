Who plays it

A solo musician, a small ensemble, or the DJ.

Three answers are in common use in Colorado, and all three are legitimate. What separates them is how each one handles a day that doesn't run to the second.

A solo musician
A guitarist, a harpist, a violinist or a cellist. The most portable of the four is the guitarist, and the only one that plays outdoors on grass without a deck under it. One musician, one van, and a setup nobody notices before the ceremony starts.
A small ensemble
A string duo or trio, or a guitar and bass duo. Two or three musicians give a processional more weight in a large or a hard-surfaced space, and the same ensemble carries the cocktail hour afterwards, which is what most Colorado weddings end up booking.
The DJ
Most DJs will cover a ceremony, and it's a sensible choice when the ceremony and the reception happen in the same building: a second speaker at the ceremony site and a microphone for the officiant. A recorded track can't stretch to fit a longer aisle or land its last chord on the kiss, though, and that's the part live musicians are there for. Where a wedding wants both, our DJ takes the reception and live musicians take the ceremony, on one contract.

The officiant's microphone. A ceremony has speech in it, and outdoors an unamplified officiant is inaudible past about the fourth row. An announcement microphone comes with every size here, so the officiant, the readings and the vows carry through the same small system as the guitar.
