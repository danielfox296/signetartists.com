9,600 feet

What the altitude changes.

Breath. Singers and horns tire faster in thin, dry air, so sets hold to 45 to 60 minutes with real breaks between them, and music continues through the breaks at every size. Pacing is the difference between a band that finishes strong and one that fades early.

Tuning. The temperature swing between sound check and the last set runs wide up here, and instruments drift with it. Retunes between sets are part of the plan, and the sound check runs longer than the same band would take in Denver.

Timelines. Load-in usually runs 45 to 90 minutes depending on the size of the band. At 9,600 feet we take the generous end of that window and fix the dock or door time with the venue in advance, because carrying gear is slower work at this altitude.

Power. The band needs one or two dedicated 20-amp circuits depending on its size, and dedicated means not shared with catering warmers, which is the most common load-in fault we plan against. Footprints and power are set out by size, and the altitude of every mountain town is cited on the mountain corridor page.
