Dock time, elevator windows, altitude.

The core is pedestrian. Cars stop at the parking structures in Vail Village and Lionshead, so hotels receive vendors at loading docks on scheduled windows and move gear by freight elevator. We settle the dock time, the elevator window and the route to the floor with the venue in writing before the day, along with where cases wait once the evening starts.

Power is a one-line question. One or two dedicated 20-amp circuits depending on the size of the band, dedicated being the word that matters. Footprints, circuit counts and input lists are published by band size, so the venue gets a link rather than a relay of questions.

8,150 feet is a working fact. Singers and horn players pace differently in thin, dry air, and the temperature swing between sound check and the last set means instruments drift and get retuned. We write a longer sound check into the run of show, and the night holds its shape.

The cap is knowable. Vail's outdoor limit in the resort villages was reported at 65 dBA, measured ten feet from the permitted area, and the town was reviewing its rules as outdoor programming grew, so we confirm the current figure with the venue. A dinner set already runs 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which makes the cap a late-set question rather than a dinner one. Every figure, cited and dated, is on the mountain corridor page.
