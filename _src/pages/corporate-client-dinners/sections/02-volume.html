The volume is the product

Most of what goes wrong with live music at a dinner is a volume decision. A band playing at party level makes guests shout across a table they were invited to talk at, and the host spends the night apologising for it. So the dinner figure is the one we publish and hold to: 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, measured where your guests actually sit.

A duo is built for that number. Upright bass and guitar playing standards leave the middle of the sound open, so conversation sits on top of the music rather than under it, and two players hold a dinner for hours without drifting louder. The dinner reads as expensive and unhurried, which is the whole job. Sets run 45 to 60 minutes with 15 to 20 between them, and audio continues through every break, so the evening never goes silent.

Toasts and remarks are covered at every size: a dedicated announcement microphone is live on its own channel from the end of sound check, and the music stops while your speaker is still steps away from it.
