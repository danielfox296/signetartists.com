Outdoors

What a terrace hears.

A Colorado cocktail hour is outdoors more often than not, and open air behaves nothing like a ballroom. There are no walls to hand the sound back, so guests hear what the players actually put out and nothing else. That changes what each format does with the hour.

From a guitar. Close, detailed and warm within about thirty feet, and thinning fast beyond it. A guitar has no low end to carry it across a lawn, so an outdoor hour is amplified where the same booking indoors might not be, and the amplification is there to hold the same conversation level across a wider spread rather than to make anything louder. Guests near the terrace rail hear the strings. Guests at the far end hear an evening with music in it.

From a jazz trio. Fuller and steadier across distance, because the upright bass gives the sound a floor and low frequencies travel further outdoors than anything else does. Drums played with brushes keep the pulse without a single hard attack in the set, which is what stops an outdoor trio reading as a concert. On a big terrace or a lawn spread wide, the trio is the size that reaches the far edge of the crowd at the same level the near edge is hearing.

Outdoors adds three requirements to any size: a covered, level and dry performance area, power run to it, and a move-indoors call written before the day. Colorado evenings drop fast after sunset, and instruments and electronics both stop behaving in the cold. The rest of the outdoor working detail is on the technical page.
