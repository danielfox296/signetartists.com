The builds

Sung, instrumental, or both at once.

Voice and guitar
The sung version. Cocktail hours, welcome drinks and patios take it best: familiar songs, sung, at talking level, with enough presence to mark the occasion without running it.
Guitar and upright bass
The instrumental version. Dinner takes it best: the melodies stay familiar, the lyrics stop competing with conversation, and the arrangement leaves space in the middle of the sound where the table talk sits.
The trio: voice, guitar and upright bass
Both builds on one night. The sung set carries cocktails, the instrumental read carries dinner, and the third player keeps the sound full at the size a bigger crowd asks for. {{rate_range:trio}} for a Denver evening, {{rate_1h:Trio}} for a single hour, on the same terms as the duo.

Every build runs dinner and cocktail sets at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, and neither a two- nor a three-piece of this kind can drift to dance floor level by accident. Outdoors it plays on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it; Colorado patio evenings cool fast, and the move-indoors call is written before the day.

Footprints and power by size are on the technical page. When the same night should grow into a dance floor, Tejas Singh ladders the duo shape up to a quartet on one contract.
