Configurations

Three configurations of the same booking.

Voice and guitar
The sung version. Cocktail hours, welcome drinks and patios are where it fits best: familiar songs sung at a talking level, with enough presence to feel like live music without taking over the conversation.
Guitar and upright bass
The instrumental version. Dinner is where it fits best: the melodies stay familiar, and without lyrics the music sits under the table talk instead of competing with it.
The trio: voice, guitar and upright bass
Both configurations on one night, with a sung set and an instrumental set, and a third musician to keep the sound full for a bigger crowd. It books for a single hour or a whole evening, just like the duo.

Every configuration plays dinner and cocktail sets at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, and a two- or three-piece of this kind won’t drift up to dance floor volume. Outdoors it plays on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it. Colorado patio evenings cool fast, so we agree a move-indoors plan with you ahead of time.

Footprints and power by size are on the technical page. When the same night should grow into a dance floor, the duo can grow to a quartet on one contract. One voice and a guitar on its own is the singer and guitarist.
