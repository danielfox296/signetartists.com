The configurations

Music that gets out of the way of the speeches.

The speeches are the point of a retirement party, so the music is built around them: silent for the remarks, back the moment the glass goes down, and sized to the place.

The jazz duo, or one guitar
Upright bass and guitar in 8 by 6 feet, or one jazz guitarist in 6 by 4, playing the standards at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table. The standards are the repertoire that reaches a table of colleagues aged twenty-five to seventy at once, and the jazz duo and trio page covers the size.
A singer with a guitar
One voice and a guitar playing the songs the office knows and the one the retiree has always asked for, learned for the night if it isn’t already in the book. Tejas Singh plays it, and the first learned song is included in every booking.
Live band karaoke for the department
The band plays the hits from the 80s and 90s on its own and hands the microphone to the colleagues who’ve been threatening to sing for a decade, with the words on a screen and a host keeping the sign-up list. The retiring colleague’s one karaoke song is a request we take the week before. It needs 14 by 10 feet and two dedicated circuits, and the live band karaoke page covers how a guest gets to the microphone.
The party band
A trio or a quartet for the send-off that ends on a floor. Tony Medina plays it up to a four piece, the same band plays the dinner first at conversation volume, and the DJ can take the late end through the same sound system.

What live music costs in this market is on the pricing page, and the cost guide sets out the Colorado figures for each kind of night.
