The stage

Published stage-rental guidance for a seven to ten-piece band runs to 24 by 24 feet. That figure is written to be safe for any band anyone might book, and it is more than four times what the quartet, the largest band we sell, asks for. Renting to it costs you a few hundred dollars and a chunk of floor your guests wanted.

{{stage_plot}}

So there are two numbers below. The first is what we ask for, where the band is comfortable and the sightlines work. The second is the smallest we will play on. Between them nothing breaks; the band just gets tighter, and tightness is audible before it is visible.

{{stage_table}}

Depth before width if you have to choose. A band squeezed narrow still works; a band with no depth puts the drums in the singer’s back.

Risers, floors and ceilings

We do not require a stage
A band plays fine on a floor, and most of our Denver bookings are on one. A riser is a sightline decision for rooms over about 150 guests, not a technical requirement.
We do not supply one either
If you want a riser, it comes from the venue or your production company. Tell us the height and we will plan the load-in around it; 12 to 24 inches is normal and anything over 24 needs a rail behind the drums.
Ceiling height
Ten feet clear above the stage surface. Lighting stands go to nine, and a low ceiling over a riser is the one constraint that cannot be solved on the day.
Surface
Level and solid. Deck that flexes under a drummer will be heard through every microphone on the stage. Carpet is fine; grass and gravel need a deck under the band.
