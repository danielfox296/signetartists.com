Mountain corridor

The rules above 7,000 feet.

Noise caps, curfews, altitude and travel for a band playing Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge, Aspen, Telluride or Steamboat. Every figure below is cited and dated at the foot of the page, and the towns we could not confirm are named as unconfirmed instead of guessed at.

Ninety decibels on the floor. Fifty at the fence.

Most planning conversations about volume happen at the town level. Colorado also caps noise statewide, and where the state number is the lower of the two, that is the number you have to meet. The Noise Abatement Act sets maximum levels measured 25 feet or more from the property line, and exceeding them is prima facie evidence of a public nuisance.

Zone7am to 7pm7pm to 7am
Residential55 dB(A)50 dB(A)
Commercial60 dB(A)55 dB(A)
Light industrial70 dB(A)65 dB(A)
Industrial80 dB(A)75 dB(A)

Two riders matter for an evening event. Between 7am and 7pm the limit rises by 10 dB(A) for up to fifteen minutes in any one hour, which is useful for a ceremony and useless for a dance floor. And after 7pm, a residential zone sits at 50 dB(A) at 25 feet, which is below conversation. A private estate or ranch reception in a residential zone is constrained by that number whatever the band does.

A town permit may not cover you

On 8 September 2025 the Colorado Supreme Court held that a municipality cannot authorise a private for-profit venue to exceed the state caps. The 1987 amendment that exempts cultural and entertainment events applies to property used by a government body or a nonprofit, and the court read that narrowly: Salida could not issue amplified-sound permits for concerts at a commercial bar.

For a planner this changes one habit. A sound permit from the town, held by a commercial venue, is no longer the end of the question. Ask the venue who holds the property and under what exemption, and get the answer before the contract rather than after a complaint.

Travel tiers

Front Range
Boulder, Fort Collins, and Colorado Springs including the Broadmoor. A day trip, so these price from the Denver column with a $300 travel line rather than from the resort column. The Broadmoor is a resort property on Front Range travel, which makes it one of the better value venues in the state for live music.
I-70 corridor
Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park. $600 travel. Same-day return is realistic for an early call and unrealistic for a late one, so a late curfew usually brings lodging with it.
Far resorts
Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat Springs and Crested Butte. $600 travel plus lodging at cost. These are two-day commitments for the band whatever the run of show says, which is what the resort column is pricing.

Holiday and peak weekends add 25%, and New Year's Eve 50%, both published on the rate card rather than quoted late. Every act on the roster prices both columns on its own page.

Town by town

Checked 7 August 2026. Municipal codes change, and several of these towns were actively revising their noise rules when we looked. Treat this as the starting question to put to your venue, not as the answer.

Aspen
The strictest and the clearest. Outdoor amplified sound at a private event is capped at 80 dBA at the perimeter, between 9am and 8pm. A public event with a Special Event Permit gets 100 dBA until 9pm. Neighbours within 250 feet get seven days’ notice, and a property with a noise variance may hold two events a week. An 8pm outdoor cutoff reshapes a wedding timeline, so plan the loud hour indoors or plan it earlier.
Telluride
Amplified sound, indoors and out, runs 7am to 11pm on Fridays, Saturdays and defined holidays, provided it is not plainly audible beyond the parameters the permit sets. The permit application asks for expected decibel levels at stated distances from each source, which is a question we can answer for you in writing.
Breckenridge
Reported limits are 55 dB(A) from 7:01am to 10:59pm and 50 dB(A) outside those hours in residential zones, and 70 and 65 in commercial. The town manager can permit above them at their discretion. Verify with the venue: we could not reach the code text directly.
Steamboat Springs
Reported at 60 dB(A) from 7am to 7pm and 55 dB(A) from 7pm to 7am. Amplified sound in a public place needs permission from the city manager.
Vail
Reported at 65 dB(A) outdoors in the resort villages, measured ten feet from the permitted area, with the town manager able to allow amplified sound for up to four consecutive days. Vail was reviewing these rules as outdoor programming grew, so confirm the current figure with the venue.
Beaver Creek, Winter Park, Snowmass Village, Crested Butte, Mountain Village
Not confirmed. We would rather say so than publish a number your venue then contradicts. Send us the venue and we will get the answer from the town before you have to.

One pattern holds across all of them. The published cap is a property-line or perimeter figure, and a dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor. Those two numbers are not comparable, and the distance between the band and the property line is what reconciles them. That distance is a site question, which is why we want the venue name early.

Altitude

Denver sits at 5,280 feet and the corridor runs well above it. Breckenridge is the highest town on our travel card at roughly 9,600 feet, Mountain Village above Telluride is about 9,545, Winter Park about 9,052, Telluride 8,750, Crested Butte 8,909, Vail 8,150, Beaver Creek near 8,100, Snowmass Village 8,104, Aspen 7,908 and Steamboat 6,732.

Two consequences reach the run of show. Singers and horn players tire faster in thin dry air, so a band that plays four hours at 9,600 feet paces the night differently from the same band in Denver. And the temperature swing between a five o’clock sound check and an eleven o’clock last set is wider up there than it is on the Front Range, which means more retuning across the evening.

Neither is a problem. Both are reasons to build a longer sound check into the document and to put water on the stage, and both are why a band that has worked these rooms before behaves differently at 8pm from one that has not.

Travel and lodging, and why they are separate lines

Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park add $600. Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat and Crested Butte add $600 plus lodging at cost. Both figures sit on the rate card and on the contract as their own lines, so the performance fee your client sees is the same number a Denver client sees.

Lodging is at cost rather than marked up, and it appears on the far-resort tier for one reason: I-70 and the passes. A drive that takes two hours in September takes considerably longer on a February Sunday, and a band that has driven six hours to Telluride is not driving home at one in the morning. Where the arithmetic favours it, tell us and we will price the night without lodging and drive it, though we will say plainly when we think that is the wrong call.

Winter dates carry one more line worth knowing about early: Independence Pass closes, so Aspen from Denver runs the long way round through Glenwood Springs.

Four questions for a mountain venue

Everything above turns into these. Asked at contract, they are administrative. Asked in the week of the event, two of them can change the shape of the night.

  • What is the cap, as a number, and is it measured at the property line or at the perimeter of the event?
  • What time does outdoor amplified sound have to stop, and does it differ by day of the week?
  • Who holds the sound permit, and is the property government, nonprofit or commercial?
  • How far is the stage from the nearest property line?

Send us the venue name and we will ask all four ourselves. The rest of it is on what we need from your venue.
