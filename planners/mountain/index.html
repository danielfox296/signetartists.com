Mountain venues

Live music at Colorado's mountain venues.

Noise caps, curfews, altitude and travel for a band playing Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge, Aspen, Telluride or Steamboat. Every number below is cited and dated at the foot of the page, and the towns we couldn't confirm are named as unconfirmed.

Colorado's statewide noise limits

Most planning conversations about volume happen at the town level. Colorado also caps noise statewide, and where the state number is the lower of the two, that's the number you have to meet. The Noise Abatement Act sets maximum levels measured 25 feet or more from the property line, and exceeding them is prima facie evidence of a public nuisance.

Zone7am to 7pm7pm to 7am
Residential55 dB(A)50 dB(A)
Commercial60 dB(A)55 dB(A)
Light industrial70 dB(A)65 dB(A)
Industrial80 dB(A)75 dB(A)

Two details in the Act matter for an evening event. Between 7am and 7pm the limit rises by 10 dB(A) for up to fifteen minutes in any one hour, which is enough for a ceremony but not for dancing. And after 7pm, a residential zone sits at 50 dB(A) at 25 feet, which is below conversation. A private estate or ranch reception in a residential zone is constrained by that number whatever the band does.

A town permit may not cover you

On 8 September 2025 the Colorado Supreme Court held that a municipality cannot authorise a private for-profit venue to exceed the state caps. The 1987 amendment that exempts cultural and entertainment events applies to property used by a government body or a nonprofit, and the court read that narrowly: Salida could not issue amplified-sound permits for concerts at a commercial bar.

For a planner this changes one habit. A sound permit from the town, held by a commercial venue, is no longer the end of the question. Ask the venue who holds the property and under what exemption, and get the answer before the contract.

What a mountain date costs

A resort date costs more than a metro date everywhere in this market. The gap is travel, the extra day the calendar loses, and lodging when the night runs late enough that driving I-70 home afterwards isn't sensible. All three are itemised on our quote.

Travel tiers

Front Range
Boulder, Fort Collins, and Colorado Springs including the Broadmoor. A day trip, so these carry a short travel line and no lodging. The Broadmoor is a resort property on the Front Range travel tier, which makes it one of the better value venues in the state for live music.
I-70 corridor
Vail, Beaver Creek, Breckenridge and Winter Park. The corridor adds travel. Same-day return works for an early finish, and a late curfew usually brings a night's lodging with it.
Far resorts
Aspen, Telluride, Steamboat Springs and Crested Butte. These add travel, plus a night's lodging billed at what it cost. These are two-day commitments for the band whatever the run of show says.

Lodging is billed at cost, on the quote as its own line.

A holiday weekend prices above a standard date across this market. What live music costs in Colorado, sourced figure by figure, is on the pricing page and in the cost guide. The town matters as much as the date up here, so send both.

Town by town

Checked 7 August 2026. Municipal codes change, and several of these towns were actively revising their noise rules when we looked. Treat this as the starting question to put to your venue.

Aspen
The strictest and the clearest. Outdoor amplified sound at a private event is capped at 80 dBA at the perimeter, between 9am and 8pm. A public event with a Special Event Permit gets 100 dBA until 9pm. Neighbours within 250 feet get seven days’ notice, and a property with a noise variance may hold two events a week. An 8pm outdoor cutoff reshapes a wedding timeline, so plan the loud hour indoors or plan it earlier.
Telluride
Amplified sound, indoors and out, runs 7am to 11pm on Fridays, Saturdays and defined holidays, provided it is not plainly audible beyond the parameters the permit sets. The permit application asks for expected decibel levels at stated distances from each source, which is a question we can answer for you in writing.
Breckenridge
Reported limits are 55 dB(A) from 7:01am to 10:59pm and 50 dB(A) outside those hours in residential zones, and 70 and 65 in commercial. The town manager can permit above them at their discretion. Verify with the venue: we could not reach the code text directly.
Steamboat Springs
Reported at 60 dB(A) from 7am to 7pm and 55 dB(A) from 7pm to 7am. Amplified sound in a public place needs permission from the city manager.
Vail
Reported at 65 dB(A) outdoors in the resort villages, measured ten feet from the permitted area, with the town manager able to allow amplified sound for up to four consecutive days. Vail was reviewing these rules as outdoor programming grew, so confirm the current figure with the venue.
Beaver Creek, Winter Park, Snowmass Village, Crested Butte, Mountain Village
Not confirmed. Send us the venue and we'll get the answer from the town.

One pattern holds across all of them. The published cap is a property-line or perimeter figure, and a dance floor runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor. Those two numbers aren't comparable, and the distance between the band and the property line is what reconciles them. That distance is a site question, which is why we want the venue name early.

Altitude

Denver sits at 5,280 feet and the corridor runs well above it. Breckenridge is the highest of these at roughly 9,600 feet, Mountain Village above Telluride is about 9,545, Winter Park about 9,052, Telluride 8,750, Crested Butte 8,909, Vail 8,150, Beaver Creek near 8,100, Snowmass Village 8,104, Aspen 7,908 and Steamboat 6,732.

Two consequences reach the run of show. Singers and horns tire faster in thin dry air, so a band that plays four hours at 9,600 feet paces the night differently from the same band in Denver. And the temperature swing between sound check and the last set is wider up there than it is on the Front Range, which means more retuning across the evening.

Both are reasons to build a longer sound check into the document and to put water on the stage.

Travel and lodging

The I-70 corridor adds travel. The far resorts add travel and, past the passes, a night’s lodging billed at what it cost. Both sit on the quote and on the contract as their own lines.

Lodging bills at what the receipt says, and it appears on the far-resort tier because of I-70 and the passes. A drive that takes two hours in September takes considerably longer on a February Sunday, and a band that has driven six hours to Telluride isn't driving home the same night. If you'd rather we price the night without lodging and drive it, tell us and we'll look at it with you.

One more winter fact worth knowing early. Independence Pass closes, so Aspen from Denver runs the long way round through Glenwood Springs.

Four questions for a mountain venue

Everything above comes down to these four. Ask them at contract; in the week of the event, two of them can move the loud hour or end it early.

  • What is the cap, as a number, and is it measured at the property line or at the perimeter of the event?
  • What time does outdoor amplified sound have to stop, and does it differ by day of the week?
  • Who holds the sound permit, and is the property government, nonprofit or commercial?
  • How far is the stage from the nearest property line?

Send us the venue name and we'll ask all four ourselves.
