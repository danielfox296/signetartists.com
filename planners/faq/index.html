Questions

Everything people ask, answered here instead of by email.

Grouped by the part of the job it belongs to. If the answer you need is not here, it is a good question and we would like to be asked it; anything asked twice ends up on this page.

The band and the people

Do you have regular personnel, or is the band filled with whoever is available?
The contract names a configuration: the size, the instrumentation and the repertoire, and that is exactly what performs on your date. Behind it, every act on the roster is an existing group with its own repertoire and its own regular personnel, and a named act is published under the name of the person who fronts it. Any substitution, the front included, is named to you in advance along with the reason. A flamenco act is not assembled out of whoever was free, and a funk act is never asked to play dinner volume it does not play.
What happens if a player cannot make the date?
Any substitution is named to you in advance. Subs are how every roster in this business works; the part that goes wrong is a planner discovering one at load-in. You get the name and the reason as soon as we know, which is the promise we can actually keep.
Who do we deal with on the night?
One named person. You get their name and mobile number in writing before the event, and they are reachable from load-in to load-out.
Can we see the band play before we book?
No, and it is worth being straight about why. These are private-event groups. They do not play public dates you could attend, and we do not stage showcases, because a showcase is a performance for an audience that came to judge it rather than for a crowd that came to a party. Video on each act's page is the honest substitute, alongside published rates and paperwork you can check before you commit to anything.
What do the players wear?
Black tie, business formal or all black, whichever your event calls for. Tell us the dress code you have set for guests and we will sit one notch inside it. If you need something specific, put it in the contract and that is what the players wear.

Booking and paperwork

What holds a date?
A signed contract and a 50% deposit, both at booking rather than anywhere near the event. Until both land the date stays available to anyone else asking for it, and we will tell you if someone does. Approved corporate accounts on net-30 are the exception: those hold on signature, and the full fee is invoiced after the event.
When is the balance due, and how do we pay?
The balance is due 14 days before the event. ACH, a check made out to Entuned, LLC, or card through Stripe, all at no fee. Every method costs you the same, so pick whichever your finance process prefers. Corporate clients can be approved for net-30 instead, invoiced at the event and due 30 days after it, with a purchase order number on the invoice if your finance team uses one.
What is the cancellation policy?
Postponing is almost always cheaper than cancelling and we will say so at the time. One move to a new date inside twelve months is free, subject to availability, and everything paid transfers. If we cannot make any date that works, you get everything back. A straight cancellation more than 90 days out costs the deposit and nothing further. At 90 days or fewer the full fee is due, less any travel and lodging we have not already spent. Ninety days is the line because a date released inside it is one we cannot realistically fill again, and holding your date meant turning other work away. If we ever cancel, every payment comes back inside 7 days and we help you and your planner find a replacement.
Do you carry liability insurance?
Yes. A certificate naming your venue as additional insured comes in the same email as the contract, along with a W-9 and one itemised invoice. You do not have to ask for any of them.
Can you work with our procurement or AP process?
Yes. We can be set up as a vendor, quote against a purchase order, and invoice in whatever form your finance team needs. Ask early rather than late; vendor onboarding is usually the slowest thing on a corporate booking.
How far in advance should we book?
Corporate holiday parties are typically decided August through October for November and December dates, and New Year's Eve books earliest of all. Weddings and galas run longer: ten to twelve months, and twelve to eighteen for a mountain venue or a fall Saturday.

The night itself

How long are the sets, and how long are the breaks?
Sets run 45 to 60 minutes with 15 to 20 minutes between them, set against your run of show rather than a fixed clock. Audio continues through every break at every size, so the night never goes silent.
Can we use your microphones for speeches?
Yes. A dedicated announcement microphone is on its own channel at every size, live from the moment we finish sound check. Hand it to whoever is speaking. No cable swap, no gap, no borrowing the singer's mic.
Will you learn a song we choose?
The first one is included in every booking. Each one after that is $250. Send it at least four weeks out, with the version you mean; there are usually several and they are not the same chart.
Do you make announcements or act as MC?
The bandleader will make short announcements from the stage: dinner is served, the dance floor is open, please welcome your speaker. We do not run the evening as an MC. If your program needs a host, that is a separate hire and we will work to their cues.
Can we add time on the night?
Yes, and it costs what the earlier hours cost. There is no overtime rate. Added time bills at the same hourly figure in 30-minute increments, agreed on the night with whoever holds the budget, so the only question at 11pm is whether the crowd wants one more.
How loud is it, really?
Dinner and cocktails run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, which is a volume you can talk across without raising your voice. Dancing runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor. If your venue enforces a cap, tell us the number at contract and we will build the night under it rather than argue about it at 10pm.

The venue and the gear

Do you provide sound and lighting?
Sound sized to the venue is included at every size. Stage lighting and a dedicated front-of-house engineer are optional add-ons, quoted as their own line. We do not light the space, the tables or a dance floor as a design element; that is your lighting vendor's work, and we will coordinate with them.
Do you need a stage, and do you provide one?
We do not require one and we do not supply one. A band plays fine on a floor. If you want a riser, the venue or your production company provides it, and the footprints we need at each size are published on the technical page.
What power do you need?
Dedicated 20-amp circuits within reach of the stage, one or two of them depending on the size of the band. The requirement most often missed is that they be dedicated: sharing a circuit with catering warmers or a lighting truss is the single most common cause of a problem at load-in. Exact counts are on the technical page.
Can you play outdoors?
Yes, on a covered, level and dry performance area, with power run to it and a plan for the weather written before the day. Colorado evenings drop fast, and instruments and electronics both stop behaving in the cold and the wet. Cover is not optional and neither is a defined move-indoors call time.
The venue has a curfew and a decibel limit. Is that a problem?
No. Mountain and residential venues run on their own rules and we honour them as written. Tell us the cap and the curfew when you book and both go into the plan, including how the last twenty minutes are shaped so the night ends on a high note rather than a cut-off.
Do the players need a green room or meals?
Neither is a requirement. A green room, where one exists, does more for your event than for us: it keeps cases and spare gear out of your guests' sightline and gives the band somewhere to change that is not a corridor. On meals, we do not ask for them. If meals are being served, the band would like them, and a vendor meal is exactly right.

The music

How much say do we get over the songs?
As much or as little as you want. You can build the list yourself, hand us a do-not-play list and leave the rest, or describe the crowd and let the band read it song by song. Most clients do the middle one, and the do-not-play list is the part worth spending time on.
Do you take requests during the event?
From you, yes, and from guests where it fits. The bandleader's job is reading the floor, so a request that would empty it gets a straight answer and an alternative rather than a yes and a bad twenty minutes.
