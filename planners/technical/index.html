Technical

What we need from your venue.

Every question a venue asks before it will confirm a band, answered on one page. Stage, power, channels, load-in and volume, solo to quartet, plus the DJ. Send the link to the venue and to your AV lead; the six things worth confirming at contract are at the bottom.

The stage

Published stage-rental guidance for a seven to ten-piece band runs to 24 by 24 feet. That figure is written to be safe for any band anyone might book, and it is more than four times what the quartet, the largest band we sell, asks for. Renting to it costs you a few hundred dollars and a chunk of floor your guests wanted.

So there are two numbers below. The first is what we ask for, where the band is comfortable and the sightlines work. The second is the smallest we will play on. Between them nothing breaks; the band just gets tighter, and tightness is audible before it is visible.

Depth before width if you have to choose. A band squeezed narrow still works; a band with no depth puts the drums in the singer’s back.

Risers, floors and ceilings

We do not require a stage
A band plays fine on a floor, and most of our Denver bookings are on one. A riser is a sightline decision for events over about 150 guests, not a technical requirement.
We do not supply one either
If you want a riser, it comes from the venue or your production company. Tell us the height and we will plan the load-in around it; 12 to 24 inches is normal and anything over 24 needs a rail behind the drums.
Ceiling height
Ten feet clear above the stage surface. Lighting stands go to nine, and a low ceiling over a riser is the one constraint that cannot be solved on the day.
Surface
Level and solid. Deck that flexes under a drummer will be heard through every microphone on the stage. Carpet is fine; grass and gravel need a deck under the band.

Power

This is the requirement venues get wrong most often, and it is the one that ruins a night. Dedicated is the operative word. A 20-amp circuit shared with catering warmers, a coffee urn or a lighting truss will trip, and it will trip at the moment everything is switched on at once, which is the moment the venue is full.

Circuits need to be within about 50 feet of the stage. We bring our own distribution and cable and we do not need the venue to supply either. What we need from the venue is the circuits, confirmed in writing, before the day.

Outdoors, and other places without a wall socket

A generator works if it is a quiet one, properly grounded, and sized with headroom above the figures above. A domestic generator at the edge of a lawn is not that, and the noise floor it adds sits underneath every quiet moment of the evening.

Cover over the band and over the desk is not optional outdoors, in any forecast. Colorado evenings drop fast enough that the temperature at the last set is a different instrument problem from the temperature at sound check, and by the end of a damp night the players are retuning between songs. If there is a move-indoors plan, it needs a decision time written into the run of show and a person named to make the call.

Load-in and strike

Times below are from the vehicle arriving to the band being ready for sound check, assuming a ground-floor space with parking within a reasonable push. Add 30 minutes for an elevator, and add 30 more if the elevator is shared with catering during their load-in.

SizeLoad-in and setupStrikeVehicles
Solo45 min30 min1
Duo60 min45 min1
Trio60 min45 min1
Quartet90 min60 min1
DJ45 min30 min1
Doors and corridors
36 inches clear. The widest single item is the bass amp or a keyboard case; nothing needs a double door, but a 30-inch doorway with a turn in it will cost you 20 minutes.
Elevator
A car of about 4 by 6 feet takes everything in several trips. Tell us the window we have it in and we will plan inside it.
Sound check
30 minutes once the band is set, and it finishes 60 minutes before the first guest arrives. That gap is not padding. It is where the problems found at sound check get fixed.
Where the cases live
Out of sight of guests, and not in the kitchen path. Any back-of-house corner works. If there is nowhere, tell us at contract and the cases go back to the vehicles, which adds 20 minutes to the strike.
Strike
Begins after the last note, not before. We do not break down during the final set, and nothing gets carried through a space with guests still in it unless the venue requires it.

Volume

Volume is a decision somebody makes in advance and writes into the run of show. Left undecided it gets made at 8:40pm by whoever is standing nearest the desk, and that person is guessing.

Ceremony, cocktails and dinner, measured at the nearest table
65 to 72 dBA
Remarks, awards and anything on a microphone that is not us
Silent
Dancing, measured on the floor
88 to 95 dBA
Peaks during the last hour
100 dBA

If the venue enforces a cap, give us the number when you book. A band told at contract that the ceiling is 95 dBA builds a different night, with the arrangements and the instrumentation chosen to land inside it. A band told at 10pm just gets quieter, and everyone can hear it going wrong.

Curfews are honoured as written. Where there is one, the last twenty minutes get shaped so the night finishes on the song it should finish on, rather than stopping mid-set because the clock ran out.

What we need confirmed, and when

Six things. All of them are answers the venue already has, and none of them take longer than one email to get. Confirmed at contract, they stop being risks.

  • The circuits, by count, and that they are dedicated
  • The stage or the floor area, in feet, and the riser height if there is one
  • The load-in door, the route, and the elevator window if there is an elevator
  • Where the vehicles park and for how long
  • Any decibel cap, as a number, and any curfew, as a time
  • Somewhere out of sight for cases and for players to change

Not sure of an answer? Send us the venue name. Most of the venues on the Front Range and in the mountain corridor are known quantities, and where one is not we will call them ourselves.
