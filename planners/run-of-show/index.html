Run of show

Three nights, already timed.

Most run-of-show documents carry the band as one row that says band plays, and the detail gets filled in during the week of the event, by email, at speed. Here is that detail already worked out for the three shapes a night usually takes. Take them, change the times, delete what does not apply.

How to read these

The cue column is the one worth arguing about before the day. Every moment in an evening belongs to somebody, and the failures are almost always ownership failures rather than timing failures: two people assuming the other one was going to nod at the band, or nobody knowing who decides the speeches are running long.

Agree the cue column at sound check with the person holding the master document. Our bandleader takes their cues from that one person and from nobody else in the building, which is the only version of this that survives a night with four hundred guests and a bar.

Look twice at two of those rows. 7:00pm is the only reliable way to move four hundred people from a bar into a seated dinner, and a band doing it takes about a third of the time an announcement does. 9:15pm is the changeover: a quartet cannot get from a dinner setup to a dance setup silently, and the DJ covering that gap is the difference between a transition and a lull.

The row people move most often is 6:45pm. A first dance immediately after the entrances, before anyone has picked up a fork, keeps the whole crowd watching. Held back until after dinner it competes with coffee, and half the guests are outside.

A company holiday party

Two hundred staff, a quartet, a hotel ballroom, an early hard stop. Shorter, looser, and the one where the program is smallest and the dance floor matters most.

The failure mode specific to this night is 8:00pm. Remarks at a holiday party start late, run long, and land on a crowd that has been drinking since half past six. Put a hard finish on them in the document, give it to whoever is speaking in advance, and put the band back up on time regardless. A crowd forgives a short speech. It does not come back from a long one.

If the program wants a louder chapter, live-band karaoke adds $1,500 and works best here, in the second set, once the night is warm enough that people will actually get up.
