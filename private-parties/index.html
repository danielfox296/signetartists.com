Three shapes

The three shapes a private booking takes.

Nearly every enquiry on this page is one of these. Each has a size that fits it and a figure already on the card.

The dinner party

A table of twelve, or three tables of eight. The music sits under the conversation for the whole night: standards on upright bass and guitar, at a level you can talk across. An hour on its own is $1,050; arrival through dessert runs $2,000–2,800.

The format is the jazz duo. A solo guitarist holds the same night at $900–1,200 where the table is smaller.

The cocktail party that ends in dancing

When the furniture is going to end up against the wall, trio size is the honest answer: guitar, bass and drums fill a floor without a stage, $2,800–3,800 for the night. Tejas Singh is the act we field first here, the one booking on the roster that changes size during the night, a duo through drinks that becomes the band the dancing needs, on one contract.

Where dancing is a maybe rather than the plan, an acoustic duo covers the night at $2,000–2,800.

The milestone with a moment in it

A fortieth, an anniversary, an engagement dinner. There is a toast and sometimes a surprise, and the band plays around both: a dedicated announcement microphone is live from the end of sound check, so nobody borrows the singer’s mic mid-speech. Tejas Singh at quartet size is the dance floor booking, $4,000–5,500.

When the moment should be the thing guests photograph, Dirty Flamenco is the specific flavor: real palos, the traditional forms, at trio size, $2,800–3,800, with a dancer configuration for a set piece. Engagement dinners sit here too, and the wedding cocktail hour that follows is its own page.

What booking a band at a home or club actually involves.

Most of what a host wants to know before writing is whether a band works in the space at all. Here is the whole of it, written to be forwarded to a co-host, a spouse or the club manager.

Space
A solo sets up in 6 by 4 ft, a duo in 8 by 6, a trio in 12 by 8. No stage required: a band plays fine on a floor, and those are working footprints, the space players and gear actually occupy.
Power
One dedicated 20-amp circuit within reach of the setup covers a solo, duo or trio; a quartet takes two. Dedicated is the word that matters. Sharing a circuit with the caterer’s warmers is the most common load-in problem, and one question to the caterer settles it before the day.
Load-in
45 minutes for a solo, up to 90 for a quartet, scheduled before guests arrive, with strike in 30 to 60 minutes at the end of the night. Tell us where to park and which door to use, and the gear moves once.
Outdoors
Patios and gardens work on three conditions, written down before the day: a covered, level and dry performance area, power run to it, and an agreed move-indoors call time. Colorado evenings drop fast, and instruments and electronics stop behaving in the cold and the wet.
Neighbours and volume
Dinner runs 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a volume you can talk across without raising your voice. Dancing runs 88 to 95 on the floor. A shared wall, a close neighbour or a club decibel policy goes into the plan at booking, and the night is built under it.
Paperwork
Clubs and managed buildings usually ask for a certificate of insurance. One naming the venue as additional insured comes in the same email as the contract, with a W-9 and one itemised invoice.

Footprints, circuits and channel counts for every size are on the technical page, in a form a venue can check line by line.

Where

Denver, Boulder and the calendar they share.

In Denver, the card’s city column applies as printed, with no travel line inside the metro, and the calendar is the real constraint: a holiday gathering at home competes with every office party in the city for the same December Saturdays. Boulder parties skew smaller, homes and clubs where the crowd sits close to the band, so solo and duo are the sizes to price first, and a Front Range travel line is added to the quote.

If the date is in November or December, book on the corporate season’s clock: those dates are decided August through October, New Year’s Eve books earliest of all, and peak weekends carry their own line on the quote. A party at a mountain house runs on the card’s resort column, with the corridor’s logistics covered on the mountain page. An office event is its own set of pages: the holiday season, client dinners and retreats all live on the corporate side.

Questions

What hosts ask before they write.

What does a band for a dinner party cost?
A dinner party usually books at solo or duo size: a solo runs $900–1,200 and a duo through cocktails and dinner runs $2,000–2,800 in Denver, with sound included and no overtime rate. Every size is published in both the Denver and mountain columns on the pricing page, and travel outside the metro is its own line on the quote.
Do you play private homes?
Yes. Dinner parties, cocktail parties and milestones at homes and clubs are the bookings this page exists for. The requirements are small: floor space in a corner, one dedicated 20-amp circuit within reach, and somewhere to park for load-in. Clubs that ask for a certificate of insurance get one with the contract.
How much space does the band need?
A solo takes 6 by 4 ft, a duo 8 by 6, a trio 12 by 8, and none of them needs a stage. Those are working footprints, the space players and gear actually occupy, and the full table for every size is on our technical page.
How loud will it be?
Dinner and cocktail sets run 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, a volume you can talk across without raising your voice. Dancing runs 88 to 95 dBA on the floor. If your venue or club enforces a cap, tell us the number when you book and the night is built under it.
How far ahead should we book a private party?
For a November or December date, decide as the corporate season does: those dates are settled August through October and New Year's Eve books earliest of all. The rest of the year runs lighter, and a specific act on a Saturday is still worth claiming early.
Can you play outdoors at a home?
Yes, on a covered, level and dry performance area with power run to it, and with a move-indoors call time agreed before the day. Colorado evenings drop fast, and instruments and electronics stop behaving in the cold and the wet, so the cover and the call are both required.
