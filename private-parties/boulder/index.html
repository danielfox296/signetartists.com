The sizes

The sizes Boulder books.

A quartet is for a dance floor that has to fill. Most Boulder evenings are conversation first, in venues and neighborhoods that sit close together, and a solo, duo or trio covers that while staying under the volume ceiling. When a date wants four pieces, we'll say so.

A solo through a patio dinner is the smallest of these and the one Boulder books most. A trio is the biggest size most Boulder venues allow, and it fills a dance floor without needing a stage.

The venues

Chautauqua to the St Julien, and the houses in between.

Boulder's private events concentrate in a short list of venues. Chautauqua's Community House and Dining Hall carry the milestone dinners and anniversaries. The Hotel Boulderado and the St Julien hold the downtown celebrations. Flagstaff House holds the dinner that is booked for its view.

Each has its own reasons to watch the volume. Chautauqua sits inside a residential historic district. The Boulderado and the St Julien put their event floors a corridor away from sleeping guests. Flagstaff House sits up a mountain road with the city below it. The conclusion is the same at all of them: ask for the venue's sound policy early, choose a size that sits comfortably under it, and put the number in the contract.

The same applies at home. Boulder neighborhoods sit close, and for half the year the evening wants to be outdoors: a solo or duo on the patio at dinner volume, 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, is the booking that keeps the evening on the right side of the fence line. A trio plays indoors.

Logistics

The drive, the load-in and the last set.

Travel from Denver is one short line on the quote, with no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line, so the fee for the music itself is the same one a Denver host sees. November and December dates run on the corporate season's clock, decided August through October, and New Year's Eve books first.

Load-in at these sizes is small: one van, 45 to 60 minutes, through whichever door the venue prefers. The Boulder question worth settling early is parking. Downtown is metered, Chautauqua shares its lot with a trailhead that fills on summer evenings, and a house on the Hill shares its curb with students during the university year. Tell us the address and we plan the carry; a solo needs 6 by 4 feet and a single dedicated circuit at the end of it, which most Boulder houses have to spare.

We honour the curfew as written. Give us the cap and the cut-off when you book and both go into the plan, including how the last set lands on the cut-off. Footprints and power for every size are on the technical page, and the Denver page carries the house-booking detail at metro scale.

Questions

Asked about Boulder bookings.

How late can a band play in Boulder?
As late as your venue's own rules allow; we honour caps and curfews as written. Boulder venues and neighborhoods set earlier ceilings than Denver's. Give us the cap and the cut-off at contract and the night is built under both.
Does travel from Denver add to a Boulder quote?
One short line on the quote, covering the whole trip down and back. There's no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line for Boulder, because the band drives home from Boulder, and the fee for the music itself is the same one a Denver host sees.
What size fits the Community House at Chautauqua or a Boulderado event floor?
Solo to trio. These are conversation-first venues with neighbours and guests close by, and three pieces is the most sound the evening usually wants. A solo or duo holds dinner at 65 to 72 dBA; a trio adds the lift for toasts and a small floor while staying under the ceiling.
Can music play outside at a Boulder home?
Yes, at dinner volume. We play outdoors on a covered, level and dry area with power run to it, and Colorado evenings cool fast, so instruments come indoors as the night does. A patio set at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table keeps the evening on good terms with the street.
