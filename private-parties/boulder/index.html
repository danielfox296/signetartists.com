The venues

Chautauqua to the St Julien, and the houses in between.

Boulder's private events concentrate in a short list of the market's places. Chautauqua's Community House and Dining Hall carry the milestone dinners and anniversaries. The Hotel Boulderado and the St Julien hold the downtown celebrations. Flagstaff House holds the dinner that is booked for its view.

Each carries its own sound reality. Chautauqua sits inside a residential historic district. The Boulderado and the St Julien put their event floors a corridor away from sleeping guests. Flagstaff House sits up a mountain road with the city below it. The conclusion is the same at all of them: ask for the venue's sound policy early, choose a size that sits comfortably under it, and put the number in the contract.

At home the same shape holds. Boulder neighborhoods sit close, and for half the year the evening wants to be outdoors: a solo or duo on the patio at dinner volume, 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table, is the booking that keeps the evening on the right side of the fence line. Trios go indoors, or early.

Logistics

The drive, the load-in and the last set.

Travel from Denver is one flat line on the quote, the published Front Range rate. No mileage arithmetic and no lodging line: Boulder is a drive home. It sits on the rate card and on the contract as its own row, so the performance figure you compare is the same one a Denver host sees. November and December dates run on the corporate season's clock, decided August through October, and New Year's Eve books first.

Load-in at these sizes is small: one van, 45 to 60 minutes, through whichever door the venue prefers. The Boulder question worth settling early is parking. Downtown is metered, Chautauqua shares its lot with a trailhead that fills on summer evenings, and a house on the Hill shares its curb with the university year. Tell us the address and we plan the carry; a solo needs 6 by 4 feet and a single dedicated circuit at the end of it, which most Boulder houses have to spare.

The curfew is a design input, and honoured as written. Give us the cap and the cut-off when you book and both go into the plan, including how the last twenty minutes are shaped so the night lands on an ending rather than a stop. Footprints and power for every size are on the technical page, and the Denver page carries the house-booking detail at metro scale.

Questions

Asked about Boulder bookings.

How late can a band play in Boulder?
As late as your venue's own rules allow; we honour caps and curfews as written. Boulder venues and neighborhoods set earlier ceilings than Denver's, so we put the loud chapter early and finish at dinner volume. Give us the cap and the cut-off at contract and the last set is shaped to land inside both.
Does travel from Denver add to a Boulder quote?
One flat line, the published Front Range travel rate, printed on the rate card next to every performance figure. It covers the whole trip; there is no mileage arithmetic and no lodging line for Boulder, and the performance rate itself is the same one a Denver host sees.
What size fits the Community House at Chautauqua or a Boulderado event floor?
Solo to trio. These are conversation-first venues with neighbours and guests close by, and three pieces is the most sound the evening usually wants. A solo or duo holds dinner at 65 to 72 dBA; a trio adds the lift for toasts and a small floor without crowding the ceiling.
Can music play outside at a Boulder home?
Yes, early and at dinner volume. We play outdoors on a covered, level and dry area with power run to it, and Colorado evenings cool fast, so instruments come indoors as the night does. An early patio set at 65 to 72 dBA at the nearest table keeps the evening on good terms with the street.
